## F1 — Who is the primary target. **CONCEDE**, with one condition held.

**TARGETER convinced me, and it was this sentence in r1-targeter.md D2:** *"I cannot buy 'sole-trader
domestic painter' in Ads Manager."*

That sentence reorganises the fight. I had been arguing targeting when I was actually arguing design.
If Wazza cannot be bought, then "target Wazza" is not a position, it is a wish. The abandoner can be
bought — he typed *tradify alternative* into Google himself. So he gets the first dollar.

**Now the direct answer you asked for, both halves.**

*Is the notepad man reachable?* Not by intent. He does not search. He has never typed a software
question into a phone in his life — when he wants to know something he rings a bloke he knows. The only
way you reach him is to interrupt him at 8pm on a couch, and that is Meta, and Meta will not sell him to
you by name. So he is reachable, but only by accident and only by creative.

*Will he pay $99 a month?* Yes, and price is not his objection — $99 is a tin and a half of premium
acrylic, and he already pays a Dulux account, public liability, licence renewal, a phone plan and rego
without blinking. His objection is the **direct debit he cannot see how to stop**. A painter has been
done over by a gym, a tool-hire account or a phone plan that kept billing, and that memory does the
deciding, not the amount.

*But is the abandoner the better first customer?* On the evidence, yes, and here is the real-world reason
rather than a preference: **the abandoner has already had the conversation.** He has already sat at the
kitchen table and told his missus or his accountant that a monthly software bill is a cost of doing
business. That conversation is a one-time cost and he has already paid it. The notepad man has not, and
at 8:40pm on a Tuesday that conversation is what makes him put the phone down, not the price.

**The condition I hold, and it is not small.** The abandoner arrives with a yardstick. He quit ServiceM8
or Tradify, but he learned what an app is *supposed* to have while he was in there, and he will ask about
things we do not do: job scheduling, staff hours, materials ordering, Xero sync. In week one. By email.
To a man who has ruled out calls and has a day job of answering support. Wazza never asks that, because
he has no yardstick. So: **buy the abandoner, build for the notepad man, and close the comparison before
it opens** — one line on the page and in the ad, in his words:

> This is not a job-management app. It measures the room, writes the quote and chases the ones that go
> quiet. That is all it does. If you want timesheets and stock, go back to the other one.

That line loses some clicks and saves the only resource we cannot buy more of, which is Aaron's inbox.

**Falsifiable test, and I will be bound by it:** read the first twenty support emails. If more than a
third name another app or ask for Xero, scheduling or staff, the abandoner is costing us more in support
than he pays in subscription, and the first dollar moves to Meta and the notepad man.

**Quote volume, settled.** My 2–6 a week and MONEY's 5–15 a month are not two businesses, they are one
business in two seasons. A domestic repainter quotes 3–8 a week from September to March and 1–3 a week
through the winter wet. **Call it about twenty quotes a month averaged over a year.** That is the number
the message cap should be sized against, and at roughly three messages a job it lands near 60 a month —
comfortably inside 150, which is MONEY's point and it survives.

## F2 — Channel and hook. **CONCEDE on the hook. DEFEND the running order with new matter.**

**Hook: TARGETER and ARCHITECT are right and I was wrong.** I wanted to open on pain — *"Fourteen grand
out and you hate ringing about it."* Two things kill it.

First, the one only I can see: **pain is the most crowded three seconds in his feed.** Every hipages ad,
every debt-collection ad, every trade app, every insurance mob opens by telling a tradesman what is wrong
with his life. He has built a callus. He scrolls past pain without registering it. He has never once seen
a hand sticky-tape an A4 sheet to a wall.

Second, the one MONEY should have caught: **"fourteen grand" is an implied claim about a typical
painter's ledger and we have zero customers to back it.** Under ACL that is a number I would have to
stand behind and cannot. Cut it.

So: **A4 sheet in frame one. Pain in line two of the primary text, not in the video.** Where the pain
line lives, in his words:

> Stick an A4 sheet on the wall. One photo. The room's measured.
> It prices it at your rates and sends a three-page quote with your name, your licence and your insurance
> on it.
> Then it chases the ones that go quiet, three times, in your words, business hours, and it stops the
> second they say yes.
> Your jobs stay on your phone. Nothing goes to a server.
> Free to start. No card.

**What I defend, with new matter: Search gets the first dollar, and the reason is Aaron, not the funnel.**
Search is low volume and that is its *advantage* in month one. One man can read every sign-up, answer
every support email the same day, and watch the first forty activations one at a time. Turning Meta on
first means a few hundred sign-ups from a broad audience arriving in a week at a man who has ruled out
hiring anyone. The first channel should be chosen for the size of the inbox it creates, not the size of
the audience it reaches. Meta comes on when the first forty have gone through without drowning him.

## F3 — When the email is asked for. **DEFEND**, with a fact about behaviour and a number that already
exists in the relay logs.

MONEY, you have costed the wrong object. **You priced a one-field form. What is built is a four-step
hand-off across two applications.** The door is: type email → tap Start quoting → *leave the app* → open
Gmail → find the set-up email (possibly in Promotions, possibly in spam, because it is a new sending
domain with no reputation) → tap the link → land on a screen headed **"Set up your app?"** with a box
wanting a code that starts `j:` and a button that says **Load**.

**Here is the moment, specifically.** It is 8:40pm. He is on the couch with the telly on and a beer
half gone. He has seen twenty-two seconds of video and he is mildly curious, which is the exact amount of
belief an ad can buy. He types his address — and painters mis-type gmail addresses on phones constantly,
because the screen has paint on it and so do the fingers. Then the app tells him to go to his inbox. **He
switches apps.** That is the fatal act. Gmail opens on the seventeen unread things he has been avoiding —
a supplier invoice, a bloke chasing a variation, a school notice. Nothing is there from us yet because
the send takes forty seconds. He deals with one of the seventeen. The telly is still on. He never comes
back. Not because he decided against us. Because he was *in his inbox*, which is the single most
distracting place you can send an Australian tradesman at night, and we sent him there ourselves before
showing him one number.

**The number that settles this, and it is not a guess — it exists today.** The relay creates a Stripe
customer at the door and mints a sending token that is redeemed when the link is clicked and the app
loads. So we can already measure **customers created versus tokens redeemed**. That gap is the door's
true cost and it appears in the first week of spend.

- If the gap is under 10%, MONEY is right, the door is nearly free, I fold completely.
- **If the gap is above 25%, MONEY's model is wrong in a way that inverts the conclusion.** His $366/mo
  advantage for the door rests on 70 extra addressable emails a month. If a quarter of them never
  redeemed the token, those are not 70 leads, they are 70 rows belonging to people who never saw a price,
  never saw a PDF, never saw the product at all.

**And the second half, which MONEY has not answered: the dormant list has no sender.** The 0.31% figure
is the rate at which a dormant email must convert for the door to win. Convert *how*? The brief's own
hard constraint says one person runs this, no calls, no demos, no sales anything, ever, and there is no
server and no lifecycle tool. Nobody is writing that sequence. **A dormant list nobody mails converts at
zero, not 0.31%,** so the door's whole advantage is being paid for by work the constraints forbid. If
Aaron ever does build that sequence, the constraint has moved and I will re-argue it then.

**Where I have moved, and it is toward ARCHITECT, not toward MONEY.** I said "at the first Send". That
is one screen too late and it risks the relay having nothing to send under. ARCHITECT's screen 3 is
better: the email sits on the **"Who's this quote for"** screen, next to *his own* name, before Send, at
the point where it is obviously the thing that makes the quote his. One screen, no inbox, no Load.

> Your name (it goes on the quote): ______
> Your email (so the quote sends in your name, and the app stays yours): ______
>
> We keep your email. Your jobs, prices and clients stay on this phone.

And the **"Set up your app?" / Load** screen goes in the bin regardless of where the email sits. The app
minted that code itself ten seconds earlier. Asking a man to confirm something he just did, with a code
starting `j:`, is the single worst screen in the product.

## F4 — The free allowance. **CONCEDE the number. DEFEND the unit and the expiry.**

**MONEY convinced me on the number**, with this from r1-money.md §2: *"5→10 costs $18/customer and needs
conv 5.00%→5.35% (+0.35pp): buyable. 5→25 needs 6.40%, 5→50 needs 8.14% — unpromisable today."* My 12
was a feel. Ten is costed. Take ten.

**But the unit is mine and here is the fact that decides it: an Australian tradesman has not counted a
text message since about 2012.** Every plan has had unlimited standard SMS for thirteen years. He has no
mental model for a text as a thing with a price. Tell him "10 messages" and he does not hear a small
number, he hears *nothing at all* — it is a unit in a currency he does not carry. He counts jobs. He
counts quotes out and jobs on. That is the only ledger in his head.

So: **meter in messages under the bonnet because that is what costs money — MONEY is right — and display
in jobs because that is what he reads.** Ten messages is honestly described, given the sequence stops the
moment a quote is accepted:

> **Free: your first few jobs, quoted and chased.**
> Ten messages. A job usually takes three or four — the chasing stops the second they say yes.

No earnings claim, no "three jobs" promise I cannot keep. "A few" and "usually" are true.

**Now the failure case you put to me, and it is the right question. Job four is a big one and he runs out
mid-chase.** Answer honestly: **he does not lose the job.** The app still writes the message; he taps Send
and it goes from his own phone, free, forever. That is the soft wall and it is the thing that makes the
whole model decent. So running out mid-chase is not the failure.

**The failure is running out mid-chase *with no warning*.** What actually happens if we get it wrong: the
Tuesday 9am nudge on the $14k Thomson job does not go, because he was two messages short and nobody told
him. He finds out Thursday. That is the one way this product makes him look like he forgot about a
customer, and it is exactly the sin he bought it to stop committing. So the fix is not a bigger free tier,
it is a warning one message early, with the name on it:

> Two messages left. That covers Sharon's nudge on Tuesday and one more, then you send them yourself.

**And I defend against ARCHITECT's 30-day expiry, with something he cannot see from the flow.** Painting
is weather. A bloke who signs up in the last week of June — tax time, wet, dark at half four — may not
quote a house for three weeks, and that is not disengagement, that is winter. A 30-day clock punishes rain.
Worse, it expires having shown him nothing, so the first thing the product ever does to him is take
something away. Ten messages, no expiry, and they sit there until he quotes. The balance line on the
Follow-ups tab does the job the expiry was meant to do.

**One thing of MONEY's I want kept and will not trade:** free messages release only after the account is
real, with a hard per-account and per-day cap at the relay. 1,000 harvested addresses × 10 texts = $1,800
is two months of ads and it is the one number in this document that could actually kill the business in a
weekend.

## F5 — How fast activation must happen. **DEFEND, and I am correcting my own number upward.**

I said 48 hours. **I was wrong, and so is everyone below a week, and the reason is the diary.**

Here is the real world. He sees the ad at 8pm on a Tuesday on the couch. **He will not quote a house at
8pm on a Tuesday.** He has no house to quote. His next quote is Thursday 10:15 in a hallway in Seaton,
because that is when the customer said she would be home. Sign-up time is set by Meta; first-quote time is
set by a stranger's work roster. Those two clocks have nothing to do with each other. A 24-hour window
(MONEY) does not measure whether the product worked, it measures whether he happened to have a quote
booked for tomorrow. On a Friday-night sign-up, 72 hours (TARGETER) expires on Monday night before his
week has properly started.

**So: seven days to a real quote sent to a real client.** A painter's unit of time is the week.

**But I concede ARCHITECT's ten minutes, as a different clock measuring a different thing**, and his
framing convinced me: *"activation is one real quote PDF sent to a real client from the app, and it must
happen inside ten minutes of first open."* He is right that ten minutes matters — just not about the real
client. **Two clocks:**

1. **Ten minutes, first open to a priced room on screen.** This measures the flow, and if it fails the
   flow is broken and no ad can fix it. It is checkable in one session.
2. **Seven days, sign-up to a real quote sent to a real client.** This measures the product, and it is
   gated by his diary.

**What I do differently because of seven days:** nothing in the app nags before day seven. No "finish
setting up" push on day two — he is not avoiding us, he is on a roof. And I do not let anyone judge a
creative on 48-hour activation, because the creative will look dead on Saturday and fine on Wednesday.

**Falsifiable:** take the first forty activators and look at the gap between sign-up and first sent quote.
If the median is under 24 hours, my diary argument is wrong, the men who sign up are the men quoting
tomorrow, and I take 48 hours without complaint.

## F6 — Where the paywall appears. **CONCEDE the moment to ARCHITECT. DEFEND the placing. Words below.**

**ARCHITECT convinced me with:** *"The paywall sits at the second nudge on a real job, not at zero."* I
had it at the first nudge, day three. The difference is real and I should have seen it. **At the first
nudge he is nervous** — he has not yet watched a message go out under his name and he is quietly worried
it will sound pushy and cost him the job. Asking for $99 while he is nervous is asking at the worst
moment. **By the second nudge he has seen one go out and nothing bad happened.** That is when it is his.
Second nudge.

**I reject TARGETER's "the offer belongs at the win", for two real-world reasons.**

One: **when a quote is accepted, a painter is not looking at his phone.** He is working out when he can
start, whether he has enough of that colour, and ringing her back. Putting a price screen in front of the
happiest thirty seconds he has ever had with the app spends the goodwill instead of banking it.

Two, and this one is fatal: **painters do not mark things.** Half the accepted quotes in this app will
never be marked accepted — he will just start the job. The brief already concedes the same problem on the
other side: the app cannot see his bank account, so he must mark invoices paid. If the offer only fires
on a marked win, a large share of painters never see the offer at all. **The second nudge fires by
itself, on a clock, without him marking anything.** That is why it is the right trigger.

**What I hold: it appears on the Follow-ups tab with the customer's name and the actual written message
on screen.** Not a toast, not a modal over the top of his work. He must be able to read the thing he is
buying. You are selling the artefact, not the idea.

**The words, exactly as they appear on the phone:**

> **Sharon hasn't come back on the Thomson quote.**
>
> Second nudge is due Tuesday 9am. This is what it says:
>
> *"Hi Sharon, just following up on the quote for the front rooms and hall. No rush — give me a yell if
> you've got any questions about it. Wazza — Steele Painting — 0412 345 678"*
>
> You can send that yourself. Tap Send and it goes from your phone. That stays free, whether you pay us
> or not.
>
> Or it goes Tuesday morning without you, and so do the next 150 messages a month, for $99. A job usually
> takes three or four messages.
>
> Cancel from this screen. Two taps, no notice period, no one to ring.
>
> **[ Send it myself ]     [ Let it send ]**

No exclamation marks, no "unlock", no "upgrade", nothing about growth. The two buttons are the same size.
"No one to ring" is doing more work than any other line on that screen and it must stay — it answers the
direct-debit fear from F1 in five words.

## F7 — The number that decides more spend. **DEFEND $30. It is a budget, not a wish, and here is why.**

**Stated plainly: it is a budget.** It is what the number must be, and it is derived from a conversion
rate I do not believe the other seats have got right.

**I concede the metric and the sample size.** TARGETER is right that cost per activated sign-up is the
steering number and sign-up cost is vanity, and right again with *"Judge at 40 activations minimum, not
before."* Taken, both.

**The threshold is where I hold, and the disagreement is really about one rate: activated → paid.**

TARGETER says 20%. ARCHITECT says 12%. **I say it will be nearer 10%, and I say it because of what we
just built on purpose.** A painter who sends one real quote free and then taps Send himself has a
completely working product. He measures rooms, he prices at his rates, he gets a three-page PDF with his
licence on it, the app writes every chase word for word — and he taps Send. **That is a very good free
product, and we designed it that way deliberately, and every seat here has defended the soft wall.** You
cannot build a generous free tier and then forecast a 20% paid rate off it. The soft wall is worth it —
MONEY costed it and the asymmetry holds — but it has a price and the price is paid here.

**Run it out:**

| Cost per activation | activated→paid | CAC | Against MONEY's $400 ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| $45 | 20% (TARGETER) | $225 | fine, but on the optimistic rate |
| $45 | 12% (ARCHITECT) | $375 | **$25 of headroom. One bad guess and spending stops.** |
| $45 | 10% (mine) | $450 | **over the ceiling. Business does not run.** |
| $30 | 12% | $250 | works |
| $30 | 10% | $300 | works, with room to be wrong |

**That is the whole argument.** $45 only survives if the optimistic conversion rate is right. $30 survives
even if I am right and everyone else is wrong. A one-man business with no salvage — no sales call to
rescue a stalled sign-up, no onboarding session, nobody to ring a lapsed customer — must buy its headroom
up front, because it has no way to recover it later.

**So, the decision rule, whole:**

- **Steer weekly on cost per painter who sends a real quote to a real client inside seven days.**
- **Target $30. Above $45 for two consecutive weeks, the creative or the flow is wrong — fix it, do not
  buy through it. Above $60, stop spending that day.**
- **Judge nothing before 40 activations.**
- **Gate monthly on activated→paid ≥ 12%. Below that, more spend just buys men who quote once for free.**
- **MONEY's payback ≤ 4 months stays as the quarterly sanity check** — it is the only line made of
  observed money — but it is far too slow to steer on and nobody should be waiting on it week to week.

**What would change my mind:** the first forty activations producing paid conversion at 20%. If it does, I
take $45 the same day and we spend more.
