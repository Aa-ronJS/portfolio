# R1 — THE TARGETER

## D1. The target, named exactly
**The domestic repaint painter who quotes homeowners himself.** One man, or one man plus a labourer/apprentice — never 4+, because at 4+ there is a partner or a bookkeeper doing quotes in Tradify already. Age 38–55. Licensed (NSW Fair Trading painter's licence, QLD QBCC) because the licence line on the PDF is only a selling point in licensed states. Work: interior/exterior repaints of existing owner-occupied houses, 3–8 quotes a week, wins roughly one in three, loses several to whoever quoted first. Software he has: a phone, Gmail, maybe a bookkeeper with Xero. Software he refuses: anything with a laptop login, anything called "job management". The sharpest sub-segment is the **ServiceM8 / Tradify / Fergus abandoner** — he already accepted that software should do this, and already decided the existing lot is too heavy. **Explicitly excluded: new-build subcontractors to volume builders.** They never quote a homeowner; they submit progress claims to a builder. They are a large share of Australian painters and every dollar spent reaching them is burnt. This exclusion is the whole ballgame and it is not buyable on Meta — only creative can do it.

## D2. Platform, placement, hook
**Two channels at once, different jobs. Google Search takes intent; Meta Reels manufactures it.**

*Google Search (AU, NSW+QLD first, mobile-only bid boost, exact+phrase):* `painting quote app`, `quoting app for painters`, `painting quote template australia`, `tradify alternative`, `servicem8 too expensive`, `servicem8 alternative`, `how to price a repaint australia`, `hipages leads worth it`. Low volume, hot, cheap. This is the only place I can *buy* the abandoner directly.
- H1 `Painting Quotes From Your Phone` H2 `One Photo Measures The Room` H3 `Free To Start — No Card`
- Desc: `Stick an A4 sheet on the wall, take one photo, the room's measured. Prices at your rates. Your licence, your insurance, your name on the PDF. It chases the quiet ones for you.`

*Meta, Reels + Stories, Instagram and Facebook, AU, men 30–60, 9:16.* Honest admission: **I cannot buy "sole-trader domestic painter" in Ads Manager.** Detailed targeting has nothing reliable at this shape post-2022. So: broad Advantage+ audience, and **the creative is the targeting** — the first frame must repel everyone who is not him. Secondary: Google **custom segments** seeded with the search terms above, served on YouTube/Discovery — that is the closest thing to buyable intent in a feed, and it is a real interface object.

*Creative format:* one take, 22 seconds, phone-shot by a hand with paint on it, no music, flat Australian voice, burnt-in captions. No stock. No logo until 0:19.
*First three seconds:* a hand sticky-tapes an A4 sheet to a bare bedroom wall. The phone lifts. One photo. The room's dimensions land on screen. Caption frame 1: **"A4 on the wall. One photo."** VO: "Watch this."
Then: price appears at his day rate → PDF slides up with *his* licence number and insurer → thumb hits send → cut to a ute driving off. Final caption: "Quoted before he left the driveway."

*Meta primary text, word for word:*
> Sticky-tape an A4 sheet to the wall. Take one photo. The room's measured.
> It prices the job at your rates and sends a three-page quote with your name, your licence and your insurance on it — before you've backed out of the driveway.
> If they go quiet, it chases them. Three times, in your words, business hours, never on a public holiday. Stops the second they say yes.
> Nothing goes to a server. Every job stays on your phone.
> Free to start. Five messages. No card.

Headline: **`Quote it in the driveway.`** Description: `Built for one-man and two-man painting outfits.` Button: Learn More.
*Why he stops:* at 7pm he is on the couch with three unwritten quotes in his head and one he sent nine days ago that never got answered. "Before you backed out of the driveway" is the sentence that names the exact thing costing him jobs.

## D3. Landing page
Keep it for Meta, skip it for Search. Cold social traffic has no idea what this is and the app door asks for an email with zero context; the page exists to run the A4 demo as a looping muted video above the fold and nothing else. Search traffic already typed the intent — send it straight into the app.

## D4. When the email is asked for
After the measure, before the send — not at the door. "Where should I send your PDF?" after he has seen his own room measured converts far better than a cold email gate, because he is paying for something he has already received. I will fight for this even though the door is built.

## D5. Free allowance
Five is the wrong number in the wrong unit. A painter counts **jobs**, not messages, and five messages is one quote plus two chases — he never sees a follow-up sequence finish, and the finished chase *is* the product. Make it **ten messages, framed "your first two jobs, chased to the end."** Cost ≈ $1.80 of SMS.

## D6. Activation
**A real quote, sent from the app, to an address that is not his own, within 72 hours of sign-up.** Demo rooms don't count. If he hasn't done it by day 3 he never will.

## D7. Paywall
The *offer* appears at the win, the *wall* at zero. When he marks a quote accepted: "That's one. Your chasing did that. $99 a month keeps it going — 150 messages." At zero balance, exactly as built: "You're out of messages. Every message still gets written for you — tap Send and it goes from your phone, free, forever. Or let it send them: $99 a month, 150 messages." Never block the work.

## D8. Pay → back to work
Stripe Payment Link → one page → one button: **"Put my 150 messages on this phone."** → the app, on the job he was on. He types nothing. Stripe already has his email; anything he retypes is a place to lose him.

## D9. First thirty days
Retention is caused by the chase landing a job, so the only thing that matters is making that visible. Monday-morning in-app card: "Last week: 4 quotes out, $11,400. 2 chased. 1 accepted." Plus two Stripe-triggered emails only (day 7, day 25) — with no server there is no other lifecycle mechanism, and nobody should pretend otherwise.

## D10. The number
**Cost per activated sign-up ≤ $45, with activated→paid ≥ 20%.** That is CAC ≤ $225 against a claimed $1,667 LTV. Sign-up cost is a vanity number; the paid-conversion rate is too slow to steer on. Judge at 40 activations minimum, not before.

# Where I will fight

**1. "Go broad on Meta and let the algorithm find them."** No. The algorithm optimises to whoever clicks, and painters who sub-contract to volume builders will click a painting ad all day and can never buy this — they don't quote homeowners. There is no Ads Manager exclusion for them. The only filter that exists is the first three seconds of the creative, which is why the ad must open on a *homeowner's empty bedroom*, not a job site.

**2. "Skip the landing page, send the ad into the app."** Wrong for cold social. The app's first screen asks for an email. A man who has seen 22 seconds of video has enough belief to watch a demo, not enough to hand over an address. Right for Search only.

**3. "Five messages is a generous free tier."** It isn't a tier, it's a teaser that cuts off mid-demonstration. Five buys one quote and two chases — he never once experiences a follow-up running to completion, which is the single behaviour that justifies $99. Ten messages costs about $1.80 and buys the actual argument.

# What would change my mind

**NSW/QLD first.** I am cutting geo on licensing regimes. If the first 300 sign-ups show VIC converting at or above NSW, the licence line is not load-bearing, the geo cut is costing reach for nothing, and I drop it same day.

**Search before Meta.** I am assuming buyable intent exists. If the Search terms report shows the whole keyword set under roughly 200 impressions a week at top-of-page bids, AU volume is too thin to be a first channel and Meta Reels becomes the only engine — with Search demoted to a cheap always-on capture layer. I would know this inside ten days.
