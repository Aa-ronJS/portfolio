## F1 — Who is the primary target. **CONCEDE.**

| | churn | LTV | LTV:CAC |

| Tradify abandoner | 10% | $1,000 | **4.4x** |

**The volume number, because FIGHTS asked for one and the four of us gave four different ones.** The ranges

ARCHITECT's 3–8/week at the low end, and above MONEY's 5–15/month. **Settle on 3–4 quotes a week, 12–16 a month.**
It matters for more than a persona: 14 quotes a month × 2.5–4 messages each = **35–56 messages**, plus deposit

**What I hold, uncontested by anyone:** new-build subcontractors to volume builders are excluded. They never
quote a homeowner, they submit progress claims. They are a large share of Australian painters, they will click
a painting ad all day, and there is no Ads Manager exclusion for them. The only filter that exists is the first
frame of the creative — which is why the ad opens in a **homeowner's empty bedroom**, not on a site.

**What changes in the creative because of this concession:** the A4 sheet goes up on the bedroom wall and the
camera catches **a spiral notepad and a mug on the windowsill** in the same frame. Every line about "faster than
the app you gave up on" is deleted. The ad is now for a man who has never paid for software.

## F2 — Channel: **CONCEDE, completely.** Hook: **DEFEND, with a new test design.**

### Channel: Google Search gets zero dollars. Meta gets the first dollar and every dollar until 100 paying customers.

MONEY's sentence did it: **"Max CPC at 5% conv is $2.28."** I was going to spend at four to six times that.
Here is the working I owed the table.

**Australian search volume, my own eight keywords, honest monthly exact-match estimates:**

| Keyword | AU/mo | What the searcher actually wants |
|---|---|---|
| painting quote template australia | ~140 | A free Word doc. Wrong intent — he downloads it and leaves. |
| servicem8 alternative | ~70 | Comparison shopping. Lands on a page with zero reviews. |
| tradify alternative | ~30 | Same. |
| painting quote app | ~20 | Real intent. |
| hipages leads worth it | ~20 | Lead-gen gripe, not quoting. |
| quoting app for painters | ~10 | Real intent. |
| how to price a repaint australia | ~10 | Pricing, not software. |
| servicem8 too expensive | ~0–10 | Real intent, no volume. |
| **Total** | **~300** | of which **~60** is genuine buying intent |

My own round-one kill test was: *"if the whole keyword set is under roughly 200 impressions a week at
top-of-page bids, AU volume is too thin to be a first channel."* 200/week is ~870/month. **The set delivers
about 300 impressions a month at exact match.** It fails my own test by about 3x before I have spent a dollar.

**And the CPC is worse than the volume.** The only terms with volume are competitor-brand-adjacent, where the
auction contains ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus, AroFlo, simPRO, Jobber, Housecall Pro and the comparison
aggregators. AU top-of-page for that cluster runs $8–$15. Take $10:

- $10 CPC ÷ 12.5% click→signup = **$80 a sign-up**, against $12 on Meta.
- To justify $10 against MONEY's $2.28 ceiling, signup→paid would need to be 5% × (10 ÷ 2.28) = **21.9%**.
- Best case the whole channel produces: 60 real-intent searches × 100% impression share × 6% CTR =
  **3.6 clicks a month.** Three clicks a month is not a channel, it is a rounding error I cannot even read.

Worse, and this is the bit I got emotionally attached to: a man who types "servicem8 alternative" is running a
**comparison**. He arrives with zero testimonials, zero reviews, zero case studies and a hard constraint that
there will never be a demo call. That is the single worst traffic in the world for this product. I was going to
spend the first month buying the most expensive clicks available to show them the emptiest evidence page we own.

**Meta cold, for contrast, working shown:** AU broad Reels CPM ≈ $12. 1,000 impressions = $12. Outbound click
rate on a working hook, broad cold AU ≈ 1.0–1.2% → 10–12 clicks → **CPC $1.00–$1.20**, at or under the $1.50
base assumption and half MONEY's $2.28 ceiling. That is the engine.

**Google Search is switched off entirely.** Not "demoted to a cheap capture layer" — that was me keeping a
corpse warm. At 3 clicks a month the capture layer costs more in Aaron's attention than it returns.
Revisit at 100 paying customers, when there are testimonials to put on the page that catches a comparison shopper.

### Hook: MECHANISM leads the video, PAIN leads the primary text. New matter, and a cleaner test.

New argument, which nobody has made: **"Fourteen grand out and you hate ringing about it" is not distinctive
in an Australian trade feed.** Debt-collection services, invoice factoring, Marketplace-style "get paid faster"
fintech and every bookkeeping app in the country run that exact sentence. It is a pain he has been sold to on
five times this month and learned to scroll past. Pain-first fails not because pain is wrong but because
**that particular pain is already saturated inventory**.

The A4 sheet is the opposite: it is the only image in the category nobody has seen. A stop-scroll is a **visual
event in 0.8 seconds**, before a caption can be read. Whatever the sentence is, the thing that stops the thumb
is the sticky tape going on the wall.

But PAINTER is right that the mechanism does not make him *tap*, and the primary text is where the tap is
decided, so:

- **Video 0:00–0:03** — hand sticky-tapes an A4 sheet to a bare bedroom wall, notepad and mug on the
  windowsill. Burnt-in caption: **"A4 on the wall. One photo."** VO, flat Australian: *"Watch this."*
- **Primary text, line one** — **"Four quotes in your head, and one you sent nine days ago that never got answered."**
- Headline: **"Quote it in the driveway."** Description: **"Built for one-man and two-man painting outfits."**

**The falsifiable test, better designed than my round one:** hold the *visual* constant (A4 in both arms) and
split only the **first caption frame** — arm A "A4 on the wall. One photo." vs arm B "Nine days and no answer."
10,000 impressions per arm. Read **hook rate (3-second video plays ÷ impressions)**. If pain beats mechanism by
1.3x or more on hook rate *and* is not worse on cost per activated painter, I lead with pain and say so in
writing. Anything less and mechanism stays in frame one.

## F3 — When the email is asked for. **DEFEND**, and the arithmetic is answered directly.

MONEY told me feelings about friction are not an answer to a number. Agreed. Here is the number.

### 1. MONEY's own table already says the door loses.

From r1-money.md §2:
- A, door: 12.50% × 5% = **0.625% of clicks pay**. Profit@100 **$8,314**.
- B, after first quote: 6.25% × 12% = **0.75% of clicks pay**. Profit@100 **$8,680**.

MONEY's break-even is 9.35% signup→paid for B. **MONEY assumed B at 12%**, which clears its own bar by 2.65
percentage points, and its own table records B as **$366/month better**. So the 9.35% figure does not defend the
door — MONEY's own inputs clear it. The door is rescued entirely by the second number, the 0.31%. Which means
**the whole case for the door rests on the dormant emails converting**, and nothing else.

Note also why B's rate is mechanically higher: removing a gate **cannot reduce** the number of painters who
reach "first quote sent." The paid numerator is unchanged or better, the sign-up denominator halves, so the rate
roughly doubles by construction. 5% → ~10% is not an optimistic assumption, it is arithmetic. The bar is 9.35%.

### 2. The 0.31% requires a campaign that does not exist and that Aaron has refused.

$366 ÷ (70 × $1,667) = 0.31%. Correct arithmetic, wrong denominator, because it silently assumes those 70
dormant addresses **can be mailed at $0 marginal cost**. Check that against the brief:

- *"There is no other database."* The Stripe customer record is the whole list. There is no ESP, no lifecycle
  tool, no sequence builder, no server to trigger from. MONEY's own D6 says the Stripe message counter **is the
  entire telemetry** — so there is no event to trigger a nurture on either.
- *"No calls, no demos, no onboarding sessions, no sales anything, ever. He refuses."* A hand-run re-engagement
  campaign to a dormant list is sales anything.

So the conversion rate of a dormant email that is never emailed is not 0.31%. **It is 0%.** Strike the 0.31%
and MONEY's table stands as written: B beats A by $366/month.

If MONEY wants the 0.31% back, name the tool that sends it, who writes it, and where it is triggered from,
given no server. I do not believe that tool can exist inside the constraints.

### 3. Even if it could, the 70 are not 70.

- Mobile-typed email addresses carry a **6–8% typo rate**. Those are dead on arrival.
- MONEY's own abuse control (§3) requires that free texts release **only after the emailed set-up link is clicked.**
  So the door *already* pays an inbox round-trip tax. Promotions-tab burial and plain abandonment on a set-up
  link sent to a cold sign-up runs high; at a 15% never-click rate on top of typos you are at **~60 addressable**,
  and the 0.31% bar becomes 0.36% — still of a campaign that will never be sent.
- Every door sign-up mints a Stripe customer. 70 junk customers a month means the customer list — **the only
  measurement surface that exists** — is roughly 93% noise. D10 is computed off that record. You cannot read
  activated-to-paid when the denominator is polluted with people who never opened the app.

### 4. What MONEY asked me: what does the door cost in painters who never get through it?

PAINTER already produced the qualitative answer and it is the most specific evidence in the entire folder:
Wazza types his email on a door that says "Start quoting" and lands on a screen headed **"Set up your app?"**
with a box wanting a code starting `j:` or `z:` and a button saying **Load**. *"Load what? I haven't got anything yet."*
That is not a friction feeling, it is a named screen in a named file (app.js:1690–1702) that asks a man to
confirm a thing he did four seconds ago. MONEY's 12.5% click→signup assumption has that screen inside it.

### 5. My concession inside this fight, which MONEY should take.

I am **not** arguing for "ask later, somewhere." I am arguing for a **hard gate at the Send button on the first
real quote**, before a single relay message leaves. That is stricter than the door on the only risk MONEY
actually costed: **1,000 harvested addresses × 10 free texts = $1,800.** Under the door, a scraper needs a form
fill. Under my gate, a scraper must build a priced quote with a client name on it before he can harvest a single
free text. The Send gate is a **better** abuse control than the door, not a worse one. Keep MONEY's release rule
(free messages unlock only after the emailed link is clicked) and the per-account and per-day relay caps exactly
as written.

### The test that settles it.

50/50 split at app entry, 400 clicks per arm. The measured number is **cost per activated painter** — quote PDF
sent through the relay to an address that is not his own — not cost per sign-up. Call it at 40 activations total
(see F7). If the door is cheaper per activation, I concede in one line and it stays.

## F4 — The free allowance. **CONCEDE the number and the unit to PAINTER. DEFEND no expiry against ARCHITECT.**

**Number and unit: 12 messages, shown on screen as jobs.** PAINTER's framing wins: *"your first three jobs,
quoted and chased to the end."* My ten was two-and-a-half jobs, which is exactly the fault I accused five of
having — it stops mid-demonstration.

The cost, answered in MONEY's own terms: 12 × $0.18 = **$2.16** worst case, all texts. At 5% signup→paid that
is **$43.20 per acquired customer** versus $36.00 at ten. A **$7.20** difference on a $225–$276 CAC — **2.6%** —
needing a conversion lift of **+0.13 percentage points**. MONEY judged 5→10 (+0.35pp) "buyable". 10→12 is a
third of that. It is not close.

Why exactly twelve and not fifteen: a job that goes all the way quiet is 1 send + 3 chases = **4 messages**.
Three complete cycles is 12. Fifteen buys three complete cycles plus three orphans, and the orphans do nothing
except widen the abuse exposure MONEY costed. Twelve is the smallest number that lets a follow-up sequence run
to completion three times, and the completed chase is the entire product.

**Metering:** MONEY is right that the *counter* must be in messages, because messages are what cost money. It is
only the *display* that says jobs. On screen: **"3 jobs, quoted and chased to the end — 12 messages left."**
Both units visible, so nobody is surprised by the arithmetic.

**Expiry — DEFEND, with a number and a test.** ARCHITECT wants 30 days to make the free thing ask for money.
It never binds on the man we want and only ever fires on the man we do not.

At 12–16 quotes a month (F1) and 2.5–4 messages a quote, an active painter burns **35–56 messages a month**.
Twelve messages is **gone in 7 to 9 days.** The soft wall at zero therefore arrives inside the first fortnight
all by itself. A 30-day expiry can only ever fire on a painter who sent fewer than three quotes in a month —
who, by definition, is not the business described in F1 and was never going to pay $99.

What the expiry does buy, reliably, is **support email**. A painter who loses six unspent messages on day 31
writes to the one address one man answers. Trading a $1.08 liability for an inbound support email is a bad
trade against the only hard ceiling in the business.

**Test:** on the first 100 activated painters, measure days from first send to twelfth message. If the **median
exceeds 30 days**, the allowance is too slow to ever trigger the wall, ARCHITECT is right, and I take the expiry
same day. I predict the median lands at 8–12 days.

## F5 — How fast activation must happen. **CONCEDE. 24 hours.**

MONEY's window, and ARCHITECT's design target. Taking the challenge head on, because "nothing changes" would
mean the window is decoration and I would deserve to lose it.

**Why 72 was wrong, and it is not a product reason — it is an attribution reason.** Meta gives me a **1-day-click**
column and a **7-day-click** column. There is no 3-day column. A 72-hour activation window matches no reporting
surface that exists, so my spreadsheet and Ads Manager would permanently disagree and I would spend every Monday
reconciling two numbers instead of killing an ad set. **24 hours is the only window where the number in Ads
Manager and the number I report are the same number.** That alone settles it.

**What I actually do differently, 72 hours versus 24:**

| | 72-hour window | 24-hour window |
|---|---|---|
| Meta optimisation event | Cannot use activation — too stale. Forced to optimise for sign-up, a proxy that rewards form-fillers | Optimise for the in-session `quote_sent` custom conversion. The optimiser learns from the event we actually want |
| Attribution column read | 7-day-click (contaminated by view-through and by clicks four days old) | 1-day-click, clean |
| Earliest I may kill a dud ad set | Day 4 | Day 2 |
| Cost of one dud ad set at $25/day | **$100** | **$50** |
| Creative test cycles per month | ~4 | ~8 |

At four ad sets and a 50% dud rate, that is roughly **$200 a month saved and double the test cycles**. The
window is not decoration; it is worth two creative generations a month, which is the only compounding asset
in a paid account.

**Where ARCHITECT is right and I now agree:** the **product** must be built so it *can* happen in ten minutes —
no door, no set-up wall, no "Set up your app? / Load" screen. Ten minutes is the design target and the thing the
flow is engineered against. **24 hours is the measurement window**, because a real painter opens the ad on the
couch at 7pm and does the first quote in a driveway at 9:40 the next morning, and that man activated. A
ten-minute *measurement* window would mark him a failure and I would kill the ad set that found him.

## F6 — Where the paywall appears. **CONCEDE to PAINTER.**

Two things convinced me, one of them a fault in my own copy.

**1. My own sentence is an ACL problem.** I wrote: *"That's one. Your chasing did that."* With zero customers,
zero testimonials and no attribution data — the app cannot see his bank account and cannot know why a quote was
accepted — that is an **unbacked causal claim about a commercial outcome** put on screen at the moment of
purchase. Under the constraint "no earnings claims that cannot be backed," it is indefensible and I withdraw it.
PAINTER's moment carries no claim at all: it states a scheduled fact.

**2. The win arrives too late and too rarely.** At 14 quotes a month and a one-in-three win rate, acceptances
land 3–14 days after a quote goes out — the first one roughly **day 9**. The second-nudge moment lands on
**day 7 of quote one** and then recurs on every quiet job, several times a week. The offer must sit on the
frequent, early event, not the rare, late one.

**3. And the win is the wrong emotional moment anyway.** At acceptance he has already got what he wanted and
owes us nothing. At the second nudge he is looking at a named client, a written message, and a Tuesday. That is
anticipated loss, which converts, versus satisfied relief, which does not.

**The words on screen, at the second nudge on a live job:**

> **Sharon hasn't answered.**
>
> Tuesday 9:00am this goes to her, in your words:
>
> *"Morning Sharon, just checking you got the quote for the lounge and hall — happy to run through it if you've
> got questions. Cheers, Dave — Hillside Painting."*
>
> Tap Send and it goes from your phone. Free, always.
>
> **$99 a month and it goes on its own** — 150 messages, the second and third nudge on this one, and the three
> overdue-invoice chases. That's about thirty jobs a month. Cancel in two taps from this screen.
>
> `[ Turn the sending on ]`   `[ Not now ]`

**What I hold, and everyone already agrees:** the wall at zero stays **soft**, forever. MONEY's asymmetry is the
proof — a hard wall's upside is 1.3% of profit and its downside is 5–11%, with break-even churn at 9.6%. And a
painter who taps Send himself costs **$0.00 of Twilio, forever**. The leak is free. Never block the work.

## F7 — The number that decides more ad spend. **CONCEDE the threshold. HOLD the metric.**

MONEY asked me to show the number. Here it is, and it is the reason I am conceding.

**At $45 per activation and 20% activated-to-paid:**
> CAC = $45 ÷ 0.20 = **$225**.
> Payback = $225 ÷ $100 contribution = **2.25 months**.
> MONEY's gate is 4 months (CAC ≤ $400). **It clears, with 44% of headroom.**

So my gate passes MONEY's gate — which is exactly the problem. **My gate is tighter than the business needs,
and a gate that is too tight kills profitable campaigns.** Watch what happens at the edge:

> At $45 and ARCHITECT's 12%: CAC = $45 ÷ 0.12 = **$375** → payback **3.75 months**. Still clears MONEY's ceiling.
> The activated-to-paid rate that actually breaks the 4-month ceiling at $45 is **$45 ÷ $400 = 11.25%.**

**ARCHITECT's 12% is the correct floor. My 20% is a target I invented and it would have switched off spending
that returns 2.7x inside four months.** Conceded to ARCHITECT, and the sentence that did it is MONEY's
*"Payback = CAC ÷ $100, ceiling 4 months (CAC ≤ $400)"* — because it is the only one of the four gates built
entirely from observed numbers.

**And PAINTER's $30 is not a fifth opinion, it is MONEY's base case.** Decompose it:
> $1.50 CPC ÷ 12.5% click→signup = **$12.00 a sign-up**.
> If 40% of sign-ups activate: cost per activation = $12.00 ÷ 0.40 = **$30.00**,
> and activated→paid = 5% ÷ 40% = **12.5%**, giving CAC = $30 ÷ 0.125 = **$240**.

PAINTER's $30 and ARCHITECT's 12% are MONEY's base case re-expressed. The four of us have been arguing about
one number written four ways. Settle it as:

- **Steering metric, read weekly: cost per activated painter.** Expected **$30**. Kill line **$45 sustained**.
  It is the only number visible in week one, and PAINTER is right that everything downstream is guessed.
- **Gate, read monthly: payback ≤ 4 months, i.e. CAC ≤ $400**, which at $45 an activation means
  **activated-to-paid ≥ 11.25%** — call it ARCHITECT's **12%**.

**How many events before I may judge — new matter, and it cuts both ways.** At 40 activations and a true 12.5%
paid rate you expect **5 payers**. The 95% confidence interval on 5-of-40 runs roughly **5.5% to 26%**. You
cannot read activated-to-paid at 40; the interval straddles both the kill line and double the target. But you
*can* read a cost mean at 40. So:

> **Cost per activated painter: judge at ≥ 40 activations, weekly.**
> **Activated-to-paid: judge at ≥ 100 activations, monthly, never before.**

Anyone who kills this campaign on a paid-conversion rate measured over 40 activations is reading noise.
