# What the Tiny Course page is actually teaching

The page is a long sales letter for a $35 product. Strip the hype, the income
screenshots and the "chill with my family" lifestyle framing and there is a
small, coherent business model underneath. This is that model, stated plainly,
with the parts that are real separated from the parts that are marketing.

## The model in one paragraph

Sell a very short, complete, cheap course (15 to 30 minutes, $35) to cold
traffic from paid ads. Price it so the ads pay for themselves at roughly one
sale per $15 to $20 of spend. Because the course is profitable on its own you
can teach everything you know in it, with nothing held back for an upsell.
Because it is short, nearly everyone who buys it finishes it. A buyer who has
finished something useful trusts you, so a small fraction message you asking
for more, and that is where the real money is: high-ticket coaching or
consulting sold over chat to people who came to you, with no sales call.

## The six moving parts

1. **The product is deliberately tiny.** Three screen-recorded videos and two
   checklists. Slides, no webcam, no fancy microphone. Recorded in an
   afternoon. The point is not laziness; it is completion rate. A 12-module
   course gets abandoned at module 2. A 22-minute course gets watched to the
   end, and only a finished course produces the trust the rest depends on.

2. **The price is set to the ad economics, not the content.** $35 is the
   sweet spot he claims: cheap enough that cold traffic buys it on impulse,
   expensive enough that one sale covers a day or two of ad spend. His stated
   target is $1 of ads producing $2 of course sales. Everything above
   break-even is profit before a single coaching client.

3. **Teach everything.** This is the ethical core and also the commercial
   trick. Most low-ticket offers hold back so the buyer needs the upsell,
   and buyers can feel it. A course that is profitable by itself has no
   reason to hold back, and a buyer who gets the whole thing comes back on
   their own. He calls this pull marketing versus push marketing.

4. **The "I finished it" message.** The ascension mechanism is a single
   sentence inside the course and in the receipt email: when you finish,
   reply and tell me what you built. Some buyers do. That reply is the
   opening of a conversation that ends, for one or two in ten, in a $5K to
   $10K engagement closed over Messenger or email. No calls, no application
   form, no "strategy session".

5. **Worldwide ads on $5 a day.** The one tactical claim with a real edge.
   Meta ads targeted at the USA are the most expensive inventory there is.
   Set the location to worldwide, let the algorithm find buyers, and the cost
   per sale falls sharply. He claims 70% of sales come from outside the USA
   and a 2 to 3% conversion rate on cold traffic. The test is $5 a day for 7
   days, $35 total, which is the price of one course: if the first week does
   not sell three or four copies the idea is wrong, not the ads.

6. **One-sentence ad, one-page sales page, one product.** No funnel, no
   webinar, no lead magnet, no email sequence at launch. The ad is a single
   line. The page is a long letter with the same button repeated after each
   argument. The product is the offer. Everything else is added later, from
   profit.

## Where the ideation goes

He spends most of the page on the idea, because that is where these fail.
The stated criteria for a tiny course idea that sells:

- **A pain point with money behind it.** An audience that already spends on
  the problem.
- **Instant gratification.** The buyer can do the thing the same day they
  watch it. "How to X in 20 minutes", not "master X".
- **A hook, not a topic.** The title is the ad. If the title needs a sentence
  of explanation the idea is not ready.
- **Beta first.** Sell the idea from a sales page before recording it. If it
  sells, record it that week. If it does not, you have lost $35 of ads, not a
  month.

## What is real and what is marketing

| Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Short courses get finished, long ones do not | True and well documented in course-platform data. |
| Teaching everything produces inbound requests | Plausible and consistent with how expertise sells generally. |
| Worldwide targeting is much cheaper per impression | True. Whether the buyers convert to high-ticket depends entirely on the niche. |
| $1 in, $2 out on cold traffic | Unverifiable. Personal-development is one of the highest-converting impulse niches; do not assume it transfers. |
| Idea to first sale in 24 hours | Only if the page and ad are already live, which the page is honest about (sell the beta). |
| $10K per month | Marketing. Depends on the coaching offer behind the course, not the course. |
| 1,200 students, $40K per month, publication logos | Unverified social proof. Do not copy this pattern; the house rule is no claim that is not real. |

## What we take from it

The structure, the price logic, the teach-everything stance, the finish-and-
reply ascension, and the $35 ad test. Not the income claims, not the fake
scarcity around the bonus, and not the testimonial screenshots. `PLAN.md`
applies this to Aaron's own material, and `public/course/index.html` is the
sales page for it.
