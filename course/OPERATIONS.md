# Running it

The system has one moving part that needs a person: the replies. Everything
else runs on its own once `SETUP.md` is done. This is what the person does.

## Daily, five minutes

1. Open the `REPLY_TO` inbox. Answer every reply to the delivery email the
   same day, personally, in plain text. No template. The buyer just did the
   work and told you about it; the answer is about their build, not yours.
2. Glance at Stripe. Refund anyone who asks, same day, no questions. The
   guarantee on the page is real or it is worthless.

## Weekly, twenty minutes

Fill one row of the ledger below from Meta Ads Manager and Stripe. That row
is the whole management information system.

| Week | Spend | Purchases | Cost per purchase | Revenue | Refunds | Replies | Conversations | Engagements |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | | | |

- **Cost per purchase** below $17.50 means the course pays for its own ads
  with margin. Above it, the course is a paid lead and the reply loop has to
  carry it. Both are fine; know which one you are in.
- **Replies** over purchases is the number the method lives on. The page
  and the video-three close exist to lift it. If it is under one in ten,
  the ask is buried; move it earlier or say it twice.
- **Conversations** are replies that turned into more than one exchange.
- **Engagements** are consulting or build work that started in a reply.

Decisions from the row, in order:

1. Refund rate above 10%: stop the ad, watch the course back, fix what the
   refunds said. Do not scale a product people send back.
2. Cost per purchase fine, replies low: fix the ask, not the ad.
3. Cost per purchase fine, replies fine: raise the budget by half. Once.
   Wait a week. Repeat.
4. Cost per purchase rising two weeks in a row: the audience is tiring of
   the creative. Swap the image, keep the words.

## The reply loop, in detail

This is push versus pull. The course does not upsell. The last thirty seconds
ask for one reply, and the delivery email asks for the same one. What comes
back falls into three kinds.

**"Here is what it built."** Most replies. Answer the substance: something
you noticed in what they described, one thing to try next. Nothing else. No
offer. Some of these come back again on their own, and that is a
conversation.

**"It built X but I cannot get Y to work."** Answer the question if it is a
short one. If it is not, say so honestly: this is a few hours of work, and
you do it for people, and here is roughly what it costs. Then stop. They
asked; you answered; they decide. No call unless they ask for one.

**"Can you just build this for me?"** The engagement. Reply with three
questions from the brief template (must do, must never do, finished looks
like), a price range, and how you work: over email and a shared repo, no
calls unless they want them. Quote a fixed price for a fixed brief. The
course has already taught them how to write it.

What never happens in this loop: a follow-up sequence, a booking link sent
unasked, a discount, a deadline. The buyer came to you. Keep it that way.

## When the videos land

Once, to every buyer so far: subject "Draw It Badly: the videos are up",
one line, same link. Send it from the same address so their reply threads
stay in one place. After that, the system sends nothing on its own except
the delivery email.

## Quarterly

Read every reply again. The three most common things people built or got
stuck on are the next course. The method's second product is the first
product's inbox.
