Privacy Act changes 2026: what's actually happening.

A lot of what's being published about "December 2026" is wrong. Here's the state of the reforms — dated, sourced, and updated as the program moves.

If you've read that "the small business exemption ends on 10 December 2026" — it doesn't. That claim, repeated across dozens of IT-provider blogs this year, conflates two different reforms. Getting this wrong in either direction is expensive: panic-buying compliance you don't yet owe, or ignoring obligations you already have. Here is the actual sequence.

Already in force

December 2022 — the penalty reset

Since the Privacy Legislation Amendment (Enforcement and Other Measures) Act 2022, the maximum civil penalty for a serious or repeated interference with privacy is the greatest of $50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover. Small operators won't see those numbers, but they reset the regulator's posture toward everyone.

December 2024 — tranche 1 passes

The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 delivered the first instalment of the post-review reforms: new mid- and low-tier civil penalties the OAIC can actually use for ordinary failures, criminal doxxing offences, groundwork for a Children's Online Privacy Code — and two delayed-start obligations covered below.

June 2025 — individuals can sue

The Act's statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy commenced: a person no longer needs the OAIC to act on their behalf. Intentional or reckless invasions — a mishandled file, a shared record — can now go straight to court, exemption or not.

1 July 2026 — the quiet capture

The AML/CTF tranche-2 reforms made real estate agents, conveyancers, lawyers, accountants, trust and company service providers and precious-metals dealers reporting entities. Under the Privacy Act's existing rules, reporting entities don't qualify for the small business exemption — so roughly 100,000 businesses became fully covered by the 13 APPs on this date, at any turnover. This is the change most affected businesses haven't heard about, because it arrived through anti-money-laundering law rather than privacy law.

In one of those sectors? The obligations are current, not coming. See where you stand in three minutes.

Dated and coming

10 December 2026 — automated decision-making disclosure

This is what the December date actually is. From 10 December 2026, a covered entity that uses personal information in automated decision-making which could significantly affect a person's rights or interests must disclose it in its privacy policy: what kinds of information are used, and what kinds of decisions are made. Tenant-screening tools, credit scoring, AI-assisted shortlisting and triage all sit in frame. The OAIC consulted on guidance through mid-2026 and intends to publish it before commencement.

Agreed in principle — no bill, no date

The small business exemption's removal

In its September 2023 response to the Privacy Act Review, the Government agreed in principle to removing the $3M small business exemption — which would bring roughly 2.3–2.5 million businesses under the Act. As of September 2026 it remains exactly that: in principle. The Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 exposure draft released on 31 August 2026 does not include it, the Attorney-General told Senate estimates in February the Government was "progressing" a further tranche without naming a timetable, and the Productivity Commission has pushed back on parts of the expansion.

The honest read: the direction is one-way and the timing is unknown. When it lands, expect a transition period — and a stampede for exactly the help that's cheap and unhurried today.

What to do with this

  • AML-captured or health sector: you're covered now. Register, breach plan, policy, practice — in that order. The 13 APPs in plain English is the map.
  • Using AI or automated tools on decisions about people: your policy needs the ADM disclosure by 10 December 2026 if you're covered.
  • Genuinely exempt: your clients' contracts and the new tort are the reasons to be ready anyway; the reform is the reason to do it calmly instead of during the stampede.
