# SDP Discovery Run Sheet

**Friday 9:00 am · SDP offices, Level 2 West Harbourside Building, Stanley Esplanade, Port Moresby · NiuPay team heads down 8:45**

Introductory session with Welldone (Welly) Zingunzi, Head – Technology & Solutions, and the SDP team. Aaron runs design and discovery; Krissy and Max work the systems/data threads together; Trevor opens, holds the relationship, and closes the commitments.

---

## Walk out with three things

1. **Travel agreement** — NiuPay people join Welly's regional travel (Daru and field sites) in the coming weeks.
2. **A live discovery sprint** — named SDP counterparts, access to systems and documents, weekly cadence.
3. **A booked readout** — the heat-mapped proposal lands when Trevor and Welly return in ~3 weeks.

## Sensitivities — read before the room

- **Welly inherited this.** He's a year in; the issues predate him. Frame everything as the organisation's journey, never his gaps.
- **SOE pressure.** SDP is under pressure to use state-owned enterprises for technology. Never criticise SOEs — ask neutrally where mandates apply and where flexibility exists.
- **Softlogic.** Probe outcomes and pain, not the purchasing decision — someone in the room may have chosen it.
- **Biometrics.** Identity data for health and education services is sensitive. Lead with safeguarding, then opportunity.
- **First meeting.** One chance. Aim for 70% them talking. Every question should invite a story, not a yes/no.

## Timing

Roughly 107 minutes of material for what may be a 60–90 minute meeting — deliberately over-provisioned. **Must-hit sections: 3 (systems), 5 (identity/external), 6 (travel ask), 10 (priorities), 11 (close).** Trim 4, 8 and 9 in the room if time is short.

---

## 1. Opening — mission, and how technology serves it (~15 min)

**Goal:** get SDP talking about what the organisation is for, so every later technology question ties back to community outcomes.

- Walking through the door, what should we understand about SDP that we can't read on the website?
- What does a successful year look like — how do you know the US$50M deployed last year actually changed lives in Western Province?
- When the board and the communities look at technology at SDP today, what do they say?
- Welly — a year in, what surprised you most about the technology estate you inherited?
- If we're sitting here in three years and it's all worked, what's different for a nurse in Daru or a teacher in Balimo?

*Listen for:* the language they use for success (reach, outcomes, compliance?); whether tech is framed as enabler or as problem child.

## 2. Programs and operating model (~10 min)

**Goal:** map how the organisation actually runs — programs, sites, partners — before touching systems.

- Talk us through how a program goes from funding decision to delivery on the ground. Who touches it, and where?
- Which programs are the most operationally demanding — health, education, livelihoods, solar, agribusiness, infrastructure?
- Where do you deliver services yourselves versus alongside government or partners? Where are those seams hardest?
- What does the footprint look like beyond Port Moresby, Daru and Balimo — how many sites, how staffed, how connected?
- With the mine's eventual closure shaping your mandate, what has to be true operationally for SDP to keep serving the region for decades?

*Listen for:* programs run "alongside government" — those seams are where external integration lives; the mine-closure time horizon driving urgency.

## 3. Systems landscape and the integration layer (~15 min) — MUST HIT

**Goal:** understand the disparate systems and where Softlogic falls short — the evidence base for the internal workstream. Krissy and Max lead the probing here.

- Give us the tour of the core systems — finance, HR, program management, grants, assets. What runs on what, and roughly how old?
- Where does information get re-keyed between systems today? What's the most painful example?
- You run Softlogic as the integration layer between business systems. What did you want it to do, and where does it fall short? *(Probe: reliability? coverage? cost/skill to change? support?)*
- When something breaks between systems, who finds out first, and how?
- What's the single report or number that's hardest to produce today, and why?
- Which systems are you committed to keeping — contractually or otherwise — and which are genuinely up for review?
- Who owns each core system internally, and who supports Softlogic day to day?

*Listen for:* spreadsheets acting as systems of record; one irreplaceable person who understands the integrations; systems named with a sigh.

## 4. Data and reporting (~10 min)

**Goal:** locate the enterprise data sets, their owners, and how much anyone trusts them.

- Where do your enterprise data sets live today, and who would you call the owner of each?
- When leadership asks "how many people did we reach last quarter?", how long does the answer take — and how much do you trust it?
- What reporting is non-negotiable — board, government, communities, auditors — and how much of it is manual?
- Which data sets are duplicated or contradictory across systems? Which conflict causes the most argument?
- What would you do differently if program data from the field arrived clean and same-day?

*Listen for:* reporting deadlines that dictate the operating rhythm; numbers that differ depending on which system you ask.

## 5. Frontline delivery, identity and external integration (~15 min) — MUST HIT

**Goal:** understand the biometric identity work and the consolidation-and-uniformity opportunity across health, education and partner organisations.

- Walk us through how a community member is identified across your services today. Is it the same identity in the clinic, the school and the livelihoods program?
- Where are biometrics in use now, how were they rolled out, and what's working — and not?
- Which external organisations and government systems *should* you be exchanging data with, and which do you actually?
- What does the government relationship require of you technically — standards, systems, reporting formats?
- If identity and service records were consolidated and uniform across the province, who benefits first — and who might resist?
- What safeguards exist today around identity and health data — consent, storage, access — and where do you feel exposed?

*Listen for:* duplicate registrations across programs; partner organisations with their own incompatible identity schemes; appetite (or anxiety) about being the province-wide standard-setter.

## 6. Infrastructure, connectivity and field reality (~10 min) — MUST HIT (travel ask)

**Goal:** ground everything in what actually works in Western Province, and land the travel agreement.

- What's connectivity really like at your sites — Daru, Balimo, the remote posts? What fails first?
- How do field teams work when they're offline — paper, spreadsheets, delayed sync?
- What's the power story at remote sites — is your own solar program part of the infrastructure answer?
- What devices are in the field, and who keeps them alive?
- **The ask:** Welly, you're travelling the region in the coming weeks — could a couple of our team join you for part of it? Seeing Daru and the field sites firsthand would change the quality of everything we design together.

*Listen for:* the gap between head-office assumptions and field reality — then use it to justify the travel.

## 7. AI — governance and opportunity (~10 min)

**Goal:** separate the two AI needs — safe, streamlined staff usage now; enterprise AI over consolidated data next.

- How are staff using AI tools today — sanctioned or otherwise — and what prompted the requirement to streamline it?
- What worries leadership most about AI: data leaving the organisation, accuracy, cost, something else?
- What policy or guardrails exist today, and who owns that decision?
- If AI could reliably do one thing across your disparate platforms tomorrow, what would you point it at first?
- How would your executive and board react to AI-assisted reporting or decision support — enthusiasm, caution, conditions?

*Listen for:* shadow AI use already happening; whether "streamline" means enable or restrict.

## 8. Constraints, SOEs and the environment (~10 min) — handle gently

**Goal:** surface the political and procurement realities that will shape any proposal.

- We understand there's encouragement to use state-owned enterprises for technology — how does that shape your choices in practice?
- Where do you have freedom to choose, and where is the path effectively fixed?
- How does technology procurement work at SDP — thresholds, approvals, timelines?
- Are there data sovereignty or residency expectations — where can data live?
- What's been tried before that didn't stick, and what did the organisation learn from it?

*Listen for:* the real boundary of the SOE mandate — infrastructure vs. applications vs. services; failed projects that left scar tissue.

## 9. Team, capability and ways of working (~7 min)

**Goal:** know who we'd work with, and where capability gaps are.

- Tell us about the technology team — how many people, where based, and what do they carry day to day?
- What's the mix of run versus change — how much capacity exists for new work at all?
- Which vendors and partners are in the picture today, and how are those relationships?
- Where would you most want to grow your own team's capability, so anything we build together is sustainable after us?

*Listen for:* a team fully consumed by keeping the lights on; sustainability framing — they'll value partners who build capability, not dependency.

## 10. Strategy, priorities and the heat map (~10 min) — MUST HIT

**Goal:** get Welly's own ranking and success criteria — this feeds the 3-week heat-mapped proposal directly.

- Of everything we've discussed — internal integration, external consolidation, AI, strategy — what's keeping you up at night, in order?
- What do you need to be able to show your executive and board in six months? In two years?
- Is there an existing technology or data strategy on paper, and what's its status?
- What would make a partnership with NiuPay a clear success by the end of the year?
- What should we absolutely *not* propose?

*Listen for:* the difference between Welly's ranking and what his executives measure him on; the answer to "what not to propose" — it maps the political terrain.

## 11. Close — agreeing next steps (~5 min)

**Goal:** leave with commitments, not vibes. Trevor closes.

- **Travel:** which legs can NiuPay join, rough dates, who from our side.
- **Counterparts:** who at SDP pairs with Krissy and Max for the systems and data deep-dive?
- **Access:** system demos, architecture docs, sample reports, the Softlogic configuration — what can be shared, and under what terms?
- **Cadence:** weekly check-in through the 3-week discovery window; book the readout session for when Trevor and Welly return.
- **The deliverable:** confirm what the heat-mapped proposal needs to contain for SDP's executive to act on it.
