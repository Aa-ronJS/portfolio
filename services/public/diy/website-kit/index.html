Fair warning

Who it's for, and not.

For you if

Your site is the standard five pages; you can give it one to three weekends; you would rather spend $79 and your own hours than a few thousand dollars; and you like knowing how your own things work.

Not for you if

You need to move a site that already ranks (that is the one job I most often tell DIYers not to do), your site must integrate with other business systems, there is a hard deadline with money on it, or the hours honestly do not exist. The kit says all of this inside too, with the reasons.

Why trust the method

Fair questions

Before you buy.

Can I really do this without technical skills?
Yes. The skill the kit teaches is directing: describe the outcome, look at the result, say what is wrong in plain words. If you have ever supervised a trade, you have the skill. Every technical term gets one plain-words sentence the first time it appears, and one prompt in the pack exists purely to explain things that confuse you.
Which AI assistant do I need?
Any of the major ones on a paid tier; the method is not exclusive to one. I use Claude in my own practice and wrote the prompts in that style. Budget for a month or two of subscription as part of the project cost.
What does the site cost to run afterwards?
Typically $150 to $500 a year all-in: domain, hosting, email on your own domain. The kit is specific about where each dollar goes and where the cheap option is genuinely fine versus where it is how sites get hacked.
Is this a course with videos and a community?
No. It is six written documents you can read in an evening and work from for years, because written matches how the work actually happens: you, a browser, and an assistant. No drip-fed modules, no upsell webinar at the end.
What if it doesn't work for me?
Thirty days, money back, keep the files; tell me what was missing so the kit gets better. And the standing offer either way: hire me for the same job later and the kit price comes off the invoice.
