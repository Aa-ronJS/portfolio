The work

Analysis with consequences.

Requirements and scoping

The real requirement, pulled out of people who disagree with each other, processes nobody wrote down, and rules that live in one person's head. On the National Redress Scheme that meant turning legislation into the decisions a system makes about who qualifies, written so the accountable people could check them.

Process improvement and transformation

Mapping how the work actually flows, not how the org chart says it does, then fixing the process before automating it, because automating a bad process just makes the mistakes arrive faster. AI belongs in this conversation now, and I bring the honest version of where it pays.

Vendor and proposal honesty

A senior second opinion on what an agency or vendor is proposing: scope, price, feasibility, and what the contract is quietly missing, from someone who builds these systems and is not bidding on yours. Often the cheapest money a project spends.

Fractional technology leadership

A day or two a week of senior judgement for a business that is not ready for a full-time hire: owning the roadmap, keeping suppliers honest, mentoring whoever you have, and building the small things that need building along the way.

Fair questions

Before you ask.

What does a BA actually do for us?
Turns what people say they want into what the business actually needs, written so it can be built, checked and paid for. It is the part that gets rushed on most projects, and it is most of the value: getting it wrong is expensive, and you find out late.
Why hire a BA who also builds?
Because the handover between the person who scopes and the person who builds is where meaning goes missing, and with me there is no handover. It also keeps the analysis honest: I never recommend what I would not want to build, and I can prototype the answer instead of describing it.
What engagement shapes do you take?
Scoping engagements of a few weeks, contract roles of three to twelve months, and fractional arrangements of a day or two a week. Remote Australia-wide from Adelaide, with the distributed-delivery habits that mining and federal work teach you.
Big-firm consultants quote us six figures. Are you the cheap version?
I am the small version: one senior person instead of a partner, a manager and two juniors. You lose the letterhead and the slide templates; you keep the experience, gain someone who can build, and the invoice stops funding an office tower.
