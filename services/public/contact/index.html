Plain words are plenty.

You do not need a brief, a budget line or the right vocabulary. "The website is slow and the last guy stopped answering" is a perfectly good opening. Send whatever you have and I will ask the questions that matter.

Email me

What happens next

No pitch. A process.

  1. You get a reply from me, not a scheduling bot or a junior with a script. If your job is not a fit, the reply says so and points you somewhere useful.
  2. A short conversation, by email or a call, whichever you prefer. Enough for me to understand the problem and for you to decide whether you like dealing with me.
  3. A written scope and a number. Fixed quote for a defined job, day rate for open-ended work, in writing, before anything starts. No invoice will ever surprise you.

Useful to include

If you have them handy.

  • The address of the site or system, if there is one to look at.
  • What is going wrong, in your words. Symptoms beat diagnoses.
  • What good looks like. "Customers can book without ringing us" tells me more than a feature list.
  • Any deadline that is real, and which ones are just hopeful.
  • Who else is involved: an agency still holding passwords, an IT provider, the nephew who built version one.
  • Nothing else. Genuinely. Two sentences is a fine first email.
