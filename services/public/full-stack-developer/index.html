Fair questions

Before you ask.

How do I judge a developer before hiring them?
Ask for something you can check without trusting them: live software you can use, source code you can read, or a build you can run. My portfolio has all three. A developer who cannot show you any of those is asking you to buy on charm.
Agency or freelancer?
For most internal tools and integrations, one senior person who scopes and builds beats a chain of account manager, analyst and rotating developers, because nothing is lost in handovers. An agency makes sense when you need a whole team full-time, and I will tell you honestly if your job is that big.
What happens when you're not available?
Everything is built in your accounts, documented, and tested, so any competent developer can pick it up. I plan for my own absence on every job; software that only its author can run is a liability, not an asset.
Do you use AI to build?
Yes, the way a foreman uses a crew: written specifications, adversarial review, and independent checks before anything ships. It makes one senior person faster; it does not replace knowing what to build or noticing what is wrong. How, exactly, is public too.
