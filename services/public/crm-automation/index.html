CRM setup and cleanup

HubSpot, Salesforce or Zoho configured around how you actually sell, or dug out from years of duplicate contacts, dead pipelines and fields nobody remembers adding. The data audit comes first; opinions come second.

Migrations

Moving CRMs without losing history is a data job wearing a software costume. The key decision is what records merge on, and getting it wrong is how a charity ends up $3.8 million out. Migrations here are reconciled against your source of truth, not declared done when the import finishes.

Automation

The intake, the follow-ups, the handoffs between website, CRM, accounting and job management, automated end to end so nobody retypes anything. Zapier, Make or n8n where they fit, custom code where they do not, and error handling either way, because an automation that fails silently is worse than none.

Fair questions

Before you ask.

Our CRM data is a mess. Is it fixable?
Almost always. The worst case I have handled was out by $3.8 million after donors were merged on the wrong field, and it was found, rebuilt record by record, and reconciled against the client's own books. Most messes are smaller than they feel.
Zapier, Make, n8n or custom code?
The cheapest tool that will still be reliable in a year. Off-the-shelf automation is right for most jobs; custom code earns its place when volume, error handling or per-task pricing outgrows it. I build with all of them, so the recommendation is not limited by what I happen to know.
Which CRM should we pick?
The one your team will actually use, sized to how you sell, priced for what you use rather than what the sales deck showed. I have run HubSpot, Salesforce and Zoho in anger and none of them pays me a commission, so the advice is clean.
Can AI do anything useful with our CRM?
Yes, when it is wired in rather than chatting beside it: researching a company before a call and filing what it finds, drafting from your real records, keeping data tidy as it arrives. Two of my agents are live and public, so you can judge with your own eyes first.
