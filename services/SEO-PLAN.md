# The publishing plan

What to keep posting, how, and when, for the AI era of Google. This is
doctrine for the site's content machine (`tools/gen_answers.py` and
friends), written to be followed weekly, not admired. Sources: Google's own
AI-content guidance and spam policies; the operational judgement is mine
and yours. Everything off the domain (links, podcasts, partners, PR) lives
in AUTHORITY-PLAN.md; the two plans share a calendar but not an hour.

---

## 1. The rules of the game, as Google states them

- **AI-assisted content is explicitly allowed.** Google rewards helpful,
  people-first content "however it is produced" and penalises using
  automation "primarily to manipulate rankings". Our workflow (AI drafts,
  you ratify) is inside the lines as long as the ratifying is real.
- **The one fatal pattern is scaled content abuse:** mass-producing thin
  template pages, hundreds of unreviewed AI articles, doorway pages for
  keyword variations. This is why the site has 12 deep industry pages, not
  120 industry-times-service combinations, and why that stays the rule.
- **E-E-A-T decides tiebreaks, and the first E is Experience.** Content
  demonstrating first-hand doing beats content describing. Our unfair
  advantage: live tools, compilable code, real reconciliation numbers.
  **Every published page carries at least one proof element** (a live link,
  a real number, a named system you have actually wired). A page that
  cannot carry one does not ship.
- **AI Overviews and AI Mode reward the exact shape we already use:**
  a direct, self-contained answer in the first block, then depth. LLM
  answer engines cite pages they can quote cleanly. The answers template
  (question as H1, 60-word direct answer, structured depth, FAQ schema) is
  built for this; do not drift from it.
- **Identity matters more each year.** One consistent entity everywhere:
  Aaron Steele, Adelaide, the same story on the site, the portfolio, GBP,
  Upwork, Expert360 and LinkedIn. Person schema is wired; keep names,
  claims and history identical across surfaces.

## 2. The honesty note this plan owes itself

The site launches with ~70 pages built in days with AI. That is a site
launch, not scaled abuse: every page is distinct, human-ratified, and
proof-anchored. But the same tooling could produce abuse in an afternoon,
so the standing rules are:

1. Post-launch, content ships as a **steady drip, never a dump**. The
   cadence below is the ceiling as well as the floor.
2. Nothing publishes that you would not say on a call. The generator makes
   pages; it does not make claims true.
3. No programmatic suburb pages, no spun variants, no auto-published
   anything. The day the machine runs without your read is the day the
   site starts dying.

## 3. What to post: the five content types, ranked by value

**A. Work notes (case studies), as jobs land. The crown jewels.**
One page per finished engagement, anonymised where needed: the mess, the
method, the numbers, what you would do differently. Nothing ranks, converts
and earns citations like demonstrated experience, and this is the only type
AI cannot fake. Target: every finished job becomes one within a fortnight
of invoice, no exceptions. These also feed the service and industry pages
as named paragraphs.

**B. System-pair pages. The rankable long tail.**
"[System] to [System]" and "[System] automation" pages: ServiceM8 to Xero,
Shopify to MYOB, Cliniko reminders, HubSpot to Xero, simPRO scheduling,
PropertyMe owner reports, aXcelerate enrolment, Square to Shopify stock.
Buyers type exactly these; almost nobody serious writes for them; each maps
to work you can actually do. Same page shape as answers, plus a "how the
join actually works" section naming the real fields and traps. These are
the highest ranking-probability pages on the board.

**C. Answers pages. The steady workhorse.**
The existing 40 grow from one source only: **questions real people ask
you** (enquiries, calls, Upwork posts, the calculator's topic lines). Keep
a running capture file; every fortnight the two best become pages via the
generator. Invented questions read as filler; harvested ones read as
service.

**D. Deepening passes on existing pages.**
Google rewards updated, thickened pages. Each month, take the one page GSC
shows with impressions but poor clicks and make it genuinely better: a new
section, a worked example, a tightened title. Update its visible content
meaningfully; never fake freshness with a date change.

**E. Linkable assets. Occasional, compounding.**
Rain Check and Doorknock already exist; the pricing calculator is one. One
or two new free tools a year (a redirect-map generator, an AVETMISS
validator, a chargeable-weight calculator) each earn links for years.
Build them when a client problem hands you one. The DIY kits
(PRODUCTS-PLAN.md) are the paid cousins of this type: the "don't hire me"
framing is itself linkable, and every kit page gives the cost-question
answers pages a second honest place to send people.

## 4. When: the cadence

Designed for one person with AI leverage and a client load. Roughly two
hours of your ratifying time a week; the drafting is the machine's job.

**Weekly (pick the same day, protect it):**
- 1 Google Business Profile post (two sentences + link to a page; the 12
  system guides are 12 ready-made posts). GBP posts expire; weekly keeps
  the profile visibly alive. Your Google account, so yours.
- 1 answers page (type C) from the capture file, if it has anything.
  Forward the question; I write the page. If the file is empty, skip it;
  do not invent.
- 15 minutes in Search Console: what earned impressions, what got clicked,
  what to deepen next.

The system-pair line that used to be here is gone because the queue is
already written (section 7). New type-B batches happen on your one-line
request, in bulk, not as weekly homework.

**Per finished job (within a fortnight):**
- 1 work note (type A), plus its paragraph added to the relevant service
  and industry pages, plus the review ask sent to that client.

**Monthly:**
- 1 deepening pass (type D) on the highest-potential existing page.
- Repost the month's best page to LinkedIn with two sentences of context
  in your voice. Same content, second audience, zero extra writing.

**Quarterly:**
- Claims audit sweep: read 10 random pages against reality.
- Prune or merge anything GSC shows as dead for two quarters; a smaller
  honest site beats a larger stale one.
- Re-tune calculator ranges against what you actually quoted.

**Never:**
- More than ~3 new pages in any week after launch.
- Publishing anything unread.
- Chasing a trending topic outside the service map.

## 5. How: the production line

1. **Capture.** Every real question goes into the capture file the day it
   arrives, verbatim, with who asked.
2. **Draft.** Feed me the question or system pair; I draft in the house
   voice into the generator's content files, proof element included.
3. **Ratify.** You read it once with the on-a-call test. Edit freely; the
   voice survives edits, not neglect.
4. **Publish.** Regenerate, sitemap entry, push, then Request Indexing for
   the new URL in Search Console (new domains need the nudge).
5. **Interlink.** New page links its pillar and two siblings; one existing
   page gains a link to it. The generators handle most of this.

**On disclosure:** the site already discloses the method proudly; the AI
development page is literally the pitch. Keep it that way: "built with AI,
ratified by me" is a differentiator here, never a confession.

## 6. The measurement loop

Weekly GSC questions, in order: Which queries earned impressions with no
clicks (title problem or ranking position 5 to 15: deepen that page)?
Which pages earn clicks (feed that cluster more)? Any page with zero
impressions after 90 days (wrong target or needs links: fix or fold)?
Watch AI Overview presence for your queries; pages cited there confirm the
direct-answer format is working. Expect months 1 to 3 to look like nothing:
a new domain earns trust slowly, GBP and reviews pay first, and the drip
compounds from month 4 to 6. Panicking at week 6 and bulk-posting is how
sites earn the scaled-abuse label; do not.

## 7a. The enactment model (what runs without you)

"You ratify everything weekly" was a plan that quietly depended on you
having a spare two hours every week forever. The honest split:

**Already done, by me, in the repo:** the full system-pair queue above;
the 40 answers; the 12 industry pages; interlinks, schema, sitemap. When
you want the next batch (more system pairs, deeper clusters), that is one
sentence from you, not a weekly meeting: the generators and content files
are the machine, and any future session can extend them.

**Cannot be delegated, and small:** the pre-launch claims audit (read the
12 systems pages once against what you know of those products; they state
how ServiceM8, Cliniko, VETtrak and friends behave, and product behaviour
drifts); the GBP post (your Google account); GSC (your Google account,
15 minutes weekly); work notes and captured questions (they are your
calls and your jobs; I turn them into pages the day you forward them).

**The reconciliation with the "nothing publishes unread" red line:** it
stands, and it is satisfied by the pre-launch audit rather than a
page-by-page weekly gate. One honest read of the launch set beats
fifty-two rushed Friday ratifications. After launch, anything I draft
for the site sits in the repo unpublished until your next deploy, which
is itself the read opportunity; the red line is structural, not a
calendar entry.

## 8. Red lines, permanent

No doorway or suburb-spam pages. No fabricated reviews, testimonials,
case studies or authorship. No scraped or spun content. No AI text
published unread. No claims the phone call cannot back. The site's entire
strategy is being the one operator whose pages are all true; every red
line above is cheaper to keep than to win back.
