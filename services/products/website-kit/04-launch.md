# 5. Launch

Where most DIY sites quietly fail. Not in the build: in words that read
like everyone else's, proof that never got collected, and a launch that
consisted of telling nobody. This document is shorter than it is
important.

## 5.1 Words that sound like you

The AI drafting prompt in document 4 forbids invented facts and jargon;
this section is the human pass on top. Read every page aloud once.
Anywhere you would not say the sentence to a customer standing in front
of you, change it to what you would say. Specifics beat adjectives
everywhere they can: "we turn up when we said" beats "reliable",
"quotes in writing within two days" beats "professional service". The
question every page must answer is the customer's only question: what
is this, is it for me, what do I do next.

Two structural rules borrowed from how I write pages that rank:

- **Answer first, detail second.** Whatever question the page exists
  for (what do you do, what area, how do I book), the first paragraph
  answers it plainly. Search engines and humans agree on this.
- **One page, one intent.** The services page sells services; it does
  not also tell your life story. Wandering pages rank for nothing and
  convert nobody.

## 5.2 Proof, starting from zero

If you are established: collect it now, before launch. Text five past
customers the message: "I'm putting my business properly online. Could
you reply with two honest sentences about the job I did for you? And
if anything wasn't right, tell me that instead." Real names and
suburbs with permission. Photos of real work beat stock photography by
miles; a phone photo of an actual job is proof, a stock image is
wallpaper, and buyers can smell the difference instantly.

If you are brand new: do not fake it, ever. One fabricated review found
out costs more than fifty real ones earn. Say you are new and what you
did before; "twenty years on the tools, now on my own" is proof too.

## 5.3 Being found: the honest version

- **Google Business Profile is worth more than your website** for
  local searches ("[your trade] [your suburb]"), and it is free. Create
  it as a service-area business, same name and details as the site,
  add photos, and ask every happy customer for a review from day one.
  A steady trickle of real reviews will do more for your phone than
  anything else in this kit.
- **Register the site with Google Search Console** (free; ask your
  assistant for the current setup steps for your track, including
  submitting your sitemap, which Track A's SEO plugin or Track B's
  assistant makes for you). This is how you find out what searches
  your site appears for.
- **Basics on every page** (your assistant does these when asked; the
  sceptic's check prompt catches gaps): each page has a title that
  says what it is and where ("Emergency Electrician, Adelaide Hills"),
  a sentence-long description, your suburb and service area in real
  sentences, images described in their alt text.
- **The expectations paragraph:** a new domain takes months to earn
  search trust. Weeks 1 to 8 the site exists mostly for people who
  already know your name, and that is fine; that was the one job. The
  reviews-and-Business-Profile lane pays first; search traffic
  compounds later. Anyone selling you "page one in two weeks" is
  selling you something else.

## 5.4 The launch checklist

The site is launched when every box ticks, not before:

- [ ] Every page reads aloud without embarrassment.
- [ ] Phone number tapped on an actual phone: it dials.
- [ ] Form submitted: it arrives at your business address (document 3),
      and you reply to a test message.
- [ ] Site opened on a phone, a tablet if you have one, and a
      computer: nothing broken, nothing hanging off the edge.
- [ ] Every link clicked once. Every image loads.
- [ ] No placeholder text anywhere ("lorem", "[ASK", "your text
      here"): search the pages for them.
- [ ] The address bar shows the padlock (https) on your real domain.
- [ ] Google Business Profile live and matching the site exactly.
- [ ] Search Console registered, sitemap submitted.
- [ ] Ownership register (document 3) updated with everything created
      since; two-factor on; backups confirmed to exist by actually
      looking.
- [ ] Told everyone: email signature updated, socials updated, the
      announcement message sent to your existing customers, the
      review-ask sent to the first five.

Launch it at 90 per cent good. The last 10 per cent improves faster
with real visitors than with another fortnight of fiddling, and
document 6 is where that improvement lives.
