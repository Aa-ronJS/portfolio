# 6. Keep

A website is not a project, it is a small standing habit. This document
is the habit, and the honest list of moments to stop doing it yourself.

## 6.1 The monthly twenty minutes

Same day each month, calendar entry, twenty minutes:

1. **Open your own site** on your phone like a stranger: home page,
   one service page, tap the phone number, send the form to yourself.
   Two minutes, catches most disasters.
2. **Track A only: updates.** Managed hosting applies most updates for
   you (that was why you paid for it); log in, apply anything waiting
   (plugins, themes), and glance at the site afterwards. If a plugin
   you do not recognise is listed, that is a question for your AI
   assistant today, not someday.
3. **Look at Search Console** (document 5): what searches showed your
   site this month, which pages got clicked. You are not
   optimising anything yet; you are learning what the internet thinks
   you are for, which slowly tells you what to write next.
4. **Collect this month's proof:** one review ask to a recent happy
   customer, one photo of real work added to the proof page. The site
   compounds on this more than on anything technical.
5. **Check the register:** any renewal inside 60 days? Card on the
   account still valid? Ninety seconds that prevents the worst
   outage there is.

## 6.2 Small changes, forever

Content changes use exactly the working loop from document 4: describe
the outcome, make one change, look, verify with the sceptic's check.
Track A, you will mostly just edit in the admin screen and the
assistant is your helpdesk when the editor fights you. Track B, changes
go through the assistant-and-republish loop; keep using the handover
note so any session can pick up where the last left off.

Once a quarter, reread your one-pager from document 2. If the business
has drifted from it (new services, new area, different best customer),
update the page content to match reality. Sites rot by staying still
while the business moves.

## 6.3 When to stop DIYing, specifically

The kit promised honesty about its own limits. Hand it to a
professional when:

- **Anything smells hacked:** the site shows content you did not put
  there, browsers warn visitors away, customers mention strange
  emails. Do not DIY incident response; every hour matters and the
  fix is diagnosis work.
- **You are about to rebuild or move a site that already earns
  traffic.** The rankings-preserving move is the single most common
  paid rescue there is, because DIY moves silently drop it.
- **The site needs to become software:** bookings synced with your
  job system, customer logins, an online store past the basics,
  anything where the site must talk to your other systems. (That
  boundary is most of what I sell, which is exactly why this kit can
  afford to give the website part away honestly.)
- **The twenty minutes stopped happening three months ago.** No shame
  in it; a small monthly care plan from any competent local developer
  costs less than one emergency.

If the professional you hand it to is me, the kit price comes off the
invoice as promised in document 1. Either way, hand over the one-pager
and the ownership register and you will be the easiest client they
have had all year.

Good luck with it. Build the thing.
