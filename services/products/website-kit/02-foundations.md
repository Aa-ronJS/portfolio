# 3. Foundations

Domain, hosting, email: an afternoon of setup you do once, and the part
of the project where ownership mistakes get made that cost people their
own websites years later. The rule for this entire document: **every
account is created by you, in your name, with your email and your
payment card.** Not a nephew's account, not a marketing company's
"we'll handle it". Half my paid rescue work is recovering exactly these
accounts for people who let someone else hold them.

## 3.1 The domain

The domain is your address (yourbusiness.com.au) and the single most
important thing you will own.

- **Get the .com.au if you are an Australian business** (it requires an
  ABN, which you have). It reads local, and local is the game. Grab the
  .com too if it is cheap and available; point it at the same place.
- **Buy it from a mainstream registrar** (the companies whose job is
  selling domains; search "domain registrar australia" and pick an
  established one with published pricing). Typically $15 to $40 a
  year for .com.au. Anyone charging $80 is charging you for not
  checking.
- **Name rules:** your business name, or as close as available. No
  hyphens if avoidable, no clever spellings you would have to spell
  out on the phone, shorter beats cuter.
- **Turn on auto-renew and put the renewal date in your calendar
  anyway.** Expired domains get bought by squatters within hours, and
  buying yours back is a hostage negotiation.
- Record the registrar, username and recovery email in the ownership
  register (section 3.4).

## 3.2 Hosting, decided by your build track

Hosting is the computer your site lives on. Which kind you need depends
on the build track you will pick in document 4, so here is just the
shape of the decision; document 4 tells you which paragraph applies:

- **Track A (WordPress)** wants **managed WordPress hosting**: a host
  whose product is specifically "we run WordPress for you", handling
  updates, backups and security underneath you. Typically $10 to $30 a
  month from reputable providers. The $3-a-month bargain tier is where
  slow, hacked WordPress sites come from; the difference is real and
  the kit is not neutral about it.
- **Track B (AI-built static site)** wants a **static hosting
  platform**: services designed to serve simple fast sites, generous
  free tiers, custom domain support. Often $0 for a small business
  site, which sounds wrong and is not; you are only serving files.
- Either way: the host is a separate decision and account from the
  domain. Buying them together is convenient and fine, but know they
  are two things; you can change one without the other, and the
  ownership register records both.

## 3.3 Email on your own domain

you@yourbusiness.com.au answers enquiries; yourbusiness2018@gmail.com
answers them a little worse, forever. Two honest options:

- **A proper mail service** (the business email products from the major
  providers; typically $6 to $12 per user per month). Real mailboxes,
  calendars, the works. This is the default recommendation.
- **Email forwarding** (often free from your registrar): mail to
  you@yourbusiness.com.au lands in your existing personal inbox, and
  with a little setup you can send as the business address too. Fine
  at the very start; upgrade when the business email volume is real.

Set it up before launch so the site never has to show a gmail address,
and add the address to the ownership register.

## 3.4 The ownership register

Create one document (paper, notes app, spreadsheet, anywhere you will
find it in three years) listing, for each of domain, hosting, email,
and later your site platform and any tools: the provider, the account
username, the recovery email and phone on the account, what it costs
and when it renews. Ten minutes now. Every rescue job I have ever done
began with reconstructing exactly this document for someone who did not
have it, at consulting rates, under deadline, sometimes with a hostile
ex-developer holding the answers.

While you are there: turn on two-factor authentication on all of these
accounts. Your website is exactly as secure as the email account that
can reset its passwords.
