What I actually check

Not a dashboard tour. A reconciliation.

Every HubSpot health check I have seen sold is a list of empty properties and a screenshot of your lifecycle funnel. That is not where the money goes missing. It goes missing at the joins.

01

The key your records were merged on.

Email address is the default and it is usually wrong. People change email, couples share one, staff leave. I find out what your source system used as its identifier and count how many contacts and companies were merged on something else. In the charity's case, that one question was the whole $3.8 million.

02

Whether the totals agree with anything outside HubSpot.

Your revenue, donation or deal totals are compared to the system that has to be right at tax time: Xero, your payment platform, your old CRM export. I report the gap in dollars and in records, and where it comes from. A dashboard that agrees only with itself is not evidence.

03

Associations, not just objects.

Deals with no contact. Contacts with no company. Tickets orphaned when someone was merged. These are what make a report say a customer bought nothing when they bought four times, and they are invisible from the contact record.

04

Workflows and syncs that are quietly doing nothing.

Enrolment triggers on properties nobody fills any more, integration sync errors that have been accumulating for months, sequences pointing at owners who have left. Each one is listed with what it was supposed to do and whether it still does.

05

The fix list, in order, with honest sizes.

Some fixes are an afternoon. Some are a rebuild on the right key. You get them ranked by what they are costing you, with the effort beside each, so you can hand the list to anyone, including someone who is not me.
