Where it came from

Ten years of writing the brief.

I have been a business analyst since 2015 and building the automation myself since 2016, in mining, policing, federal service delivery and local government. The job was always the same: work out what a room full of people actually need, write it so a delivery team cannot build the wrong thing, and then check what came back. An AI agent is that delivery team, and it turns out to be the same skill.

  • The check that caught the agent overclaiming

    Both of my public tools carry a script whose only job is to compare the result against something I do not control. It has caught things my own tests never could, and it is what video two teaches.

  • The adversarial run that found the security holes

    Reviewing a live shopping platform written mostly by AI, a second run with no knowledge of the first logged into somebody else's account and shut down their broadcast. Video three is how to set that up on your own project.

  • The test that could only ever pass

    A performance page that always came back perfect, because it was scoring blanks out of blanks. The project's own notes had it down as verified. This is what "done is a claim" means in practice.

  • The one reported problem that was not one

    An automated tool flagged a leaked key. I traced it and told them to ignore it. A good check catches false alarms as well as real ones, or it costs you a week chasing ghosts.
