Where it came from

Built for one page. Checked on the screen.

Every effect in the course was built for my own site, answered in writing against a design pre-flight before any markup, and then checked in a browser at four widths. The card on the right is the tilt from video three. Move your pointer over it. On a phone it is flat, on purpose.

  • The call to action that never appeared

    The hero button was scroll-revealed, and the observer's margin put it outside the trigger zone, so the most important element on the page sat at opacity zero forever. The markup was fine. Only the render showed it. The fix is a rule, and it is in video two.

  • Every photo card rendered portrait

    A height attribute on the image plus an aspect ratio in the CSS, and the ratio silently lost. Every card came out tall at 933 pixels. One declaration fixes it, and checklist two makes you look.

  • The same photograph, twice on one screen

    Once as a plate, once in a tilted card, and it read as a mistake rather than a composition. The plates now belong to the statement sections only. That is a design rule, not a code one, and it is in checklist one.

  • Letters colliding at hero size

    Display tracking that looked right at 24 pixels put letters into each other at 160. Caught by looking, fixed by a single value. Motion draws the eye to type; the type has to survive the attention.
