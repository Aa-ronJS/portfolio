018 min

The drawing.

The template on your phone. Put the two red dots down first, then draw the part around them. The five parts that are required, the ones that unlock behaviour for good, and why nothing is ever mirrored. Export, send, and Claude ingests it, checks it, and turns it to face the right way.

Then the check sheet: every part over magenta, three walk phases on a ground line. You look at it before you use the character, every time, because "it probably ingested fine" is usually wrong somewhere.

027 min

The voice.

The whole episode as one voice memo. "In the chip shop, Dave walks in from the left and says." Half a second of silence. The line. That is the entire grammar, and it is seven verbs. Claude splits the take on the pauses, casts whoever you named, stages the shots, sets the captions from your words, and draft-renders it.

Mouth flaps come from the loudness of your voice, never from a transcript, which is why they land on the words. Deadpan, first take, is usually the right take.

037 min

The fixes, and the cadence.

Every correction is a sentence. Smaller. Higher. From the right. Close up on him. Three in one message, another draft, then the final render and the post. What the episode file is, and why you never need to open it. Props, stock moves, and the one rule about sound.

Then how this becomes an account and not a hobby: batch by activity, characters are capital, and after three episodes a new one is a yaml file and a voice memo.

Who it is for

You have the jokes. You do not have a week.

This is for you if

  • You write funny things and have wanted an animated account without learning animation, hiring an animator, or showing your face.
  • You have a voice you are willing to record deadpan into a phone. That is the whole performance.
  • You can draw badly. The style is the strategy: visuals so crude that one drawing per character is a finished asset, so all the time goes into the writing.

This is not for you if

  • You want it to look smooth. Twelve frames a second, a hand-held wobble and a two-frame mouth are the look, on purpose, and the pipeline refuses to smooth them.
  • You want a synthetic voice or generated jokes. The pipeline will never add either. The human voice and the human writing are the entire point.
  • You want to learn Python. You will not touch it. If you want to, the source is public and the manual is long.

  • Why the drawings have to be bad

    Because a good drawing sets an expectation the next one has to meet, and then you are an animator. A crude drawing sets no expectation, so a character drawn once in ten minutes can carry a hundred episodes, and the audience votes for the voice and the joke.

  • Why one voice memo

    The pause is the cut. Speak the direction, pause, speak the line, pause. The pipeline splits the take on the silences and every line becomes a shot whose length is the length of what you said. There is no timeline to edit because you already edited it by talking.

  • What Claude actually does

    Runs the tools, reads the check sheet, tells you which optional boxes you left empty and what each would unlock, stages the shots from your directions, and takes corrections in plain sentences. It does not write a word of dialogue. The manual it works from forbids it.

  • What a mistake looks like

    The first committed demo was staged from a robot voice as a worst-case test, and the captions came out chewed. It went up on Instagram as it was, and the chewed captions landed as part of the joke. Post the first one before you fix it.

Questions

The ones people ask.

  • Do I really never touch a terminal?

    Never. Claude Code runs in the browser on your phone, in a copy of the repository. You attach the sheet or the voice memo, paste the card, and it does the rest and sends the file back. Every command it runs is in the public manual if you are curious.

  • Isn't the pipeline already free?

    Yes. It is in the same repository as this page, under the MIT licence, and I say so above rather than hoping you do not notice. You are paying for the 22 minutes that shows the loop, the cards, and the checklists. If reading manuals is how you learn, keep the $35.

  • How long is an episode, and how long does it take?

    Under thirty seconds of video, from under two minutes of talking. The first one takes an afternoon because you are drawing a character. After three episodes you have a cast, and a new one is a voice memo and a cup of tea while it renders.

  • Can I use my own voice? Can I use someone else's?

    Your own, recorded, is the point. A friend's, recorded, is fine. A synthetic voice is not possible with this pipeline and will not become possible; the manual forbids adding it.

  • What if my drawing does not ingest cleanly?

    Claude sends you the check sheet and says which part has the problem, and you redraw that one box, not the character. Checklist one is the four rules that stop it happening: dots first, draw past the joint, close every outline, never mirror.

  • Why is it a beta?

    Because I would rather find out whether people want it before I spend a week polishing it. The cards and checklists are complete now. The recordings arrive within seven days, and every revision after that, and the guarantee covers all of it.
