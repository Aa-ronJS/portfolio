# 4. Green-zone gauge for dog ramps

**One line:** "Extend until it's green. Then your dog will use it."

**Score: 10/14.** Pain 2, workaround 1, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 1, tailwind 1, access 1.

## The problem

- Older dogs refuse ramps mainly because they're too steep, slippery or
  wobbly ([Dog Orthopedic](https://www.dogorthopedic.com/tools/dog-wont-use-ramp)).
- Guides put the safe slope at 18 to 25 degrees, nearer 18 to 20 for
  arthritic dogs. Above 30 many dogs won't climb
  ([Senior Dog Comfort](https://seniordogcomfort.com/dog-ramp-angle-and-length-a-safe-slope-guide-for-senior-dogs/)).
- With a car ramp, the angle depends on how high the car is and how far
  the ramp is extended. Owners can't see the number, and the advice is
  to check with a phone app.
- Senior dogs are underserved generally
  ([ShelfTrend](https://www.shelftrend.com/other-categories/senior-dog-products-market-analysis-seller-profit-guide-2026)),
  and Australians spend A$21.3 billion a year on pets
  ([Global Pet Industry](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/pet-spending-in-australia-increases-by-35-since-2022-reaching-a21-3-billion-in-one-year/)).

## The product

A small gravity gauge moulded into the ramp's side rail: a ball in a
curved tube, or a weighted pointer.

- Three colour bands behind it: green (under 20 degrees, any dog),
  amber (20 to 25, healthy dogs only), red (too steep).
- On a telescoping ramp the instruction is one line: "Extend until the
  ball is in the green."
- No batteries. A few cents of plastic.
- Also sold as a stick-on gauge for ramps people already own.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A pet ramp having a walking surface and a side member, and a
gravity-responsive inclination indicator fixed to the ramp, the
indicator having a movable element and a scale divided into at least
two visually distinct zones corresponding to ranges of inclination
suitable and unsuitable for an animal, such that a user can adjust the
ramp's length or support until the element lies in the suitable zone.*

Variants to include:

- Zones matched to animal condition (arthritic, older, healthy).
- A telescoping ramp where extension changes the reading.
- A separate adhesive indicator for existing ramps.
- Zone limits printed for dog size or weight.

## Bench test (about A$30 to A$60, my estimate)

**Buy:** a ball-in-tube slope gauge (sold for boats, caravans and
4WDs), coloured tape. Borrow a telescoping dog ramp.

**Do:**

1. Stick the gauge to the ramp's side rail. Mark green, amber and red
   bands using a phone angle app.
2. Set the ramp against a car boot at three extensions.
3. Ask five people who've never seen it to "set it up safely" with only
   the line "extend until it's green". Time them.
4. If you can, film a dog refusing it in red and using it in green.

**It fails if** people don't notice the gauge, or it can't be read from
standing height.
