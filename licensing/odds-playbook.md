# Raising the odds

The earnings estimates assumed a cold pitch with a drawing and no proof.
This file lists what raises the odds, in order of effect, and contains the
working documents for each: the follow-up emails, the deal structure and
the evidence plan.

My estimate of the effect, as judgement and not data: done properly,
these roughly double the chance of a deal on the two best ideas, from
about 10% and 8% to about 20% and 15%, and lift the chance that at least
one of the five lands from about 28% to about 45%.

## The ten levers

| # | Lever | Why it works | Cost |
|---|-------|--------------|------|
| 1 | Show it working on film | A company can say no to a drawing in five seconds. A 60-second film of the thing working removes the first objection | About A$100 per product |
| 2 | Bring a number | "The dots faded 60% and spin dropped by X" is evidence. "I think players want this" isn't | A$0 to A$500 |
| 3 | Pitch 30 or more companies per idea, at once | Stephen Key calls licensing a numbers game and says rejection is part of it | Time |
| 4 | Follow up four times, with the sell sheet attached each time | Key says people rarely follow up as often as they should, and to resend the sell sheet so nobody has to dig for it | Time |
| 5 | Split the patent by market | One patent can give several companies an exclusive each, in different markets. More deals, and each company can say yes without needing the whole thing | A$0 |
| 6 | Make yes cheap | Ask for a low-cost 90-day evaluation first, not a licence | A$0 |
| 7 | Land a small company first | A founder-led brand can decide in a week. Its launch is the proof a large brand wants | Time |
| 8 | Give the kitchen ideas to an agent | An agent with existing contacts pitches for you on commission | 25 to 30% of royalties |
| 9 | Pitch to the calendar | Brands choose features 12 to 18 months before launch (my knowledge, unverified) | A$0 |
| 10 | Strengthen the two best patents | A licensee's lawyer will test them | Low thousands (my estimate) |

Sources: Key on follow-up and numbers
([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/stephen-key/5-reasons-why-following-up-with-potential-licensees-is-essential.html),
[inventRight](https://inventright.com/innovate-this/the-odds-of-you-licensing-that-idea));
field-of-use licensing ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.org/edocs/mdocs/sme/en/wipo_kipo_kwia_inv_sel_14/wipo_kipo_kwia_inv_sel_14_g_mendes.pdf),
[Enhance PD](https://enhancepd.com/patent-license-agreement-explained/));
agent terms ([IPWatchdog, 2009](https://ipwatchdog.com/2009/08/20/introducing-lambert-lambert/)).

## Lever 1 and 2: proof

Do these after the bench test in each product file works.

| Product | The film | The number to bring |
|---------|----------|---------------------|
| Reproof tab | One take: spray a treated tab (blank), abrade it, spray again (REPROOF shows), re-treat, spray (blank) | Abrasion strokes or wash cycles until the word shows, against the same count for an untreated jacket shoulder to wet out |
| Paddle wear marks | Bright reference dots beside faded hitting-zone dots after 10 hours of play | Spin before and after. Ask a reviewer who measures spin to test it: Pickleball Studio runs calibrated spin tests ([The Dink](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/from-rubiks-cube-to-pickleball-paddles-the-rise-of-chris-olson-pickleballs-paddle-review-pro/)) |
| Pan click | The click, then two eggs: one stuck, one sliding | Click temperature over ten runs, with the spread |
| Mug tilt rail | Unloading: puddles on one side, dry on the other | Millilitres of water left on five flat mugs against five tilted |
| Fit mark | Same child, right size then outgrown | Share of ten parents who get it unprompted |

**Demand evidence, after filing only:**

- **Twenty-person survey per product.** Three questions: Have you had
  this problem? What do you do about it now? Would this change which
  brand you buy? Quote the counts in the pitch.
- **Sell a small batch where the product can stand alone.** The stick-on
  reproof patch and the mug rail can be made by hand in tens. Real sales
  are the strongest evidence there is. Crowdfunding has led to licensing
  deals for this reason: Hugalopes with Jakks Pacific, and Hickies laces
  with Brookstone ([drodio](https://drodio.com/from-kickstarter-to-major-toy-deal-the-story-of-the-hugalopes/),
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=858456)).

**Never** invent survey numbers, claim sales that didn't happen, or say
"patent pending" before the provisional is filed.

## Lever 4: the follow-up sequence

Send the first email from the product file. Then these, each with the
sell sheet attached.

**Day 7**

> Subject: Re: [original subject]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Sell sheet attached again so you don't have to hunt for it. The short
> version: [one-line benefit].
>
> If you're not the right person for new product ideas, who is?
>
> Aaron Steele

**Day 21, with something new**

> Subject: [Product]: test result
>
> Hi [name],
>
> A quick update. [One new fact: the bench number, the survey count, or
> a reviewer's result.]
>
> Film here: [link]. Sell sheet attached.
>
> Worth fifteen minutes?
>
> Aaron Steele

**Day 45**

> Subject: [Product]: before I offer it elsewhere in [category]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm offering exclusive rights for [their market] to one company. I'm
> in conversation with others in [category] and wanted to check with
> you before going further.
>
> If the answer is no, a one-line reason would help me a lot.
>
> Aaron Steele

Only send the day 45 email if it's true: there must be other companies
in the pitch round. Pitching 30 at once makes it true.

**Day 90:** one last note when anything material changes (a deal
elsewhere, a patent step, press). Then stop. Key's warning is that
people who send too many emails get avoided.

## Lever 5: split the patent by market

| Product | Markets to license separately |
|---------|-------------------------------|
| Reproof tab | 1. Care products (the stick-on patch) 2. Outdoor clothing 3. Tents, swags and camping 4. Marine covers and awnings 5. Workwear and rainwear for work |
| Paddle wear marks | 1. Pickleball 2. Padel 3. Table tennis 4. Replaceable face sheets |
| Pan click | 1. Stand-alone gadget 2. Boxed with cookware 3. TV and direct sales |

Each licensee gets an exclusive in its own market. Start with the market
where the company profits most directly: care products for the reproof
tab.

## Lever 6: the offer, on one page

Use this as the opening position. Have a lawyer draft the real agreement.

| Term | Opening position |
|------|------------------|
| First step | 90-day evaluation option: exclusive look in their market for a small fee (A$1,000 to A$2,500), credited against royalties |
| Licence | Exclusive in one defined market and territory |
| Royalty | 5% of net wholesale for a stand-alone product; a fixed amount per unit for a feature on another product |
| Advance | Modest, recoupable against royalties |
| Minimum | Annual minimum royalty from 18 months after signing |
| Performance | Product on sale within 18 months, or the licence becomes non-exclusive or ends |
| Patent costs | Licensee pays for the full applications in its markets |
| Improvements | Yours stay yours; theirs stay theirs |
| Audit | Right to inspect sales records once a year |

The numbers are my starting suggestions, drawn from typical ranges:
royalties of 3 to 6% for consumer goods, small or zero upfront payments,
and minimums starting about 18 months in
([Enhance PD](https://enhancepd.com/upfront-fees-vs-royalty-payments-explained/),
[inventRight](https://inventright.com/inventor-stories/how-to-negotiate-minimum-guarantees)).
About 35% of patent licences end early, which is why the minimum and
performance terms matter.

## Lever 7: small companies first

| Product | First targets | Why |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Paddle wear marks | Six Zero (Australia), Reload (US), CRBN (US) | Founder-led. Six Zero's founder is a materials engineer |
| Pan click | Solidteknics (Australia) | Founder-led maker of uncoated pans, where preheating matters most |
| Reproof tab | Grangers, Gear Aid, then Nikwax | Smaller care brands move faster, and their interest is leverage with the largest |

## Lever 8: an agent for the kitchen ideas

The pan click and mug rail are the lowest-value ideas and take the same
effort to pitch. An agent can do it on commission.

- **Lambert & Lambert (US):** historically charged an evaluation fee,
  then worked on contingency for 25 to 30% of licensing revenue. That
  figure is from 2009; confirm current terms.
- **MarketBlast:** a free submitter account gives access to company
  "hunts", including ones run for Lifetime Brands, Cuisinart and
  Shurtape.
- Avoid anyone who charges large fees up front to "submit" an idea.

## Free money worth checking

These depend on where you live and whether you have an ABN. I haven't
confirmed any are open.

- **Innovate NSW:** matched funding up to A$15,000 to prove a concept or
  build a prototype.
- **Gold Coast SME Boost voucher:** up to A$5,000 per project. Round one
  closed in March 2026.
- **ACT prototyping vouchers:** up to A$10,000 of workshop access. Pilot
  due to end mid-2026.
