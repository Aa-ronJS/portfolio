# 1. Reproof tab

**One line:** "Your jacket tells you when it needs reproofing, before you get wet."

**Score: 13/14.** Pain 2, workaround 2, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 2, tailwind 2, access 1.

## The problem

- PFAS ("forever chemical") water-repellent coatings are being banned.
  California's ban on PFAS in clothing began January 2025, and the
  exemption for severe-wet-weather gear ends 1 January 2028
  ([Taft Law](https://taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/new-california-bill-restricts-pfas-content-in-textiles)).
- The PFAS-free replacements wear out faster and need re-treating about
  every one to two seasons ([Alpkit](https://us.alpkit.com/blogs/spotlight/reproofing-your-waterproof),
  [Woven](https://woveapp.com/blog/pfas-free-rain-jackets/)).
- When the coating fails the outer fabric soaks ("wets out") and owners
  think the jacket leaks. Retailers say this is behind most leak
  complaints ([Trekitt](https://www.trekitt.co.uk/blog/why-is-your-waterproof-jacket-wetting-out/)).
- The only check today is pouring water on the shoulders
  ([Alpkit](https://alpkit.com/blogs/spotlight/how-to-reproof-waterproof-clothing)).

## The product

A 25 x 40 mm tab on the shoulder, where pack straps rub hardest.

- **Top layer:** thin, pale fabric carrying the same water-repellent
  coating as the jacket.
- **Under it:** the word REPROOF printed in a dark colour.
- **While the coating works:** water beads off, the pale layer stays dry
  and opaque, and the tab looks blank.
- **When the coating fails:** the pale layer soaks, turns see-through (as
  wet pale fabric does), and REPROOF shows. It disappears again when dry
  and stays hidden after re-treating.

Two versions:

| Version | Sold by | How |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Sewn-in | Jacket brands | Added at the factory, coated in the same bath as the jacket |
| Stick-on patch | Reproofing brands | Packed with the bottle; re-treated along with the jacket each time |

An alternative build uses water-reveal ink (white when dry, clear when
wet), which is sold for screen printing
([SFXC](https://www.sfxc.co.uk/products/hydrochromic-ink-paint-for-fabric)).

## Wider market: outdoor fabrics

The same tab works on anything with a re-treatable water-repellent
finish: tents, swags, awnings, boat covers, outdoor cushions.

- Glen Raven, which makes Sunbrella outdoor fabric, tells owners to
  re-treat "after thorough cleaning or as needed" and names 303 Fabric
  Guard as its preferred product
  ([Sunbrella](https://www.sunbrella.com/blog/cleaning--protecting-with-303)).
- The check is the same water-drop test: "if the water soaks in, it's
  time to re-treat"
  ([Sailrite](https://www.sailrite.com/how-to-treat-sunbrella-fabric)).
- So there is already a fabric maker and a re-treatment brand working
  as a pair, with no signal between them.

## Prior art found

| Reference | What it is | Why it doesn't block |
|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| [US 5,435,010](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5435010) | Clothing that changes colour when wet | Reacts to any water; says nothing about a coating failing. Issued 1995, so expired |
| [US 10,993,484](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10993484) | Fabric with coated and uncoated areas that show a pattern when wet | The pattern shows whenever it rains. Ours shows only after the coating wears out. Closest reference: have an attorney read its claims |
| [US 2009/0064919](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20090064919A1/en) | Tag that changes colour with water or sweat | A use indicator, not a wear indicator |

| [US 9,578,908](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9578908) (Under Armour; active, runs to about 2035) | Clothing with a coloured part hidden under a second coloured layer that fades with wear and washing, exposing the first | **Found on a second pass. The most important one to design around.** Its claims need the covering layer to fade. Ours never fades: it turns see-through only while wet, and only once the coating has gone. It also covers clothing only, not tents, awnings or covers |

I found no product and no patent for an indicator of coating failure.

**Design rules that keep clear of the two live patents** (my reading, not
legal advice):

- The covering layer must not fade or wear away. The reveal comes from
  wetting only.
- The tab is coated all over. No pattern of coated and uncoated patches,
  which is what Nike's US 10,993,484 claims.
- The stick-on patch for care brands is the safest first product: it is
  sold as an accessory, and the same design works on non-clothing.

A closer read of US 10,993,484 confirms it is about showing that the
ground or air is wet. It says nothing about the coating wearing out.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A wear indicator for a water-repellent finish on a textile article,
comprising: (a) an indicator layer of textile carrying a water-repellent
finish and positioned on the article at a location subject to abrasion;
(b) a marking beneath or within the indicator layer that is concealed
while the indicator layer is dry and revealed when the indicator layer
absorbs water; such that the marking is revealed on exposure to water
only after the finish on the indicator layer has degraded.*

Variants to include:

- The indicator layer is the garment's own face fabric with a marking
  printed on its inner side.
- The marking is concealed by a water-reveal (hydrochromic) ink beneath
  the finish.
- The indicator carries a finish chosen to fail slightly earlier than
  the garment's, giving advance warning.
- A separate adhesive or iron-on patch supplied with a re-treatment
  product.
- Use on tents, footwear, gloves, workwear and bags.
- A graded version with two or three markings that appear at different
  stages of wear.

## Bench test (about A$100 to A$150, my estimate)

**Buy:** pale ripstop nylon offcuts, a PFAS-free spray-on DWR (for
example Nikwax TX.Direct spray), a dark fabric marker, water-reveal ink,
fine sandpaper, a spray bottle.

**Do:**

1. Write REPROOF on a dark-on-pale backing. Lay the pale ripstop over it.
2. Treat the ripstop with the DWR and let it cure.
3. Spray with water. The word must stay hidden.
4. Rub the ripstop 50 strokes with sandpaper, or wash it five times
   without reproofing. Spray again. The word must appear.
5. Re-treat and spray. The word must stay hidden again.
6. Film steps 3 to 5 in one take. That's the pitch video.

**It fails if** the word shows through when dry, or the tab wears much
slower than a jacket shoulder. Fix by changing fabric weight or tab
position.

## Risks

- The tab must wear at the same rate as the shoulder, or slightly faster.
- Brands may not want a visible "this has worn out" message. The
  reproofing brands don't have that problem.
- US 10,993,484 needs an attorney's read.
