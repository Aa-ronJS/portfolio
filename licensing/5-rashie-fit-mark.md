# 5. Fit mark for sun-protective swim shirts

**One line:** "When the sun turns stripy, it's time for the next size."

**Score: 10/14.** Pain 2, workaround 1, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 1, tailwind 1, access 1.

## The problem

- Sun-protective swim shirts (rash vests, "rashies") are rated UPF 50+
  when relaxed. Stretching the fabric opens the knit and lowers the
  protection. CHOICE says to look for a relaxed fit, especially across
  the shoulders, because stretching reduces UPF
  ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/shopping/everyday-shopping/clothing/articles/rash-vests-rashies?nb=1)).
- Brands print the same warning on their own listings: protection "may
  be reduced if the material is wet, stretched or from the effects of
  normal wear" ([Country Road](https://countryroad.co.nz/stripe-logo-rash-vest-60297619-569)).
- REI advises replacing any item that fits too tightly
  ([REI](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/sun-protection)).
- Children outgrow them, and parents keep using last summer's. Nothing
  on the garment says it's now too small. The only advice is to check
  it wet for transparency.

## The product

A printed mark across the shoulders or chest: a solid sun icon.

- It's printed in dark ink on the relaxed fabric, as a pattern of fine
  bars set close together.
- At a normal fit the bars sit closed and the sun looks solid.
- When the fabric is stretched past a set point, pale base fabric shows
  between the bars and the sun turns visibly striped.
- The bar spacing is set so the stripes appear at the stretch where the
  fabric's protection drops below its label rating.

It's one extra print in a process the garment already goes through.

## Why a brand would want it

- It sells the next size up at exactly the right moment.
- It turns a small-print warning the brand already gives into a visible
  safety feature.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A sun-protective garment of stretchable fabric having an ultraviolet
protection rating in a relaxed state, and a printed indicator on a
region of the garment subject to stretch when worn, the indicator
comprising printed elements separated by gaps that are visually closed
in the relaxed state and visually open when the fabric is extended
beyond a predetermined amount, the predetermined amount corresponding
to an extension at which the protection of the fabric falls below a
stated rating.*

Variants to include:

- Placement across the shoulders, chest or upper back.
- A contrasting colour or word revealed in the gaps.
- Calibration to a particular fabric and rating.
- Use on swim leggings, sun sleeves and sports tops.

## Bench test (about A$40 to A$80, my estimate)

**Buy:** two plain pale children's rash vests in consecutive sizes, a
fabric marker or screen-print ink, a ruler.

**Do:**

1. Lay the smaller vest flat. Draw a 5 cm sun made of 1 mm bars with
   hairline gaps.
2. Stretch the fabric over a board by 10%, 20% and 30%. Photograph each.
   Note where the stripes become obvious at arm's length.
3. Put the vest on a child it fits and on one a size too big for it.
   Photograph both.
4. Show ten parents the two photos with the line "stripy means too
   small". Count how many get it.

**It fails if** the stripes show at a normal fit, or can't be seen at
arm's length.

## Risks

- Protection is thinner than for products 1 and 4 because of the nappy
  fit-indicator reference.
- I couldn't find a usable figure for how much UPF drops per percent of
  stretch. The one study in the results had a garbled number. The
  calibration needs a lab test on the licensee's fabric.
- Every pitch is a cold approach.
