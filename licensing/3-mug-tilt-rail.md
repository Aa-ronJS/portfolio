# 3. Mug tilt rail

**One line:** "No more puddles on your mugs."

**Score: 11/14.** Pain 2, workaround 2, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 1, tailwind 0, access 2.

## The problem

- Mugs sit upside down in the dishwasher, and the dished base holds a
  puddle of dirty water. It spills over the clean dishes when you unload.
- The complaint is big enough that "tilt your rack" hacks went viral and
  were covered by [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/this-tiktok-dishwasher-hack-will-keep-your-mugs-dry),
  [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/dishwasher-top-rack-tilt-hack-37562091),
  [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2170326/tilt-dishwasher-rack-trick-explained/),
  [Parade](https://parade.com/food/dishwasher-rack-tilting-trick) and
  [Hunker](https://www.hunker.com/13774029/dishwasher-mug-hack/).
- The hacks don't work well. One test found the rack tilts only about 4
  degrees, and on some machines the door won't close or the spray arm is
  blocked ([House Digest](https://www.housedigest.com/1272744/we-tried-it-dishwasher-hack-prevent-pooling/)).
- Appliance manuals admit the problem and tell owners to lean cups at
  the sides of the rack ([Kenmore manual](https://manualslib.fr/manual/32867/Kenmore-587-1522.html?page=20)).
- Two searches found no accessory sold for this.

## The product

A silicone-covered rail about 20 cm long that presses down over a row of
tines on the top rack and stays there.

- Along the rail are four or five raised wedges, each about 12 mm high.
- Each mug sits with one side of its rim on a wedge. On an 8 cm mug that
  gives a tilt of about 8 to 9 degrees, twice what the rack hack manages.
- Slots underneath fit different tine spacings.
- Sold in packs of two.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A retrofit accessory for a dishwasher rack having upstanding tines,
comprising an elongate body with engagement formations on its underside
for releasable attachment to a row of tines, and a plurality of spaced
raised supports on its upper side, each positioned to raise one side of
the rim of an inverted drinking vessel so the vessel rests at an incline
and liquid drains from its base.*

Variants to include:

- Slots sized for a range of tine spacings and wire diameters.
- Individual clip-on wedges instead of a rail.
- A retaining lip to stop the mug sliding off under spray.
- Support height chosen to give 6 to 15 degrees of tilt.

## Bench test (about A$40 to A$80, my estimate)

**Buy:** mouldable silicone putty (for example Sugru), a strip of
dishwasher-safe plastic or a silicone trivet to cut up.

**Do:**

1. Mould five 12 mm wedges onto the strip, spaced to suit your mugs.
   Press slots into the underside to grip the tines.
2. Run a normal cycle with five mugs on the rail and five flat beside
   them.
3. Open the door and photograph the bases before touching anything.
4. Repeat on two other dishwashers (friends, family).
5. Film the unload: puddles on one side, dry on the other.

**It fails if** the tilted mugs fall over, come out dirty inside, or the
rail won't stay on different racks.

## Risks

- The handle-down loading trick is free and partly works.
- Rack designs vary a lot. Fit is the main engineering problem.
- No tailwind: this sells on a universal annoyance alone.
