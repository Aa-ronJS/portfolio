# 4. Wear marks for pickleball paddles

**One line:** "When the dots fade, your spin is gone."

**Score: 12/14.** Pain 2, workaround 2, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 1, tailwind 2, access 1. (Was 13 before the closer patent search below.)

## The problem

- Modern paddles get their spin from a rough face. That roughness wears
  off. One source estimates a 40 to 50% loss of surface grit after about
  50 hours of play, and players report shiny sweet spots after six months
  ([Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/how-paddle-grit-wears-out/),
  [The Pickleball Gang](https://thepickleballgang.com/best-selkirk-pickleball-paddle/)).
  These are blog estimates, not lab data.
- Worn texture can't be restored and no maker resurfaces paddles
  ([Pickleball Galaxy Q&A](https://www.pickleballgalaxy.com/answers/4032964/Please-list-level-of-grit-comparing-Joola-paddles-with-abrasion-grip-or-friction-surfaces)).
- There is no way to measure it at home. The advice is to run a finger
  over the face, or compare it with a new paddle
  ([The Pickler](https://thepickler.com/pickleball-blog/pickleball-paddle-dead)).
- Patent filings by paddle makers describe the wear problem themselves:
  grit surfaces "become smooth after a short playing period"
  ([US 2021/0252356](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20210252356A1/en)).
- The sport is large and growing: 24.3 million Americans played in 2025,
  up 479% in five years
  ([SFIA](https://sfia.org/research/u-s-pickleball-participation/)).
  Paddle sales are estimated at roughly US$300 to 350 million a year
  ([market reports](https://www.marketresearch.com/Deep-Insights-Research-Co-DIR-v4285/Global-Pickleball-Paddles-Research-Competitive-41677581)).

## The product

Two sets of small printed marks on the paddle face, applied at the
factory.

- **Wear marks:** a ring of dots at the edge of the sweet spot. The ink
  is "kiss printed": the roller touches only the peaks of the rough
  texture, so the colour sits on the same peaks that create spin.
- **Reference marks:** identical dots near the throat, where the ball
  never hits.
- As the peaks wear down, the wear marks fade. The player compares them
  with the reference marks. Same brightness: the face is fine. Faded:
  the grit has gone.

This builds the "compare it with a new paddle" test into every paddle.
It adds one print pass and no parts.

## Why a paddle brand would want it

- It gives players an objective reason to buy the next paddle.
- It's a visible feature competitors don't have.
- Paddles sell for around US$100 to US$280 (my knowledge, not this
  search), so even a 2 to 3% royalty is dollars per paddle, not cents.

| [US 8,430,761](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8430761) (Nike, now Karsten; filed 2009) | Golf club wear indicators, including an outer coating that wears through to a different colour | Also listed as expired. Same effect on novelty |

Two searches found no paddle wear indicator on sale. **A second, closer
patent search found the golf references above.** What is left to claim is
narrower than I first said:

1. the marking sits only on the peaks of a spin texture, so its fading
   tracks the loss of roughness and not general face wear; and
2. matching reference marks in a zone the ball never hits.

That is a real difference, but a patent examiner may call it an obvious
step from the golf patents. I've cut the score for "something to
protect" from 2 to 1 and the odds of a deal from 8% to 6%. Have an
attorney confirm both golf patents have lapsed and give a view on what
can be claimed before filing.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A paddle or racket having a hitting face with a textured surface of
peaks and valleys, and wear indicia comprising a marking material
applied substantially only to the peaks within a first region subject
to ball impact, such that abrasion of the peaks removes the marking
material and the visible fading of the indicia corresponds to loss of
surface roughness.*

Variants to include:

- Reference indicia of the same material in a second region not subject
  to impact, for side-by-side comparison.
- Graded indicia in two or three colours or ink depths that vanish at
  different levels of wear.
- Indicia forming part of the brand graphics.
- Application by roller, pad or screen printing after the texture is
  formed.
- Use on padel rackets, table tennis rubbers and replaceable topsheets.

## Bench test (about A$80 to A$150, my estimate)

**Buy:** an inexpensive raw-carbon-fibre paddle, an oil-based paint
marker or stamp pad, a few outdoor pickleballs, fine sandpaper.

**Do:**

1. Lightly drag the ink across the face so it catches only the high
   points. Make six dots near the sweet spot and six near the throat.
   Check under a magnifier that the valleys are clean.
2. Photograph both sets.
3. Rub the sweet-spot dots with a ball, 500 firm strokes, or play 10
   hours. Photograph again.
4. Confirm by feel that the faded area is smoother than the throat.
5. Film the before and after next to each other.

**It fails if** the ink fills the valleys, wears off much faster than
the texture, or changes how the face plays.

## Risks

- **Rules.** USA Pickleball requires a design registration form for
  graphic changes to approved paddles, and tests surface roughness
  ([rules summary](https://www.playpickleball.com/2025-usa-pickleball-rules-section-2-court-and-equipment/)).
  The marks must not change the roughness reading. Aftermarket marks on
  an approved paddle are not allowed, so this is factory-only.
- **Brands may not want to advertise wear.** Grit wear is excluded from
  warranties, and a visible indicator could prompt claims. Pitch it as
  a replacement driver.
- **The wear figures behind the pain are blog estimates.**
- No open submission routes: every pitch is a cold approach.
