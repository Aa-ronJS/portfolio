# 2. Pan-ready click disc

**One line:** "It clicks when your pan is ready. No more stuck eggs."

**Score: 12/14.** Pain 2, workaround 2, five-second 2, fits 1, protectable 1, tailwind 2, access 2.

## The problem

- PFAS bans are pushing people off traditional non-stick. Minnesota
  banned PFAS in cookware in 2025
  ([ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/minnesota-becomes-first-state-to-ban-pfas-in-cookware-021225.html)),
  and 65% of US adults say they're concerned about these chemicals in
  their pans ([Consumer Reports survey, via Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/discover/cookware-market-statistics-2026)).
- The two replacements both depend on getting the temperature right:
  - **Stainless steel** sticks unless it's properly preheated. The
    standard advice is the "water drop test": flick water in and see if
    it rolls like mercury
    ([The Skillful Cook](https://theskillfulcook.com/how-to-know-when-stainless-steel-pan-is-ready/)).
  - **Ceramic non-stick** loses its slickness when overheated
    ([Caraway](https://www.carawayhome.com/blog/ceramic-vs-nonstick)).
- T-fal's red Thermo-Spot is built into its own pans and only shows
  "ready" ([T-fal](https://www.t-fal.com/ceramic-excellence-non-stick-frypan-12-inch-grey.html)).
  Nothing works with the pans people already own.

## The product

A coin-sized stainless disc you drop in the pan while it heats.

- Inside is a bimetal snap disc: two bonded metals that flip from dished
  to domed at a set temperature. The same part switches off electric
  kettles ([Material Sample Shop](https://www.materialsampleshop.com/products/bimetal-snap-disc)).
- At the set temperature the dome pops up with a click and shows a
  coloured top.
- It resets by itself as it cools. No batteries.
- A small ring on top lets you lift it out with a fork or tongs.

| Version | Set point | Message |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Stainless and cast iron | about 190°C | "Ready: add oil" |
| Ceramic and non-stick | about 230°C | "Too hot: turn it down" |

I found no loose, reusable pan indicator on sale. The claim has to rest
on the combination: a free-standing food-safe indicator for use inside
cookware, with an audible and visible signal.

## Draft description for a provisional

*A reusable temperature indicator for placement on the cooking surface
of a cooking vessel, comprising: a food-safe housing with a flat
heat-conducting base; a bistable bimetallic element within the housing
that changes from a first to a second stable shape at a predetermined
temperature and returns on cooling; and an indicator surface that is
hidden in the first shape and displayed in the second; the change being
accompanied by an audible sound.*

Variants to include:

- Two elements in one housing with different set points (ready, too hot).
- A lifting feature for removal with a utensil.
- A rim-hung version with a conducting foot resting on the pan floor.
- Feet or a coating that will not scratch ceramic surfaces.
- Set points chosen for particular cookware types or oils.

## Bench test (about A$60 to A$120, my estimate)

**Buy:** a handful of KSD301-type snap thermostats rated near 190°C
(sold cheaply online for appliance repair; each contains a bimetal snap
disc), an infrared thermometer, a stainless frying pan.

**Do:**

1. Put a thermostat face-down in a dry pan on medium heat.
2. Note the infrared reading when it clicks. Repeat ten times.
3. Straight after the click, add oil and fry an egg. Compare with an egg
   added to a pan heated for only 30 seconds.
4. Repeat on gas, electric and induction.
5. Film the click and the two eggs side by side.

**It fails if** the click point varies by more than about 15°C, or the
induction hob heats the disc directly and it clicks early. Bimetals are
usually iron-based, so the induction check matters most.

## Risks

- Induction hobs may heat the disc directly.
- It's hot when you remove it. The lifting ring has to be obvious.
- Protection is moderate: the snap-disc mechanism is old, so the claim
  is on the cookware accessory.
