# Cat Picasso

**Hook:** "Your cat makes the painting. You hang it up and fit a fresh canvas."

**Score: 10/14.** Pain 1, workaround 1, five-second 2, fits 2, protectable 1, tailwind 1, access 2.

The wrap-around, replaceable design is Aaron's. It replaced the flat
board in the first sketch and fixed that sketch's main weakness.

## What it is

A scratching post with a removable wrap. The wrap is several layers of
card in different colours under a dark outer layer. Each scratch tears
through to a colour. When the cat has finished the "painting", the wrap
comes off, unrolls flat and goes in a frame. A fresh wrap goes on the
post.

## Why the wrap is the right design

| The flat board had this problem | The wrap solves it |
|---------------------------------|--------------------|
| Cats may ignore a flat face | Adult cats in one study preferred upright poles covered in rope or cardboard, and used flat cardboard less ([Walkerville Vet summary](https://www.walkervillevet.com.au/?p=18524)) |
| One sale per customer | Every finished artwork needs a new wrap. Refills are the business |
| Needs its own stand | A wrap can strap onto a post the owner already has |
| Odd shape to frame | A wrap sized to the post unrolls to a standard frame size |

Replaceable wraps are already a proven product. Omlet sells a
replacement sisal sleeve for US$55, and One Fast Cat and others sell
their own ([Omlet](https://www.omlet.us/cat-scratching-posts/),
[One Fast Cat](https://onefastcat.com/products/woven-sisal-sleeve)).
Owners already accept buying the scratching surface again. Nobody
sells one that turns into something worth keeping.

## The design

- **Layers:** three or four sheets of single-face corrugated card, the
  kind with one ridged side, which bends around a cylinder. Outer layer
  dark. Inner layers in strong colours.
- **Size:** 30 cm around and 40 cm tall, so it unrolls to a 30 x 40 cm
  rectangle, a common frame size. That fits a post about 9.5 cm across.
- **Fastening:** two hook-and-loop straps, with the seam at the back.
  An overlap of a few centimetres lets one wrap fit posts from about
  8 to 11 cm.
- **Catnip** rubbed into the outer layer.
- **Inks and glue** must be safe if swallowed in small amounts. I found
  no guidance on this, so it needs a supplier's safety data.

**What's in the box:**

| Pack | Contents |
|------|----------|
| Starter | Post and base, one wrap, a 30 x 40 cm frame, a gallery card ("Untitled No. 1, by ______") |
| Refill | Two wraps in a named colour set, two gallery cards |
| Wrap only | One wrap with straps, for a post you already own |

| Reference | What it is | What it means |

They confirm a replaceable cover can't be claimed |
| [US 7,765,956](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7765956B2/en) | A shell that presents changeable scratching surfaces | Not read at claim level |
| Decorated scratchers (a framed "oil painting" scratcher sold by Chewy; hand-decorated ones on Etsy) | A picture printed on or beside the scratching surface | The picture doesn't change. The cat doesn't make it |

Two searches found no scratcher whose colours are revealed by the cat,
in products or patents. The second search was mainly about sleeves, so
the reveal idea has had one proper search.

**What might be protectable:** coloured layers that the cat's claws
expose, in a wrap that removes and lies flat for display. I don't know
if that would be granted. In novelty and pet products the name, the
look and being first usually matter more than a patent. A registered
design and a trade mark on the name are the cheaper protection.

## Bench test (about A$40 to A$70, my estimate)

**Buy:** rolls of coloured corrugated card from a craft shop (one black,
three bright), double-sided tape, two hook-and-loop straps, catnip
spray, a 30 x 40 cm frame. Use a post you or a friend already own.

**Make two wraps,** so you can compare:

| | Wrap A | Wrap B |
|---|--------|--------|
| Ridges run | Up and down | Around the post |
| Layers joined | At the edges only | All over |

**Do:**

1. Fit wrap A. Photograph it from the same spot every day.
2. After two weeks, or when it looks finished, swap to wrap B.
3. Unroll each one, press it flat under books overnight, and frame it.
4. Show the framed result to five people without explaining it.

**It passes if:**

- the cat uses it within three days;
- at least three colours show by the end of week two;
- it unrolls and frames without falling apart;
- at least one person asks where they can get one.

**It fails if** the cat ignores it, the layers come away in sheets
instead of shredding, or the result looks like rubbish and not like a
painting. The second failure is fixed with the glue pattern. The third
is fixed with the colour choice and the frame.

Film the daily photos as a time-lapse. That clip is the whole pitch.

## What a deal might pay

- **Modest:** 30,000 starter kits and 60,000 refill packs a year at
  about US$18 and US$9 wholesale, with a 5% royalty: about A$80,000 a
  year.
- **Strong:** ten times that: about A$800,000.
- **My odds of a deal:** 7%. Expected worth about A$39,000.

These are my estimates. They put it at the top of the board, mainly
because of the refills.

## Risks

- **It depends on the cat.** Nothing here is known until one has used it.
- **Easy to copy.** Speed and the name matter.
- **Mess.** Shredded card on the floor is normal for cardboard
  scratchers, but the framed piece must not keep shedding.
- **Safety of inks and glue** is unverified.
