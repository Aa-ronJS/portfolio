# Play: toys, games and novelty

A second lane, started because the first one produced useful but dull
products. These ideas are judged on a different first question: **would
someone show this to a friend?**

## Why this lane is different

- **The industry is built for outside inventors.** The Toy Association's
  2026 guide says many toy companies "rely very heavily on the outside
  inventing community". Companies have inventor-relations staff and
  attend pitch events.
- **No patent is needed to pitch.** Toys and games are licensed on a
  prototype and a video. Board game designers typically get 5 to 8% of
  wholesale, with advances of US$500 to US$2,000 for newcomers.
- **One hit is large.** The Exit escape-room games are reported to have
  sold over 25 million copies. Bunch O Balloons raised US$920,000 on
  Kickstarter, was licensed to ZURU, and its inventor says it passed
  US$200 million in sales.
- **Most pitches still fail.** This is hit-driven. The inventor of Bunch
  O Balloons describes his own record as "a moderate success, a complete
  flop and a home run".

Each has had one product search. None has been built or played.

### Cat Picasso

- **What it is:** a flat scratch board built from layers of different
  coloured card under a dark top sheet. Each scratch tears through to a
  colour. After a few weeks the board is an abstract painting, and it
  comes with a frame to hang it in.
- **Why it could travel:** every one is unique and made by the buyer's
  own cat, which is exactly what people post. A cardboard scratcher
  featured by one Costco account drew over 4.9 million views.
- **Market:** cat scratchers are put at about US$0.8 billion a year by
  one market report.
- **Who to pitch:** Suck UK (its site invites idea submissions and it
  already sells novelty cat scratchers), Kazoo (Australian; sells a
  games-console scratcher), Petmate (inventor page), Frisco, KONG, Catit.
- **Biggest doubt:** cats prefer the edge of corrugated card to a flat
  face. It needs a cat and a week.
- **Bench test:** glue five sheets of coloured card under black card on
  a board, rub in catnip, and photograph it daily.

### Hot Hands

- **What it is:** cards coated so that a touch leaves a visible heat
  print for a few seconds. One player turns away, the others each touch
  or don't touch a card, and the detective has seconds to read the
  fading prints and name who is lying.
- **Why it could travel:** the mechanic is physical and instantly
  understood. Nobody has to explain rules to enjoy seeing their own
  handprint appear.
- **Who to pitch:** party-game publishers (Big Potato, What Do You Meme,
  Exploding Kittens, Goliath), Moose Games.
- **Biggest doubt:** cost. Heat-sensitive sheet costs more than printed
  card, so the deck has to be small.
- **Bench test:** buy a sheet of touch-sensitive liquid-crystal film,
  cut six cards, and play it with four people.

### Dark Tent

- **What it is:** a blackout pop-up tent with a lens in one wall and a
  white screen opposite. Inside, the garden is projected live, in
  colour, upside down.
- **Why it could travel:** it's a real "how is that happening" moment
  for a child, and for the adult.
- **Who to pitch:** science-toy companies (National Geographic kits,
  Thames & Kosmos, Fat Brain Toys, which takes submissions with a
  working prototype), Australian Geographic.
- **Biggest doubt:** nothing protects it, and the image is dim unless
  the tent is properly dark.
- **Bench test:** a large cardboard box, a magnifying glass taped over a
  hole, and a sheet of white paper.

### Night Visitors

- **What it is:** a fold-out tunnel with a food-safe ink pad and paper.
  Bait it, leave it overnight, and read the footprints in the morning
  against a card of local animals.
- **Why it could travel:** it turns a backyard into a mystery, and the
  paper refills are a repeat sale. In Australia the visitors are
  possums, lizards and bandicoots.
- **Who to pitch:** Australian Geographic, National Geographic kits,
  Melissa & Doug, KiwiCo.
- **Biggest doubt:** the idea exists for conservation use, so what's
  being licensed is a children's product design, not an invention.

## How ideas get into this lane

1. Start from a physical effect that surprises people the first time:
   heat, cold, water, light, shadow, balance, sound.
2. Ask what it could be the hook of. The hook must fit in one sentence.
3. Search for it. Novelty and toy ideas are often already made.
4. Build the crudest version and watch someone use it without help.
   Their face is the test.
