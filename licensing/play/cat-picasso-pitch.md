## What isn't ready

- **No prototype and no footage.** The drafts say so: "It's at design
  stage. I'm building a prototype." That sentence must be true when you
  send, so start the cat test first or the same day.
- **The time-lapse is the real pitch.** Toy and novelty companies ask
  for a "wow" reaction and good video. A drawing gets a polite reply. A
  clip of a cat making a painting gets forwarded.
- **The name hasn't been cleared.** One web search found no cat product
  called Cat Picasso. That isn't a trade mark search. Check IP
  Australia's and the US and UK registers before anyone prints it.
- **Nothing protects it yet.** Sending it tells the company the idea.
  That is normal in this industry, and there's no patent to lose here.
  If you want a registered design, Australia allows 12 months after
  first showing it (my understanding; confirm with IP Australia).

## For the forms (Kikkerland, Kazoo)

**Product name:** Cat Picasso

**One line:** A scratching-post wrap that your cat turns into a painting.

**What it is (about 80 words):**

> A removable wrap for a cat scratching post, made of brightly coloured
> card layers under a dark outer layer. Each scratch tears through to a
> colour, so over a few weeks the cat makes an abstract painting. The
> wrap then comes off, unrolls flat to 30 x 40 cm and goes in a frame.
> A fresh wrap goes on the post. Sold as a starter kit with a frame, as
> refill packs in different colour sets, and as a wrap-only pack for
> posts people already own.

**Why it will sell:**

> Every one is unique and made by the buyer's own cat. Every finished
> piece sells a refill. It is printed card and two straps.

**Stage:** Design concept. Prototype in progress.

**Protection:** None filed.
