# Cold Case

**Hook:** "Someone erased the evidence. Your freezer brings it back."

A one-evening mystery for 1 to 4 players, in the style of the Exit and
Unlock games. The case file has been tampered with. Players recover the
evidence using things every home has: a freezer, warm hands, tap water,
a pencil and a window.

## Why it could work

- **The pun is the pitch.** A cold case that you solve by putting it in
  the freezer explains itself in five seconds.
- **The category is proven.** The Exit series is reported to have sold
  over 25 million copies
  ([Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/magazine/exit-out-of-the-escape-room/)).
- **It's a series.** Each box is one case, played once, so a publisher
  can sell the next one.
- **I found no published game built on these reveals.** The individual
  tricks are known: a Royal Society of Chemistry classroom activity uses
  heat, water and erasable ink
  ([RSC](https://edu.rsc.org/ideas/escape-the-classroom-and-reveal-the-properties-of-materials/4011328.article)),
  and hobbyists note that erasable pen ink vanishes with heat and
  returns in a freezer
  ([MetaFilter](https://ask.metafilter.com/354948/What-goes-in-a-mystery-escape-room-puzzle-box)).
  One search found no boxed game that uses them. That is one search, not
  a thorough check.

## The five reveals

| Reveal | What the player does | Prototype at home | In a printed game |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Cold | Puts a page in the freezer for ten minutes; erased lines return | Write with an erasable gel pen, wave a hairdryer over it until it vanishes | Cold-activated ink |
| Warm | Holds a photo between their palms; a dark patch clears | A heat-sensitive sticker over part of the photo | Heat-sensitive ink |
| Wet | Brushes water over a "stained" receipt; hidden writing appears | Write in white wax crayon; brush with weak coffee | Water-reveal ink |
| Rubbed | Shades a blank notepad page with pencil; the dents from the page above show | Press hard with a dry ballpoint on a sheet laid on top | Blind embossing |
| Lit | Holds two pages together against a window; marks line up | Print on thin paper | Two printed sheets |

## Case one: The Dunmore Ice Works

**The story.** November 1987. The night before an audit, the ledger of
the Dunmore Ice Works disappears. The detective on the case retires a
week later and the file is closed. Decades on, the file turns up, and
someone has been through it with a hot iron.

**Suspects.** Ines Mar, the bookkeeper. Tom Welk, the night guard. Ray
Dunmore, the owner's son.

**What players must find.** Who took the ledger, where it is, and the
three-digit code that opens the sealed solution envelope.

| Step | Component | Reveal | What they see first | What the reveal adds | What it tells them |
|------|-----------|--------|---------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Guard's statement | Cold | "I saw nobody on site after nine." | "...nobody except Mr Ray, who wanted the key to cold room 4. That was 9.40." | Ray was there; the guard was told to leave it out. First digit pair: 9.40 |
| 2 | Locksmith's receipt, coffee-stained | Wet | A receipt for "1 key cut" | "Duplicate, cold room 4. Paid cash, R.D." | Ray had his own key made. Room: 4 |
| 3 | Bookkeeper's notepad, top sheet blank | Rubbed | Nothing | "Move it before the audit. Under the third rack." | Ines knew, and where. Rack: 3 |
| 4 | Staff photo | Warm | A group by a wall, one corner dark | A wall calendar with the audit ringed, and Ray holding a ledger-sized parcel | Confirms the night and the person |
| 5 | Floor plan and delivery sheet | Lit | Two unrelated pages | An X over the third rack of cold room 4 | Confirms the place |

**Solution.** Ray Dunmore moved the ledger to cold room 4 and hid it
under the third rack, with Ines Mar's help, the night before the audit.
Envelope code: 4 – 3 – 9 (room, rack, hour).

**The twist for the last page.** The ledger has been in the cold for
decades. So has the last page of the game: the final confession is on a
card that only reads when frozen.

This is a first sketch. The clue chain has not been played by anyone,
and step 1 gives away a lot early. It needs a red herring for each of
the other two suspects.

## Build the prototype (about A$20 to A$30, my estimate)

**Buy:** a pack of erasable gel pens, a white wax crayon, a soft pencil,
a few heat-sensitive stickers, thin printer paper.

**Do:**

1. Write out the five components by hand from the table above.
2. Prepare each reveal as in the "prototype at home" column.
3. Test every reveal yourself. Time the freezer step.
4. Hand the file to two people who know nothing about it. Say only the
   hook. Don't help. Note where they get stuck and when they smile.
5. Film the freezer moment. That clip is the pitch.

**It fails if** the frozen writing is too faint to read, the freezer
wait kills the pace, or players never think to use the freezer without
being told. If so, start the box with the freezer step and let the rest
play while it chills.

## Risks

- **Nothing protects the idea.** Games are licensed on trust, speed and
  the quality of the design. The title and the first case are the asset.
- **Special inks raise the cost** compared with plain printed cards.
- **One search is not proof** that no such game exists. Check the Exit,
  Unlock and Hunt A Killer back catalogues before pitching.
