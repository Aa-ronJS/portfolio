Privacy

Last updated: 22 September 2026. Plain English on purpose. If anything is unclear, a contact email will be added here.

Who this covers

Chasem (ABN ) is an Australian small business. This page covers three separate things, and the first is the one that matters most:

  • The phone app, the free web app you open in your browser and add to your home screen.
  • This website, including the photo demo and any email you give us.
  • The optional laptop pack, files that run in Claude Code on your own computer.

The phone app

What is stored on the phone

The app keeps a full working copy in your browser's storage on your phone, which is why it keeps working with no signal. That includes:

  • Your business details: trading name, your name, ABN, licence and insurance numbers, phone, email, address, logo, and your bank account details as printed on invoices.
  • Your prices, labour and paint settings, quote wording and terms.
  • Your clients: names, phone numbers, email addresses, site and billing addresses, and client type.
  • Jobs, rooms and measurements, photos you attach to a job, quotes (including earlier versions), invoices, payments, credit notes and variations.
  • A log of the messages the app has sent or scheduled: when, to whom, which channel, the first part of the text, and whether it worked.
  • If you set them up: your Stripe restricted key, your Twilio and Resend keys, the relay address and relay token, and a hashed copy of your app PIN. Keys are stored as you typed them; the PIN is stored only as a hash.

The measuring feature processes photos on the phone. Nothing is uploaded to measure a wall.

What we hold on our server, and why

If your sending runs through our system, your work is also kept on our server. It has to be. Your customer taps a link in a text from you and accepts the quote, or picks a start day: that has to land somewhere you and they both agree on, and a phone in your pocket cannot be that place. So we hold:

  • Your jobs, as the app holds them: the client's name, phone, email and address, the rooms and measurements, what you quoted and the lines it is made of, invoices, payments and their status. Not the photos — a photo you attach to a job stays on the phone, and only the fact that it is there travels.
  • Your settings: business details including your bank details as printed on invoices, your prices, your quote wording, your follow-up timing. This is what lets a new or wiped phone come back with your prices on it instead of blank.
  • The days you are not available — worked out on your phone from your own jobs, your days off and your state's public holidays — so the booking page only ever offers a customer a day you are free.
  • What your customers do: accepting a quote, the day they book, and the text of a reply they send to one of our numbers, so it can be passed to you.

What never leaves the phone: your Stripe, Twilio and Resend keys, your sending token, and the hash of your app PIN. Credentials stay on the handset you typed them into.

We can see what is on the server, because somebody has to be able to fix it. We look at a job only to sort out a problem you have told us about. We do not read your work for any other reason, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to train anything.

If you never set up sending, the app never syncs: it stays entirely on your phone, exactly as it did before.

Back-ups still matter. Clearing your browser's site data wipes the phone's copy. If your sending runs through us the server copy can bring your prices and your jobs back on a new phone, but your keys and your PIN are not in it and will need typing again. If you do not use our sending, a lost phone with no back-up means lost data. Save a back-up after every invoice and keep it in your own Drive or iCloud.

If you claim under our guarantee we may ask for screenshots from the app; cover client names before sending, and we delete the screenshots once the claim is settled.

Cross-border. Vercel, Twilio, Resend, Stripe and Google are United States companies and may store data outside Australia. Using the sending, card payment or Google Calendar features means the client information listed above is disclosed to them (Australian Privacy Principle 8); connecting your calendar discloses nothing about your clients to Google, but does let us hold a permission to your Google account. If you would rather it did not leave Australia, do not set those features up: the app still works, and you send from your own phone.

Your keys

Your Stripe, Twilio and Resend keys stay on the phone. They leave it only in two cases: with each relay request, if you pasted them into the app and your relay allows keys from the phone; or never, if you set them as environment variables on your own relay instead, in which case the phone holds only the relay address and token. Use a restricted Stripe key and, where the provider offers it, an API key rather than an account's master token. If a phone is lost, revoke the keys in the Stripe, Twilio and Resend dashboards and change the relay token.

Your clients' information

You are the one collecting your clients' names, numbers and addresses, so their privacy is your responsibility, not ours. Most sole traders are below the Privacy Act's $3 million turnover threshold, but telling clients where their details go is still the right thing to do. A line like "We keep your details on our phone and use a texting and email service to send quotes and reminders" on your quote terms covers it.

What we can see from the app

No analytics, and no usage reporting: we do not track how you use the app, which screens you open or how long you spend. With sending switched on the app does talk to our server — it syncs your work, as set out above — but only that. When you open it, your browser fetches the app files from chasem.app, and the host sees ordinary web-server logs such as your IP address and the time.

This website

The photo demo. When you use "point your phone at a wall" on this site, the photo and any notes you type are sent once, over an encrypted connection, to Anthropic's API to produce a sample quote. We do not save the photo, the notes or the result. Anthropic handles that request under its own privacy policy and API terms. Do not upload photos of people or documents; the demo is for rooms and walls. This is different from the app, which measures on the phone and uploads nothing.

Email address. The app asks for your email to open it, and we use it to send you your set-up link and anything about your account. We do not sell or rent email addresses.

Payments. Subscriptions are processed by Stripe, which collects your card details under its own privacy policy and is where your card is kept. We receive your name, email, phone and billing address, and what you are paying for. We never see or store a card number.

Advertising and analytics. This site may use the Meta pixel to measure whether our ads work. It sets cookies and shares visit data with Meta under their policies. You can limit this through your browser settings or your ad preferences on Meta. The app itself carries no pixel and no analytics.

The laptop pack

The pack is a set of files that runs inside a folder on your own computer. Your quotes, invoices, client details and price list stay on your machine. When you use Claude Code with it, the content you ask it to work on is processed by Anthropic under the terms of your own Claude subscription, which you hold directly with Anthropic. We never see your business data. The pack drafts messages; it does not send them.

Your rights

You can ask what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, and we handle it in line with the Australian Privacy Principles. If your sending runs through us, what we hold is set out under “What we hold on our server” above — it is your work, not a mailing-list entry, so ask and we will send you the lot or delete the lot. Deleting takes your jobs, settings, availability, bookings and replies with it, and we will tell you when it is done. The copy on your phone is yours either way: wipe it from the app's Set-up screen or your browser settings.

Your customers' details are yours to explain. You are the one who collects their names, numbers and addresses; we hold them because you asked us to send and chase on your behalf. A line on your quote terms like “we keep your details in our quoting system and use a texting and email service to send quotes and reminders” covers it.
