# Claude writing tells

Everything a detector (human or machine) might use to decide a piece of
text was written by Claude. Compiled September 2026.

**How much to trust each item.** Each tell is tagged with where the evidence comes from:

- **[M]** comes from a published corpus study with measured rates (mostly the
  Graphite "AI Tells" study: 10,000 pre-2023 human articles against 10,000
  articles each from nine models, including Claude Opus 4, 4.6 and 5).
- **[W]** comes from Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing* catalogue. It covers all
  LLMs, not only Claude.
- **[O]** is widely reported or observed but not measured. Treat it as a
  lead, not proof.

The multipliers are rates against human writing. "∞" means the phrase never
appeared in the human corpus.

---

## 1. Literal footprints (near-certain)

These don't come from style. They are output left behind by tools.

- `Co-Authored-By: Claude …` and `Claude-Session:` trailers in git commits;
  `🤖 Generated with [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code)` in PR
  bodies; `_Generated by Claude Code_` footers on GitHub comments. **[O]**
- Leaked XML-style tags: `<thinking>`, `<answer>`, `<antArtifact …>`,
  `<document>`, `<example>`, `<function_calls>`, `<result>`. These show up
  when output is copied raw from an API or a tool. **[O]**
- Transcript scaffolding such as `Human:` / `Assistant:` turn labels, which is
  Anthropic's own conversation format. **[O]**
- Self-reference: "As Claude…", "I'm Claude, an AI assistant made by
  Anthropic", "I don't have the ability to browse…", knowledge-cutoff
  disclaimers ("As of my last update…"). **[W][O]**
- Messages written *to the user* that end up in the final text: "Here's a
  revised version:", "I've made the following changes:", "Let me know if
  you'd like me to adjust the tone." **[W]**
- Placeholder templates left unfilled, like `[Your Name]`, `[Company]`,
  `[insert example]`. **[W]**
- Markdown in places that don't render it: `**bold**`, `##` headings and
  backtick code in emails, Word docs or wikitext. **[W]**
- Unlike ChatGPT (`oaicite`, `contentReference`, `turn0search0`) and Gemini
  (`[cite: 1]`), **Claude has no widely known citation-marker artifact.**
  Wikipedia lists none. **[W]**

## 2. Words Claude over-uses

### Measured (Claude Opus 5 vs human rate) [M]
| Word / phrase | Rate |
|---|---|
| deliberate | 26× |
| steady | 6× |
| measured | 4.2× |
| enormously | 45× (vs GPT-6 Astra) |
| every single | 112× (vs GPT-6 Astra) |
| single most | ∞ (vs Astra) |
| arguably the most | ∞ (vs Astra) |

Claude pushes things toward superlatives ("the single most important…"),
while GPT hedges ("may provide", "not necessarily").

### Older Claude hype words (fading) [M]
groundbreaking, paramount, unprecedented, revolutionary, invaluable,
profound, exceptional. Across Claude versions these fell, and the
well-known Claude tells dropped by about 29% overall.

### Generic LLM vocabulary that Claude shares [W]
delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, intricate, interplay,
meticulous, landscape, realm, underscore, showcase, highlight, foster,
enhance, garner, bolster, boast, vibrant, enduring, nuanced, robust,
seamless, leverage, navigate, multifaceted, holistic, comprehensive.
"Delve" appears about 48× more in AI text. This list says "an LLM wrote
this", not "Claude wrote this".

### Claude-flavoured vocabulary [O]
genuinely, honestly, actually (as emphasis), straightforward, worth noting,
worth flagging, load-bearing, the heavy lifting, quietly (as in "quietly
does X"), sits (as in "the file sits in…"), lands ("the point lands"),
surface (as a verb), reach for, earns its place, the real issue / the real
question, the tricky part, tension ("there's a tension between…"),
thoughtful, careful, crisp.

## 3. Phrases and sentence frames

### Definition by contrast [M][W]
- "rather than merely" (160×), "rather than simply" (43×)
- "less like a ___ and more like a ___" (105×, and rising with each
  Claude version)
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "not just X, but Y" / "no X, no Y, just Z"

### Flagging importance [M]
- "matters because" (132×), "matters more than" (93×)
- "What matters here is…", "The key insight is…", "Here's the thing:" **[O]**

### Promising gains without costs [M]
- "without losing" (6.1×), "without sacrificing" (4.3×), "without
  compromising" (2.6×)

### Transitions [M]
- "what comes next" (28×), "looking ahead, the…" (33×)

### Chat openers and closers [O]
The strongest tells in Claude's older versions. They have faded but not disappeared.
- Openers: "Certainly!", "Great question!", "I'd be happy to help",
  "Absolutely!", "You're absolutely right!", "You raise a great point",
  "That's a fair point", "Good catch", "Perfect!" (common in Claude Code).
- Claude 3.x habits: "I aim to be direct", "I should note that…",
  "I want to be clear/honest/transparent", "I notice that…".
- Closers: "Would you like me to…?", "Want me to…?", "Let me know if
  you'd like…", "I hope this helps!", "Happy to adjust…".
- Agent narration: "Now let me…", "Let me check…", "I've successfully…",
  "✅ All tests pass", "This is now production-ready".

### Reveal structures [O]
- A colon setup followed by a punchline: "Here's what's actually happening:
  …", "The short answer: …", "The fix is simple: …".
- "Short answer: X. Longer answer: …"
- "The honest answer is…", "To be direct…", "Frankly…".

## 4. Rhythm and structure

- **Rule of three.** Adjectives, benefits and examples come in threes. **[W]**
- **Staccato fragments** after a long sentence: "No setup. No config. Just
  run it." **[O]**
- **Uniform sentence and paragraph lengths.** Low variance is a statistical
  signal (see §7). **[O]**
- **Avoiding "is" and "are".** "serves as", "stands as", "functions as",
  "marks", "represents". **[W]**
- **Shallow closing analysis.** Participle tails such as "…, highlighting
  its importance", "…, ensuring reliability", "…, reflecting broader
  trends". **[W]**
- **Challenges-then-optimism endings.** "Despite these challenges…",
  "Looking ahead…", "The future of X…". **[W][M]**
- **Balanced both-sides framing.** "On one hand… on the other"; hedges
  that line up opposite claims evenly. **[O]**
- **Concrete-sounding round numbers.** "twenty minutes", "a handful", "two or
  three", "six months", "a decade". Their use *rose* across Claude versions,
  which reads as a deliberate move toward sounding specific. **[M]**
- **Mannered prose.** Claude Opus 5 scores about 2.5× human on a
  "mannered prose" index, against 1.2× for GPT-6 Astra, and the score
  *rose* 19% since Opus 4. **[M]**

## 5. Formatting

- Headings on short answers; bold lead-ins on bullets
  ("**Speed:** …"); nested bullets. **[W]**
- A "Key takeaways", "Bottom line" or "Summary" section that repeats the
  body. **[W][O]**
- Tables used where prose would do. **[W]**
- Heavy **bold** for emphasis in the middle of sentences. **[W]**
- Emoji used as bullets or headers (✅ ❌ 🚀 ⚠️), mostly in coding and agent
  output. **[W][O]**
- Horizontal rules (`---`) between sections. **[W]**
- Sentence-case headings. GPT more often uses Title Case. **[O]**
- **Em dashes.** A famous tell, but no longer a reliable one for Claude.
  Claude Opus 5 uses them at about the human rate, while GPT-6 Astra is
  88% below human and Gemini 3.1 Pro uses almost none. A heavy em-dash
  count points to *older* models, or to Claude over Gemini and GPT. **[M]**
- Curly quotes and apostrophes (’ “ ”) in a context where the writer
  normally types straight ones. **[W]**

## 6. What Claude *doesn't* do (tells by absence)

The human corpus far outpaced every AI model on these. **[M]**
- Exclamation marks: humans use them more than 100× as often.
- Parenthetical asides.
- Casual qualifiers: "pretty", "sort of", "kinda".
- First-person anecdotes: "years ago", "my mom", "last year", "my favorite".
- Direct, chatty address: "did you know", "we all know", "you can't".
- Real enthusiasm: "love to", "so excited to".
- Typos, inconsistent spelling, run-ons, local slang.
- Mixed dialects. An LLM defaults to American English even when the writer
  and topic are, say, Indian or Australian. **[W]**

## 7. Statistical signals (what automated detectors measure)

- **Perplexity.** How predictable each token is to a reference model. LLM
  text sits low.
- **Burstiness.** Variation in perplexity and sentence length across a
  document. Human text is spiky; LLM text is even.
- **Token log-probability curvature** (DetectGPT and Binoculars style).
  LLM text sits near a local maximum of likelihood, so small rewrites make
  it *less* likely.
- **Word-distribution fingerprints.** Classifiers trained on outputs from
  ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini and DeepSeek identify the source model with
  97.1% accuracy, against 20% by chance. They stay accurate when length and
  format are controlled, and are non-trivial from only the first few tokens.
  The signal lives mainly in word choice (Sun et al., *Idiosyncrasies in
  Large Language Models*, 2025).
- **Family distinctiveness.** 65% of measured tells belong to a single model
  family, which is why a detector can say "Claude" and not just "AI". **[M]**
- **Watermarking.** Anthropic has not publicly announced a text watermark
  for Claude. Detection relies on the stylistic and statistical signals
  above, not on a hidden signature.

## 8. Caveats

- **Tells move with each version.** From each family's earliest to its latest
  model, the frequency of well-known tells fell 41–86%, and only about 45% of
  tells carry over even between sibling models. A list like this goes stale
  within months. **[M]**
- **False positives are common.** Detectors have non-trivial error rates. In
  a 2025 study, people spotted AI text at about chance (around 57% on AI
  text, 64% on human text), though heavy LLM users reached about 90%. **[W]**
- **Humans are converging on LLM style.** Use of "is" and "are" in academic
  writing dropped by 10% or more after 2023, and people now pick up
  "delve" from models. **[W]**
- **One tell proves nothing.** Detectors weigh clusters. Five of the frames
  above in one paragraph, with even rhythm and no asides, is strong evidence.
  A single em dash is not.
