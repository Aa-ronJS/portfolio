# Footage rules for Corkboard Theories

Checked 18 Nov 2026. **Re-check a publisher's page before a game's
first video**: these change.

## Games

Most big publishers allow gameplay videos and many allow monetising
them, on conditions. Known so far:

| Publisher | Monetised gameplay on YouTube | Conditions worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| Nintendo | Yes, via the YouTube Partner Program (Game Content Guidelines, last updated 2 Sep 2024) | Individuals only; no selling videos; Nintendo can object to or revoke use |
| Microsoft / Xbox Game Studios | Allowed since 2015 under its Game Content Usage Rules; current version not re-checked | 2015 rules excluded game music and set conditions on titles; read the current page |
| Others | Check each: most publish a "video policy" or "content creator" page | Music in games is often licensed separately and Content-ID-claimed |

Footage source: the robot doesn't download other people's gameplay
(that's both their capture and YouTube's reused-content rule). It uses
the channel's own **footage library**, `channels/corkboard/footage/<game>/`:
clips Aaron records, or official press-kit and trailer-free media the
publisher offers creators. Clip names say what they show
(`hollow-knight_abyss-shriek.mp4`) so the robot can match them to the
script's cues. Mute game audio unless the publisher allows its music.

## Films and TV

No studio publishes a blanket licence. Short clips used as evidence in
commentary lean towards fair use, but YouTube's automatic matching
doesn't judge fair use: it claims, and usually takes the ad money. A
claim is not a strike; a disputed claim can become one. So: few, short
clips, only where the moment is the evidence, and an evidence-board
video otherwise.
