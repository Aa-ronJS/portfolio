# Treeline Reports: channel rules

Read word for word by the scriptwriter every time.

## The channel

Cryptids and the people who swear they saw them: Bigfoot, the Yowie,
the Mothman, lake monsters, thylacine sightings. Each video builds the
case for, then the case against, and lets the viewer sit in the
uncertainty. 15-18 minutes.

## Voice

Hushed campfire documentary: curious, open-minded, never gullible.
Takes witnesses seriously as people even when the evidence is thin.
Never says "100% real", "proof" or "the government is hiding".

## Every script

- Open inside a specific sighting: date, place, what the witness says
  they saw, in their recorded words where they exist.
- The case for: the best sightings, with where they were reported.
- The case against: misidentifications, hoaxes, what was debunked, and by whom.
- Verdict honestly: what we know, what we don't. Never claim a cryptid
  is real; never sneer at believers.
- Original sighting reports and newspaper accounts cited.
- About 2,500 words, written to be heard.
- B-roll every 8 seconds: landscape and atmosphere ("pine forest fog",
  "lake at dawn", "1970s newspaper clipping", "map: Point Pleasant").
- Never ask for, or describe, fake photographic "evidence".

## Packaging

- Title in the genre's own style (examples in IDEAS.md), under 70
  characters. Curious, not dishonest: the video must deliver what the
  title promises.
- Three genuinely different title/thumbnail ideas; thumbnail text 1-4
  words that add to the title.

## Never

- Fabricated sightings presented as real reports.
- Money, AI/tech hype, kids' content, politics, religion, medical, legal or
  financial advice.
- Real living private people; real usernames; real crimes presented as
  entertainment.
- Graphic gore or bodies, in words or pictures; nothing shocking in the
  first 7 seconds or the thumbnail (that's what loses ads).
- Repeating a topic already made (the list is supplied).
