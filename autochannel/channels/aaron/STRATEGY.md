# Channel framework

The operating plan for the channel: what it is, why, how each video is
made, how it grows, how we judge it, and what we change when the numbers
say so. `RULES.md` is the part the scriptwriter reads every time; this
file is the thinking behind it. Written 18 Nov 2026 from Aaron's answers
plus research (sources at the bottom).

---

## 1. The decisions

| | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | **An experiment.** Prove a near-free, mostly automated channel can earn real watch time. Money is a bonus | Aaron's call |
| Niche | **Why the ordinary world works like this**: infrastructure, logistics, everyday engineering | Best match for footage that exists free; top-paying category; dry voice is a known winning fit (Half as Interesting) |
| Format | **Long-form, 8–12 min, 16:9**, plus 2–3 Shorts cut from each | Long-form pays ~10–50× Shorts and builds subscribers ~5× faster per view |
| Cadence | **2 a week (Tue, Fri)**; go to 3 only if quality holds | Volume buys chances at a breakout; weaker videos don't |
| Identity | **Fully Aaron.** Channel: *Aaron Steele*. Show line: *Why It's Like That* | His name becomes the asset; a real person is the strongest answer to the "inauthentic" rule |
| Voice | Dry, wry, understated. A mate explaining something surprising at the pub | Aaron's call; the differentiator in a niche full of earnest narrators |
| Audience | Global English. Examples from everywhere, Australian ones welcome | Biggest pool; the accent is a feature, not a limit |
| Shelf life | Evergreen only | Videos earn for years; most views arrive a month or more after upload |
| Never | Money/hustle, AI/tech hype, kids, true crime, medical, legal, politics, mocking real people | Aaron's lines plus policy risk |
| Aaron's time | **5 minutes a day** | Approve or bin scripts from his phone |
| Budget | **Up to US$50/month**, expected ~$10 | Room for researched scripts and thumbnails |
| Judged at | **3 months** (mid-Feb 2027), on watch time and retention trends | Too early to judge on subscribers |

Runners-up if the niche fails at review: **odd geography and borders**
(same voice, map-heavy) and **animal behaviour told dryly** (good footage
for common animals). Etymology fits the voice but there's no footage for
it. Sleep documentaries are a flooded AI-slop target.

## 2. Strategy

### What we're actually testing
Can one person spending 5 minutes a day run a channel that people
**choose to keep watching**? Watch time and retention answer that.
Subscribers and money follow from them, and are too slow and noisy to
judge a 3-month experiment on.

### Why this can win
- The big channels in the niche post monthly (Wendover) or fortnightly
  (Practical Engineering). There's room for twice-weekly.
- Endless topics: every object, road, pipe and queue has a "why".
- Stock footage of roads, ports, power lines, trains and supermarkets is
  plentiful and free, so the visuals can match the words.
- A wry Australian voice in a genre of earnest American narrators is easy
  to recognise.

### What could kill it
1. **YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule**, which removes earning
   for whole channels that look template-made. Defence: section 6.
2. **Wrong facts in Aaron's own voice.** Defence: researched scripts
   with sources, plus Aaron's daily veto.
3. **Generic footage that doesn't match the words**, the most common way
   faceless channels die. Defence: write scripts around footage that
   exists; maps and diagrams for the rest.
4. **Slow intros.** Defence: the hook rules in section 3.

### Money, honestly
From 1 Feb 2027, joining the Partner Program for ad revenue takes **8,000
watch hours in a year** (or 20M Shorts views in 90 days), plus the
subscriber minimum. Fan-funding (memberships, Super Thanks) stays at 500
subscribers + 3,000 hours. A new channel won't reach 4,000 hours before
the change, so plan against the new bar: ad revenue is a **year-one**
goal at best. Education videos pay a median ~US$10 per 1,000 views once
in (AIR Media-Tech, 300 channels, 2026).

## 3. Content

### Topic families (rotate; never more than 2 in a row from one)
1. **Hidden systems**: water, sewage, power grids, gas, phone lines.
   *Where your toilet water actually goes.*
2. **Logistics**: containers, pallets, supermarkets, post, cold chains.
   *Why shipping a container across the world costs less than trucking it 300 km.*
3. **Why is it shaped like that**: roads, bridges, plugs, kerbs, cans.
   *Why every bridge has those little gaps.*
4. **Transport quirks**: airports, trains, roundabouts, lanes, signs.
   *Why airports are always so far away.*
5. **The quiet genius of…**: one ordinary object, start to finish.
   *The pallet: the most important object you've never thought about.*

Five families let the review in month 2 say *which kind* of topic works,
not just which video did.

### Every video has an argument
Not "facts about bridges" but a claim worth arguing: *bridges are
designed to move, and the gaps are the bit that's working hardest.* One
argument per video. If it needs two, it's two videos.

### Five structures, rotated (this is policy protection as much as craft)
YouTube targets "the same skeleton with the nouns swapped". The writer
picks the structure that suits the topic and never uses one three times
in a row:
1. **Mystery → answer.** Pose the odd thing, eliminate wrong answers, reveal.
2. **Follow the thing.** Trace one object or drop of water start to end.
3. **Myth vs reality.** What everyone thinks, why it's wrong, what's true.
4. **The hidden problem.** The constraints nobody sees, stacked until the design makes sense.
5. **Two places, two answers.** Why two cities solved the same problem differently.

### Script rules
- **Length:** 1,300–1,700 words (8–12 min at ~150 words a minute). The
  cloned voice must be timed and slowed to that pace (build list).
- **0:00–0:30:** prove the title with one concrete example, say why it
  matters, preview the payoff. No greeting, no name, no "in this video".
- **One big open question** set up early and paid off late. The best
  moment goes in the last third, not the first.
- **A re-hook every 60–90 seconds:** a turn, a number, a "which is
  where it gets strange".
- **Chapters:** 4–6, the first at 0:00, each at least 10 seconds long.
- **Ending:** pay off every question, one line pointing at a related
  video, stop. No outro speech, no "smash that like button".
- **Written for the ear:** numbers as said ("about forty thousand"),
  short sentences, contractions, no symbols or abbreviations.
- **Sourced:** every factual claim has a source. Sources are listed in
  the description; a claim without one is cut.

### Visual rules
- Write to the footage that exists: every 8–12 seconds of script gets a
  b-roll term Pexels can actually match ("container ship aerial", not
  "the MSC Gülsün in 2019").
- Specific places and structures get a **map or simple diagram**, never
  unrelated stock pretending to be them.
- Cut every 4–8 s; never one clip over 12 s.
- No AI-generated realistic people or events (that *would* need the AI
  label, and it looks cheap).

## 4. Packaging (titles, thumbnails)

- **Title:** a question or a surprising claim, under 60 characters,
  plain words, no caps-lock, no "SHOCKING". The thumbnail adds to it,
  never repeats it.
- **Thumbnail:** one bold object, map or contrast, plus **1–4 words**,
  readable on a phone at thumb size. Consistent colour and type so the
  channel is recognisable across a feed.
- **Three of each.** The robot makes 3 title/thumbnail pairs per video.
  YouTube's **Test & Compare** (desktop Studio only, up to 3 variants)
  picks the winner on watch time, which avoids rewarding clickbait.
  Setting it up is the one weekly desktop chore (≈5 min a week, optional).
- **Description:** two lines that restate the promise, chapters, sources,
  a link to aaronsteele.vercel.app, and the credit lines.

## 5. Growth tactics

- **Search first.** New evergreen channels get early views from search
  and suggested videos, not the home page. Titles use the words people
  type ("why are airports so far from cities"); the writer checks
  YouTube autocomplete for each topic (build list).
- **Families feed each other.** End-screen and the closing line point at
  the next video in the same family; playlists per family.
- **Shorts as trailers, not a second channel.** 2–3 per long video, each
  a single standalone claim that ends pointing at the full answer, with
  the Short's *Related video* set to the parent. That link has to be set
  by hand in Studio. Evidence that Shorts lift long-form is mixed, so
  they're judged on whether long-form views follow, and dropped if not.
- **Double down fast.** A video at 3× the channel median gets 2–3
  follow-ups in the same family within two weeks.
- **Comments:** the robot posts one question comment per video; Aaron
  pins it and hearts a few replies in the first 48 hours from his phone
  (part of the 5 minutes). The comments are mostly a source of new topics.
- **Collaborations / cross-posting:** none in the first 3 months. Learn
  the formula first.

## 6. Staying on the right side of YouTube

| Rule | What we do |
|---|---|
| Inauthentic content (templated, mass-produced) | Original sourced argument per video; 5 rotated structures; edits that follow the words; 2–3 a week, not daily; Aaron approves every script |
| AI disclosure | **Aaron's own cloned voice needs no label** (YouTube lists it as a minor edit). Turn the label on for any video with realistic AI-generated people or events (which we don't make) |
| Reused content | Stock clips are cut, dimmed, captioned and carry narration; never long unedited runs |
| Advice personas | No health, legal or money advice, ever |
| Made for kids | No |
| Credits | Pocket TTS (CC BY 4.0) and Pexels in every description |

## 7. Operating rhythm

**Aaron, daily (5 min, phone):** the robot shows the next script as a
title, the hook (first 30 seconds) and a 5-line outline with sources.
Aaron approves it, bins it, or adds a one-line note
("funnier", "wrong, the gaps are for heat not wind") for a rewrite. Then
he pins the question comment on yesterday's video.

**Aaron, weekly (optional, 5 min, desktop):** set up Test & Compare on the
week's uploads and set Related video on the Shorts.

**Robot, continuously:** keeps 4 approved scripts in the queue; renders
and publishes on Tue and Fri; cuts Shorts; posts the weekly report.

**Robot, weekly report (Monday):** each video's click-through rate by
traffic source, share of the video watched, the first-30-seconds drop,
search terms, subscribers per 1,000 views, and which family is winning,
with the decision rules below applied and the suggested next topics.

## 8. Measuring and deciding

### Targets (new channel, realistic)
| Metric | Floor | Good | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate (search/suggested) | 3% | 4–6% | YouTube: half of channels sit at 2–10% |
| Share of video watched (8–12 min) | 25% | 35–45% | Median ~30% for 5–15 min videos |
| Watched past 0:30 | 60% | 75% | |

### Rules
- **Judge videos at day 7 and day 28,** never in the first hours.
- Low click-through, good retention → **packaging problem**: re-test
  title/thumbnail, keep the topic.
- Good click-through, retention under 25% → **the script breaks the
  title's promise**: fix hooks and pacing, not topics.
- A video at 3× median → **2–3 follow-ups** in that family within 2 weeks.
- A family with 3 videos below median on both → **drop it**, add a new family.
- Never compare against the best video; compare against the median.
- Don't change niche before ~10 uploads.

### The 3-month plan
| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0–2 (now → early Dec) | Build list below; record the voice; write and approve 6 scripts; publish 2 privately and watch them critically |
| 1–4 (Dec) | Launch 2/week. Two videos from each of the first four families. Change nothing based on fewer than 7 days of data |
| 5–8 (Jan) | Kill or keep families; double down on any 3× video; try 3/week if quality holds |
| 9–13 (Feb) | Steady state; mid-Feb review |

### The 3-month review (mid-Feb 2027): pass if most of these hold
- ~24–26 long videos published, none skipped for quality
- Median share watched ≥ 30%, and trending up
- At least one video at 3× the channel median
- Watch time growing month on month (December → February)
- Aaron still spending ~5 min a day; cost under $50 a month

**Pass:** keep going, push toward 3,000 hours / 500 subs (fan-funding)
then 8,000 hours. **Mixed:** keep the best family, change the format.
**Fail:** switch to a runner-up niche with the same machine.

## 9. Build list (the technical bit, for when Aaron's home)

What exists today makes Shorts with captions. To run this plan it needs:
1. **Long-form mode**: 16:9, chapters, pacing to ~150 words a minute
   (slow the voice and lengthen pauses), cuts every 4–8 s.
2. **Researching writer**: Claude with web search, a sources list per
   script, the five structures, rotation tracking, Aaron's notes fed back.
3. **Phone approval**: script summary as an issue or message;
   approve, bin or note with one tap.
4. **Maps and simple diagrams** for named places and structures.
5. **Thumbnails**: 3 variants per video (a frame or object plus 1–4 words
   in the channel's style).
6. **Shorts cutter**: 2–3 standalone clips per long video, 9:16.
7. **Question comment** posted automatically on upload.
8. **Weekly report** from the YouTube Analytics API, with the rules above.
9. **Schedule**: Tue/Fri publishing, scripts kept 4 ahead.
10. **Channel setup**: name, handle, banner, About page, playlists per family.
