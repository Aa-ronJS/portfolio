# Restless Ground: channel rules

Read word for word by the scriptwriter every time. Portfolio-wide rules
are in ../PORTFOLIO.md.

## The channel

How the planet's violent systems actually behave: volcanoes, storms, earthquakes, the deep sea. Science first, drama from the science itself.

## Voice

Measured with an undercurrent of urgency, like a scientist who's seen it up close. Precise numbers, plain words. Would never say "apocalyptic", "doomsday" or "terrifying".

## Channel-specific

- Explain the mechanism, not the body count. No dwelling on deaths; never use a recent disaster as a hook.
- No predictions that something will erupt or strike soon.
- Prefer real agency footage and satellite loops over generic stock.

## Every script

- One argument, stated as a claim worth defending, not a topic.
- Pick the structure that suits the topic (mystery then answer; follow
  the thing; myth vs reality; the hidden problem; two cases compared);
  never the same as either of the previous two scripts.
- First 30 seconds prove the title with one concrete example and hint at
  the payoff. No greeting, no channel name, no "in this video".
- One big question set up early, answered late; best moment in the last third.
- Re-hook every 60-90 seconds. 4-6 chapters with short plain titles.
- Ending: answer everything, one line pointing at a related topic, stop.
- 1,300-1,700 words, written to be heard: numbers as spoken, no symbols.
- Every factual claim has a source; cut anything unsourced. Round honestly.
- B-roll: one plain search term per 8-12 seconds that the footage
  sources above can actually show; ask for a map, diagram or still
  where they can't.

## Packaging

- Title: a question or surprising claim under 60 characters, in words
  people search for. No caps-lock, no clickbait.
- Three genuinely different title/thumbnail ideas; thumbnail text 1-4
  words that add to the title.

## Never

- Money, AI or tech hype, kids' content, true crime, medical, legal or
  financial advice, politics, religion, mocking real living people.
- Repeating a topic already made (the list is supplied).
