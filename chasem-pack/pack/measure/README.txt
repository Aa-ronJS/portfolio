MEASURE A WALL FROM A PHOTO

Nothing to print. Nothing to buy.

1. Take one photo of the whole wall, corner to corner, lights on.
   Stand back. Main camera, not the wide-angle lens.
2. Open measure.html (double-click it). Choose the photo. Tap the four
   corners of the wall: top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left.
   The camera's own geometry works out the wall's shape from that.
3. Set the scale once: type the ceiling height (measure it with a tape
   once per room), or tap the top and bottom of a door (standard
   2.04 m), or the two edges of a power point (116 mm), or anything
   you put a tape on.
4. Tap each door and window, two corners each. Save the wall. Repeat
   for the other walls and rooms.
5. Save the measurements file into the job folder in quotes/incoming,
   next to the photos. The quote uses those numbers instead of
   estimating.

If a wall corner is out of shot, press "Can't see all corners" and tap
the four corners of a door (or a power point, A4 sheet or bank card)
instead; then tap the wall's corners where they would be.

Expected accuracy: about 1 to 2 percent with the ceiling height or a
door; 3 percent from a power point. The page tells you its estimate
for each wall. The quote carries that number.

Everything runs inside your browser. No photo leaves your computer.
The marker detector js-aruco2 (MIT licence, see lib/) is only used if
someone happens to have the old printed measure sheet in the shot.
