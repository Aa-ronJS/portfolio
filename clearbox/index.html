What I do

I attack you the way they will.

I don't test your systems from the outside and guess. I assume whoever comes after you already knows exactly how they work.

Then I look for the places your systems still rely on something an attacker now controls. That is usually where the real damage is, and it is rarely the obvious place.

→

Can someone act as a user they aren't?

Logged in as a low-level account, can I reach what only an admin or another customer should see? I test whether your systems check who is asking, or just take their word for it.

→

Can I pull data that isn't mine?

One customer's records, another tenant's files, the whole list. I test whether your systems check who is really asking, or just trust the request.

→

Can I make it do something it shouldn't?

Send the email, move the money, delete the record, call something that was never meant to be called that way. I test what the system can be made to do, because that is where real money is lost.

→

Can I get in through what it trusts?

A file it reads, a page it fetches, a message it processes. I test whether a hostile instruction hidden in ordinary content can quietly hand control to an attacker.

→

How far does one foothold go?

If a single thing is compromised, what else falls with it? I test how much of your business a single weak point actually gives away.
