1 · The buyer's path

From a Google ad to a certificate without talking to anyone

A tradie searches "vehicle first aid kit", clicks, lands on the page below, sizes the order, pays by card, names the kit locations, and holds a compliance certificate. Four screens, two minutes, no phone call. This is the whole sales motion now that you are not selling in person.

2 · The day something is used

Someone cuts a hand, scans the lid, and the refill is in the post

This is the loop the incumbents cannot run. A visit-based service finds the gap next quarter; a distributor's kit log needs someone to remember. Here the person who used the gloves reports it in twenty seconds and the parcel goes out the same day.

On the label · any phone, no login

Every item in the kit, with what is left. Tick the gloves and the dressing strips, send. The same page doubles as the six-monthly check ("nothing used, checked and complete") and the loss report.

Scroll inside the phone

Your admin, the same minute
https://yourdomain.com.au/admin

What ships today. The after-use request, the scheduled six-monthly packs that fell due, kits that need attention, AED pads and batteries inside 45 days of expiry, renewals, and calendar obligations. Type the tracking number, press Shipped, the kit is compliant again and the customer's record updates. A daily version of this list is also emailed or posted to a webhook.

The customer's live record, showing the same events
https://yourdomain.com.au/c/WUPNMUURPBW2Y85G

Four states, computed from the rows, never typed in. Compliant, due (a scheduled refill inside 14 days or an item expiring inside 45), attention (something short or a request open), overdue. The stamp at the top is the worst state across all kits, so the record is honest even when that is inconvenient. The "Refer a business" button gives them their own link and a free refill pack when it converts.

3 · The compliance calendar

The A$14 tick box that is worth more than the kits

Fire equipment every six months, test and tag, AED service, the emergency plan review, inductions, chemicals, licences. Most small businesses track none of it. Attached to a kit order it is a tick box; on its own it is a hard sale. The pricing model puts the calendar and its siblings at A$14 to 22 of extra revenue per account per month at the base take-rates, which is the single largest profit lever in the plan.

https://yourdomain.com.au/c/9Y429GUK7RAZPSBV

A childcare centre with two kits and the calendar. Fire extinguishers serviced five months ago by a named provider, test and tag due next month. Each obligation has a cycle, a state, a line on the certificate and a line on your due list. "Done" resets the cycle. Calendar Plus (A$29) adds the induction and SWMS register and chemical register; those are specified but not built.

4 · Partners and referrals

WHS consultants and bookkeepers sign themselves up

Nobody recruits partners by phone in this plan. A consultant lands on the signup page, gets three links on the spot, and earns 15 percent of the first year's plan for every client who buys through them. By year three the model has partners and referrals winning 38 percent of new accounts, which is what pulls blended acquisition cost down from A$105 to A$62.

Public · self-signup
https://yourdomain.com.au/partners

Submit, and the partner exists. They get a buy link, a self-check link and their portal address immediately. No approval queue.

The partner's portal · on their phone

Two clients, A$90 owed. The partner sees each client's compliance state and plan status, never their contact details, and can point a client at the self-check from here. Payouts are recorded automatically at signup and marked paid from your admin.

5 · The self-check

The 90-second check that turns a cold click into a lead

Only about 2 percent of ad clicks buy on the first visit. The check catches another 6 percent as leads with a phone number and a score, and an automated sequence converts about one in eight of those. That second path is a third of the accounts the paid channel wins in the model, so it matters as much as the buy page.

Eight questions, then a "4 of 8" result naming the gaps and what fixes each. A partner's link attributes the lead to them. The page never claims a passing score means compliance.

7 · The financials

What one account earns, and what a thousand of them earn

Three models produce these numbers. The unit-economics model describes one account. The channel model runs 36 months of accounts won by paid search, partners, referrals and SEO, with packing hours priced once you exceed what you can do yourself. The retention model builds churn from its causes rather than assuming it. All three are in the repository and rerun in under a second.

What the numbers rest on

The load-bearing assumption is the conversion rate of the buy page. Base case: a A$3 click, 2 percent buy directly, 6 percent take the check and one in eight of those buy after the email sequence, so about 2.7 percent blended and A$110 per account. If it is 1.5 percent the low case is what you get: A$2,253 of profit in year one on twelve hours a week. That is the honest cost of removing the in-person close.
The upside is not in the kits. Lifting revenue per account by A$14 a month through the calendar and its siblings is worth about A$160,000 of year-three profit, more than every operating efficiency combined. It is also the one thing a visit-based restocker or a distributor's kit log cannot copy without engineers.

8 · What stands between this and live

About a week of paperwork, then the three-week test

The software is the part that is done. The list below is everything that is not, in the order it has to happen. None of it needs you to sell anything.

  • Name Run the IP Australia and ASIC searches on a shortlist, register the business name and the .com.au, file TM Headstart in classes 10 and 42. Every name tried so far collided, which is why the screens say "Kit Register".
  • Entity Pty Ltd, ABN, GST registration, business bank account. One afternoon online.
  • Supply Email Aero Healthcare and one alternative for reseller pricing on the site kit, vehicle kit, refill packs and AED consumables. This is Gate G1 and the only unknown that can change the prices on the buy page.
  • Stripe Open the account, add the live keys and webhook secret to the environment, put the site on a domain. The handshake is already built and tested against a fake Stripe server.
  • Insurance Public and products liability, two quotes.
  • Stock Ten site kits, fifteen vehicle kits, refill packs, printed labels. Under A$2,000.
  • Then The three-week, A$1,500 test from the last note: three landing pages, three ad groups, A$500 each, national. Cost per account chooses whether the shop leads with kit plans, managed AEDs or generated documents. Only then order more than the first box of stock.
9 · On autopilot

One email a week and a handful of taps

Every recurring thing the business does is now a daily job: shipments pushed to a 3PL, purchase orders at reorder points, renewal notices, dunning and closure, activation nudges, the self-check lead sequence, exit surveys and win-backs, partner statements, and a Monday review that emails you the numbers. Inbound email is classified and answered with the sender's own links. Money out and anything irreversible wait in a queue; approving executes.

https://yourdomain.com.au/admin/autopilot

The queue after one run on the demo data. A complaint with a drafted reply, a A$90 partner payout to mark paid after the transfer, and the ad-budget rule proposing a cut because the demo has one paid account against A$600 of spend. Everything else on the page happened on its own: four shipments created, a A$1,160 purchase order emailed to the supplier because it was under the cap, two lead emails, a partner statement, three customer emails answered.

What stays yours. Director duties (an email "approved" to the BAS agent each quarter, the annual solvency resolution), money leaving the account above the caps, and complaints. Under ten taps a month at the volumes in the model.
The physical layer is the hard part. Aero Healthcare's reseller programme names drop-shipping, so kits may never touch a warehouse; refills go to a 3PL from about 200 accounts, before which packing is an hour a day. The model prices a 3PL at A$6.50 a parcel from day one as the worst case: year one profit drops to A$35k for seven hours a week back, and by year three the profit is the same on three hours a week.
