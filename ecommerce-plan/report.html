Recommendation

Sell first-aid kits for sites and work vehicles, AED pads and batteries replaced before they expire, and PPE consumables, to small businesses in Adelaide first, wrapped in a replenishment plan that produces a compliance certificate. Goods come from an Australian ARTG-listed wholesaler, so there is no import, no minimum order and no tariff exposure. Kits are sold upfront so each new customer funds their own acquisition. The plan revenue recurs. The moat is the software you build: a QR kit register, an after-use refill loop, an expiry engine and the certificate.

Demand is legally mandated. Every one of Australia's 996,000 employing businesses must provide first aid and keep the kit stocked; tradies must keep one in each work vehicle; the model Code of Practice expects an audit at least every twelve months, six for low-risk sites. South Australia has roughly 160,000 small businesses. Incumbents either send a technician in a van on a fixed annual fee or hand the customer a web form to log their own checks. Nobody sells the compliance outcome with the goods shipping automatically and no visit.

This is a business you sell, at least for the first two hundred accounts. That is the honest trade: a founder-led grind in industrial estates in exchange for a cash profile no consumer brand can match, a customer base that renews because the law requires it, and an exit multiple (4 to 7x EBITDA for low-churn subscription) that consumer e-commerce (1.4x profit on Flippa in 2025) will never see.

Positioning line to test: your first-aid kits, always compliant, never another visit. What is being sold is the certificate and the absence of a job, not the bandages. Price sits between The First Aid Store's A$49 per six-month kit exchange and a visit service's undisclosed annual fee.

Avoid mining and large construction tenders, anyone who wants a visit, and single-kit home offices.

Defensibility

The software moat

Build in this order, and ship the smallest version that produces a certificate before the first sale:

  1. Kit register. Every kit has a QR code, a location, a contents profile (low-risk office, trade vehicle, remote, burns, eyewash) and per-line expiry dates seeded from the wholesaler's pack list.
  2. After-use loop. Scan, tap what was used (pictures, not text), a refill ships the same day. Nobody else has this, and it generates usage data nobody else has.
  3. Expiry engine. Nightly job schedules the two refill packs a year and AED pads or batteries due within 45 days, creates the order, tells the customer what is shipping and why.
  4. Compliance record and certificate. One page per customer: every kit, last check, next check, items replaced, plus a PDF referencing the model Code of Practice and the SA WHS Regulations 2012. This is what gets forwarded to an inspector, a head contractor or an insurer.
  5. Partner portal (month 4). A WHS consultant or bookkeeper sees all their clients' compliance states and earns a referral fee.

Stack: Shopify Basic for checkout, subscriptions and invoices; a small service of your own (Postgres, an API, a QR landing page, a PDF generator) for everything Shopify does not have; Klaviyo flows; Xero; MyPost Business labels. No custom checkout, no mobile app, no integrations before fifty accounts.

Channels

Go to market

Phase 1, weeks 5 to 12: twenty accounts, in person

  • Founder-led direct sales in industrial estates, ten visits a day, two days a week, sample kit and the certificate on a tablet. Open with the evidence question: when a head contractor or your insurer asks for proof your kits are compliant, what do you send them?
  • Warm referrals. Every buyer is asked for two names on the day; the referrer gets a free refill pack.
  • Google Ads on intent terms, A$15 to 25 a day, South Australia only: first aid kit restock, workplace first aid kit compliant, vehicle first aid kit, AED pads replacement. The landing page shows the certificate, not the kit.
  • Amazon AU and eBay AU listings for the kits with a QR insert that activates a 30-day plan trial. eBay AU charges no transaction fees under A$25k a year from May 2026.

Phase 2, months 4 to 6: repeatability

  • Partner channel: WHS consultants and safety trainers (portal plus 15% of first-year plan revenue), bookkeepers and small-business accountants (referral fee), and associations (Master Builders SA, HIA SA, MTA SA, NECA SA, ADA SA branch) with a member offer.
  • Content that gets cited: one authoritative page per question a buyer types into Google or ChatGPT, with structured data and the short answer first. AI Overviews cut organic clicks 38% on the queries they appear on, and brands cited inside them gain 35% CTR. Informational blog SEO is dead; being the cited answer is not.
  • Email and SMS flows only: onboarding, after-use confirmation, refill shipped, certificate renewed, renewal 30 days out, dunning with smart retries. Flows produce ~41% of email revenue from 5.3% of sends.
  • Founder-led LinkedIn, twice a week, with real anonymised findings from the register.

Phase 3, months 7 to 12: beyond Adelaide

  • Self-serve onboarding good enough that a referral in Perth or Toowoomba buys without a call. State-specific landing pages for VIC, QLD and WA.
  • Regional Australia deliberately: regional buyers are the most Australian-owned motivated, outer-regional online spend grows faster than metro, and Adelaide dispatch beats east-coast retailers to WA, NT and regional SA.

What not to do

  • No Meta ads. The buyer is at work, searching, or being referred.
  • No on-site visits, even once. It makes you Alsco with a worse van.
  • No 200-kit mining tender in year one; no account over 10% of revenue.
  • No "TGA approved". Say "supplied by a TGA-listed Australian manufacturer" and "compliant with the Safe Work Australia model Code of Practice".
  • No national push before Adelaide retention (G3) is proven.

Startup cost for this plan is about A$13,900, of which A$3,300 is opening stock that replenishes from sales. The rest of a A$15 to 40k budget stays in the bank as runway. There is no manufacturing lead time, no freight, no customs broker and no Chinese New Year in this plan.

Execution

First 90 days

  • Week 1

    Pty Ltd, ABN, GST, bank, business name, domain. TM Headstart filed. Two insurance quotes. Trade pricing requested from two wholesalers for site kits, vehicle kits, six-monthly refill packs, after-use modules and OEM AED consumables.

  • Week 2

    Ten discovery conversations (three trades you know, two practice managers, two childcare directors, three cold). Offer written on one page. Gate G1 on the quotes.

  • Weeks 3–4

    Kit register, QR after-use page, expiry engine, certificate PDF, Shopify store with two kits and two plans. Order 10 site and 15 vehicle kits and label them. Landing page live. Google Ads at A$20 a day, SA only. Klaviyo flows.

  • Weeks 5–8

    Two sales days a week in industrial estates; one day on clinics and childcare. Register kits and generate the certificate on the spot. Amazon AU and eBay AU listings live. Weekly CAC, kits per account, annual share. Gate G2 at week 8: twenty paying accounts, ten annual.

  • Weeks 9–13

    Fix the three most common complaints. Five cited-answer content pages with structured data. Merchant Center feed and ChatGPT discovery settings. Three WHS consultants, three bookkeepers, one association approached with the partner offer. Renewal and dunning flows. Month-3 review.

  • Day 90

    35 to 50 paying accounts, most in Adelaide, half on annual. A working register, after-use loop, expiry engine and certificate. CAC under A$120 including travel. One partner sending referrals. Cash at or above where you started.

What could go wrong

Risks and the fallback

  • Sales-limited growth. The first two hundred accounts are sold by you. The plan budgets your time honestly (no salary in year one), designs the partner channel from month four, and builds self-serve from day one so referrals convert without you.
  • Low revenue per account. About A$35 a month recurring plus A$237 of kits. The levers are fleets, multi-site clinics and childcare groups, the PPE add-on and AED consumables. Track kits per account, not just accounts.
  • Incumbent response. First Aid Distributions could add auto-ship to KitCheck. Your defence is speed, the after-use loop and its usage data, and owning one state's customers before going national.
  • Regulatory. Kits and bandages are ARTG-listed by the sponsor; you resell. Do not sponsor, import, repackage or assemble. Keep every claim to the Code of Practice.
  • Customer concentration. No account over 10% of revenue; it is a valuation discount and a churn risk.

Fallback if G2 fails twice

A premium Australian-made dog dental or gut-health chew sold on subscription with email as the profit engine, on the Bell & Bone template (solo founder, A$180k a month by 2022 on sampling, email and wholesale). Australian online pet is growing 15 to 20% a year, consumables repeat every four to eight weeks, gross margins run 50 to 65%, and the Australian ingredient story supports the roughly 10% premium Australian-made commands. It needs A$15 to 25k of inventory from a local contract manufacturer, an 18 to 24 week runway, founder-led short-form video at four to six posts a week for eight weeks before launch, in-store sampling with independent pet retailers, and no paid social until twenty reviews and a working email flow exist. A real business, and a harder one.
