# Partners, referrals, and what you actually earn

Written 13 September 2026. `ecommerce-plan/channels.py` produces every table
here; change an assumption and rerun (`--case low|base|high`). Arithmetic
on stated assumptions, not a forecast.

## The partner channel

**Who a partner is.** Someone who already walks into small businesses and
sees the first-aid kit: WHS consultants and safety trainers, bookkeepers
and accountants, industry associations and buying groups, and later
franchise head offices and insurance brokers. They are not resellers. They
refer, you sell and service, they get paid.

**What a partner earns.** 15 percent of first-year plan revenue per
referred account, paid once. A typical account (1.2 site kits, 1.6 vehicle
kits) carries about A$307 of plan revenue a year, so a referral is worth
about A$46 to the partner and costs you about A$61 including a sample kit.
A WHS consultant referring three clients a month makes about A$140 a
month for sending a link, and sees the referred clients' compliance state
in the partner view before every audit. That is the pitch: money for a
problem they already find every week, and a better audit.

**What a partner produces.** Assumptions, with the ReferralCandy and B2B
referral benchmarks behind them (top-quartile referral programs convert 8
percent or more of referred leads; cash rewards outperform coupons 2.7 to
4.5 times):

| | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts per active partner per month | 0.75 | 1.5 | 3.0 |
| Active partners at month 6 / 12 / 24 / 36 | 2 / 5 / 7 / 10 | 3 / 8 / 15 / 25 | 5 / 15 / 25 / 40 |
| Partner-sourced accounts, year 1 / 2 / 3 | 27 / 63 / 90 | 59 / 270 / 450 | 128 / 900 / 1,440 |

A consultant with 40 active clients who finds expired kits in half of them
and gets one in five of those to act is 4 accounts a month, so 1.5 is not
heroic. The uncertainty is how many partners you can recruit and keep
warm, not what each produces. Budget one partner conversation a week from
month four, and expect one in four to become active.

**Why partners beat everything else on cost.** A partner-sourced account
costs A$61 in fees and samples. Paid search costs A$86 in the base case
and A$294 in the low case. Your own direct selling costs A$30 in fuel and
samples plus sixteen hours a week of your time. Partners are the only
channel that scales without your hours and without a bidding war.

## Referrals

**Mechanism.** Every buyer is asked for two names on the day. The referrer
gets a free refill pack (about A$22 landed to you). The referred business
gets a kit registered within two days. The app records who referred whom
on the customer's notes today; a proper referral field is a ten-minute
change when it matters.

**Rate.** Modelled as a share of active accounts producing a new account
each month: 1 percent low, 2 percent base, 3.5 percent high. At 2 percent
an account produces about 0.8 referrals over its 40-month life, which is
consistent with benchmark data that most advocates refer exactly once and
that referred customers are ten times more likely to refer onward.

**What it does over time.** Referrals are small in year one (about 5
percent of accounts) and become the largest single source by year three in
the base case, because the rate is applied to a base that keeps growing.
It is the compounding channel, and it costs A$22 an account.

| Referral-sourced accounts | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low (1%) | 7 | 33 | 61 |
| Base (2%) | 26 | 172 | 394 |
| High (3.5%) | 86 | 711 | 1,895 |

## What you actually earn

Costs now include hired packing and customer-service hours beyond what
you do yourself (A$40 an hour loaded), storage from 400 accounts, and
tools and insurance that grow with volume. Your own hours are sales,
content and partner work, building, and the packing you keep doing.

How to read it honestly:

- **Year one pays about A$30 an hour in the base case** and A$12 in the
  low case. If your alternative is analyst contracting at A$100-plus an
  hour, year one is an investment of roughly 1,400 hours, not an income.
  The return is the asset: 346 accounts paying about A$12k a month with 97
  percent monthly retention, and an ARR of A$146k.
- **Year two is where it becomes a salary.** Base case A$198k profit before
  paying yourself, about A$2,700 a week after tax, on 21 hours a week. Low
  case A$74k, which is a modest full-time income for a part-time week.
- **Year three's per-hour figure flatters you.** A$425 an hour assumes 47
  hired hours a week that someone has to manage, and the model does not
  charge your management time. Read year three as "a business with a
  manager's salary in it," not as a solo income.
- **The high case is a different business.** 6,500 accounts, 540 parcels a
  week, 144 hired hours. Unit costs would change (a real warehouse, a
  full-time customer service person, a second wholesaler). Treat it as
  what the levers do, not as a plan.
- **Deferred revenue.** Annual plans are cash in advance. About a third of
  the recurring revenue you hold at any point is service you still owe.
  The profit line above is accrual and is the number to pay yourself from;
  the cash balance will look higher and is not all yours.

## The asset

Left in the company, profit is taxed at 25 percent. The business itself
is worth roughly:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base case at 4x EBITDA (subscription, low churn) | 172,000 | 792,000 | 1,590,000 |
| Base case at 2x (if a buyer reads it as an e-commerce store) | 86,000 | 396,000 | 795,000 |

The 4x reads require churn under 3 percent a month, a documented partner
channel, a replaceable founder and clean books. The 2x read is what an
Amazon-style seller gets. The compliance software, the annual billing and
the partner channel are what move you from the second row to the first.

## What moves these numbers, in order

1. **Partner count.** Doubling active partners from 8 to 16 in year one
   adds roughly 60 accounts and A$8k of year-one profit, and about A$40k of
   year-two profit. One partner conversation a week is the highest-return
   hour in the plan from month four.
2. **Churn.** The gap between 2.5 and 3.5 percent monthly churn is about
   a fifth of year-two profit. Renewal notices, after-use responsiveness
   and the certificate are the churn levers, and they are all in the app.
3. **Kits per account.** Every extra vehicle kit per account adds about
   A$10 of recurring contribution a month for the life of the account.
   Fleets and multi-site groups are worth chasing from month six.
4. **Paid search CPC.** Already covered in `09-paid-and-seo-numbers.md`.
   It is a filler channel in SA and a scaling channel nationally.
5. **Your sales hours.** Sixteen hours a week in year one is the engine.
   Halving it in months one to six roughly halves year-one accounts and
   delays everything else by two quarters, because partners and referrals
   are seeded by the accounts you win yourself.
