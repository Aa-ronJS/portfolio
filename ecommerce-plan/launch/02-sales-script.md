# Sales script: the first 200 accounts

> **Superseded in part, 13 Sep 2026.** The founder has ruled out founder-led
> selling. Walk-ins, phone calls and in-person registration below no longer
> apply; see `14-site-only-selling.md` for the plan that replaces them. The
> setup items (name, entity, Stripe, insurance, wholesale, labels) stand.

You are not selling bandages. You are selling the absence of a job and a
piece of paper that ends an awkward conversation. Keep every sentence about
that.

## Walk-in (industrial estates)

**Who to talk to.** The person at the counter or in the office, then the
owner. If there are utes out the front, there are vehicle kits, and they are
almost certainly wrong.

**Open (10 seconds).**
> "Morning. Aaron, I'm local. Quick one: when a head contractor or your
> insurer asks for proof your first-aid kits are compliant, what do you send
> them?"

Then stop talking. Three answers come back:

- *"Nobody's asked."* → "They will, and the utes are the ones that get
  checked. Can I show you the thirty-second version?"
- *"We've got a bloke who comes round / we buy them from Bunnings."* → "How
  do you know what's in the ute kits right now?" (They don't.)
- *"We've got a system."* → "Great. Does it ship the refill when something's
  used, or does someone have to remember?" (It doesn't.)

**Show (30 seconds).** Phone out. Open the demo scan page. Tap two items,
send. Then open the compliance record and the certificate.
> "That's the whole thing. Someone uses gloves and a dressing, they scan the
> label on the lid, tap what they used, and the refill's in the post that
> day. Every kit, every check, every refill sits on this record. That
> certificate is what you send when someone asks."

**Price (20 seconds).** Point at the offer sheet table.
> "Workshop kit and three ute kits is $296 today. The plan that keeps them
> stocked and gives you the certificate is $396 a year, so about $33 a month.
> No visits, no contracts, cancel any time."

**Close.**
> "I can register the kits now and you'll have the certificate before I
> leave. Card or invoice?"

**Ask for two names**, always.
> "Who else around here has utes and hasn't sorted this?"

## Phone or email (clinics, childcare, offices)

Subject line that gets opened: **Your first-aid kit audit, done for you**.

> Hi [name], I run a small Adelaide business that keeps workplace first-aid
> kits compliant without anyone visiting or remembering: scan the label when
> something is used and the refill ships that day, scheduled refill packs
> twice a year, and a compliance certificate on demand. Practice managers
> use it for accreditation; centre directors for ACECQA. A site kit is
> $119, the plan is $144 a year per kit. I can drop a kit in and register it
> in ten minutes this week. Would Tuesday or Thursday suit?

## Discovery questions (for the first ten conversations, not for selling)

1. How many kits do you have, and where are they?
2. When did someone last check them? What did they find?
3. Who has asked you for evidence of first-aid compliance in the last year?
4. What do you do when something in a kit is used?
5. What did the last kit or restock cost you, and where did you buy it?
6. Do you have a defibrillator? Who tracks the pads?
7. If this were done for you, what would it be worth a month?
8. What would make you say no to this today?

Write the answers down. Question 8 is the one that shapes the offer.

## Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We just buy a new kit when it runs out." | "So right now you don't know what's in them. That's the bit an inspector writes up. This makes it known, for $12 a month a kit." |
| "Too expensive." | "The kit is the same price as the shop. The plan is $12 a month for a site kit. One improvement notice costs more than a decade of that." |
| "I'll think about it." | "Fair. What's the one thing you'd want to be sure of?" (Answer it.) "Can I leave a vehicle kit today so you can try the scan?" |
| "We've got Alsco / St John coming quarterly." | "Then you're paying for the drive. Between visits, what happens when a kit gets used? This ships the refill that day and costs less than the visit." |
| "Is it TGA approved?" | "The kits and contents are made by a TGA-listed Australian manufacturer. What I add is the tracking and the certificate; the goods are the same ones the big suppliers sell." |
| "We're a sole trader, do we need this?" | "If you're on a site, the head contractor's induction will ask. And your ute is a workplace under WHS. One vehicle kit and the plan is $59 plus $84 a year." |
| "Can you just sell me the kit?" | "Yes. The plan is optional. Most people add it when the first thing gets used." (Sell the kit. Note the account. They convert on the first scan.) |
| "What if I cancel?" | "Cancel any time; the kits are yours. You lose the refills and the certificate, that's all." |

## The two things to never say

- "TGA approved." Say "made by a TGA-listed Australian manufacturer".
- "Guarantees compliance." Say "keeps the kits stocked and gives you the
  record and the certificate". The risk assessment and first-aider training
  are theirs.

## After every visit

Log: business, contact, kits seen, outcome, reason for no, follow-up date.
`05-tracking-sheet.csv` has the columns. Follow up every "next week" on
the day you said.
